## 2. What the RHP and later filings say about DCV technology, products, ANDAs, partners, facility, inspections and capex

### Takeaway
The DCV business sits in wholly owned subsidiary **Knovea Pharmaceutical Pvt Ltd**, which runs the **Mhow (Indore) facility**: commissioned in 2025, 20 mn DCVs/yr capacity, ₹3,919 mn invested by Mar 2026, still in R&D/pilot operations at the RHP date. The first two products are DCVs of **methylprednisolone sodium succinate** and **hydrocortisone sodium succinate** (generic Solu-Medrol and Solu-Cortef Act-O-Vial equivalents). Their US market is put at **US$288 mn**.
- **First ANDA:** filed **Sep 2026**. The company calls it the "first generic filing for the DCV product in US market" but did not say which molecule.
- **Second ANDA:** scheduled for **Q4 FY27** (Jan–Mar 2027).
- **US partner:** a definitive, exclusive US commercialisation agreement for two DCV products was signed on **30 Sep 2026** with an unnamed "leading global pharmaceutical company". Payments are milestones plus a profit share.
- **Pipeline:** five further DCV products are in development under 505(b)(2). Dexamethasone and triamcinolone DCVs are not disclosed.

### Cited Findings
**Technology and positioning**
- RHP: "We are one of the first generics companies globally to successfully develop DCV through backward integration, by relying on in-house materials and not imports, for Methylprednisolone Sodium Succinate injection and Hydrocortisone Sodium Succinate" (RHP p.182, p.230, p.233, p.236; sourced to the F&S Report) — [SEBI RHP PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)
- RHP definition: DCVs are "specialised and complex drug delivery containers with two separate chambers (API in lyophilised / freeze-dried form in one chamber and diluent in another chamber divided by a stopper)" (RHP p.239)
- Platform scope: "double chamber vials, double chamber bags and double chamber syringes". The company has "achieved proof of concept for DCBs and DCSs and are presently conducting stability testing for DCBs" (RHP p.219, p.236). Pres. slide 22 adds: "Expand into Double chamber bags (proof of concept established; currently in stability testing phase)" — [Q1FY27 presentation](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/Attachhis/91eed093-577e-4264-babd-546a03192722.pdf)
- Complex-injectables R&D had 14 scientists at 31 Mar 2026, focused on "active freeze drying (a lyophilisation technique), development of double-chamber drug-device platforms, life cycle management and packaging innovation" (RHP p.148, p.230). Pres. slide 20 lists the equipment: DCV filling machine, lyophilizer, filtration vessel, X-ray inspection machine, labelling machine.

**Products and pipeline**
- RHP: "Commercialisation of our first two DCV products, Methylprednisolone Sodium Succinate injection and Hydrocortisone Sodium Succinate, is expected in Fiscal 2027 with a market size in the United States of US$ 288 million. (Source: F&S Report)" (RHP p.236). The F&S section adds "With an expected launch in fiscal 2027" and cites Evaluate Pharma (RHP p.182, fn 16).
- Pipeline: "As of March 31, 2026… we are developing five DCV products under the Section 505(b)(2) regulatory pathway… in addition to two pre-existing approved DCV products" (RHP p.148, p.231). The business section words it as "in addition to two developed DCV products" (RHP p.239). The 505(b)(2) pipeline has an "addressable market of US$ 890 million in 2025 globally… CAGR of 5.7% between 2025 and 2026" (RHP p.182, p.236; F&S citing Evaluate Pharma; fn 18: "Symbiotec is currently developing five products using DCV").
- The RHP does not name the five pipeline molecules. It discloses no dexamethasone, triamcinolone or other corticosteroid DCV. The only other named product effort is a generic Premarin (conjugated estrogens) on the API/formulation side (RHP p.148, p.231, p.239).

**ANDA filings and approvals**
- The RHP discloses no ANDA filed or approved; at the RHP date the products were "expected" to commercialise and the company was "in discussions" with partners (RHP p.233, p.236).
- Q1FY27 presentation (21 Sep 2026), slide 5: "Successfully filed first molecule ANDA for DCV in September 2026 – First generic filing for the DCV product in US market" and "Filing of second ANDA scheduled for Q4 FY27" — [Q1FY27 presentation](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/Attachhis/91eed093-577e-4264-babd-546a03192722.pdf)
- CMD Anil Satwani (slide 8): "we filed our first ANDA from our Dual Chamber Vial platform this quarter… and are progressing well towards a second filing in the coming months… our development work is steadily adding new molecules to the DCV portfolio and opening adjacent formats."

**US partner and licensing**
- RHP: "we are in discussions with multiple global specialty pharmaceutical companies to license DCV products for distribution globally, which we intend to cater to using our Mhow Facility" (RHP p.233). Economics shown on Pres. slide 17: "Product Sales / Milestone Payments / Profit / Revenue Share".
- On 21 Sep 2026: "Licensing agreement for distribution of two DCV products in US – Term-sheet signed, Agreement at advanced stage, expected to sign off shortly" (Pres. slide 5). Capex slide 7 says of the DCVs: "Majority of capacity reserved with strategic partner".
- 30 Sep 2026 Reg. 30 filing: Knovea "announced a definitive agreement with a leading global pharmaceutical company, granting the partner exclusive rights to commercialise and distribute two Dual Chamber Vial ('DCV')-based injectable products in the United States… Knovea will lead development, regulatory submissions, manufacturing and supply, while the partner drives commercialization and distribution… milestone payments through development, regulatory approval and commercial launch, alongside profit-sharing from launch onwards." It also states: "Together, the two products address a U.S. market estimated at approximately US$288 million. The innovator is currently the only company with a DCV presentation in this market." Partner name, consideration and "size of the entity" are withheld "due to confidentiality obligations"; the agreement is "long-term" — [BSE filing, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachLive/47a96a6d-0348-40db-a957-9ae203f20580.pdf)

**Facility, capacity and approvals**
- The "Mhow Facility" is "the commissioned facility of our Subsidiary, Knovea Pharmaceutical Private Limited" (RHP p.7). Knovea was incorporated 17 Feb 2020 and is wholly owned (RHP p.268, p.357).
- Facility table (RHP p.241): Mhow Facility, complex injectables, commissioned 2025, 23,470 sq m, "20 million DCV capacity". - Status at the RHP date: "we have started R&D and pilot-scale operations"; the facility is "capable of producing up to 20 million double chamber vials… with space for further expansion and for which we are in the process of applying for approvals to various regulatory bodies" (RHP p.30, p.44). Risk factor 22: "we cannot assure you that… it will receive all the regulatory approvals applied for" (RHP p.44).

- Sterile API source: the Rau Facility "houses sterile and non-sterile corticosteroid API manufacturing blocks" with aseptic filling, a lyophilizer with CIP/SIP, Class 100 environment and WFI (RHP p.241). It is US FDA, EU-GMP and WHO-GMP approved (RHP p.30).

**Capex and use of proceeds**
- Gross capex was ₹1,475 cr at 30 Jun 2026, estimated at ₹1,666 cr by end-FY27. Complex Injectables (DCV) accounts for ₹356 cr at 30 Jun 2026 and ₹376 cr by end-FY27. Of total gross capex, 61% (about ₹1,000 cr) sits in new projects "to start generating revenue in 6–12 months". Q1FY27 gross capex was about ₹68 cr (Pres. slide 7).
- ICICI Direct: "~₹1,000+ crore in the new businesses, including ₹376 crore towards Complex Injectables and ₹584 crore towards CDMO". The next capex phase includes "incremental capacities for Double Chamber Bags and Syringes" — [ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)
- No IPO proceeds are earmarked for injectables (see Q1).

### Inferences
- **The two products in the 30 Sep deal are almost certainly the methylprednisolone sodium succinate and hydrocortisone sodium succinate DCVs.** The filing does not name them, but it uses the same US$288 mn figure that the RHP attaches to those two products, and Q1FY27 slide 5 describes the deal as covering "two DCV products". This is high-confidence but unconfirmed.
- **Product revenue in FY27 looks unlikely.** The first ANDA went in during Sep 2026 and the second is due Jan–Mar 2027. A standard 10-month GDUFA review gives the earliest approval of the first product around Jul 2027 (FY28), and Mhow is a new, uninspected site, so it will probably need a pre-approval inspection. The RHP's "commercialisation expected in Fiscal 2027" therefore most plausibly means partnering and milestone income in FY27, which matches ICICI's "milestones expected in FY27E and commercial revenues from FY28E".
- The company has not said which molecule was filed first. "First generic filing for the DCV product" means a first generic *DCV presentation*, not a first generic of the molecule. Five active ANDAs already exist for methylprednisolone sodium succinate and three for hydrocortisone sodium succinate (Q5).
- "Two pre-existing approved DCV products" (RHP p.148/231) most plausibly refers to the reference DCV products (Pfizer's Solu-Medrol and Solu-Cortef Act-O-Vial) that Symbiotec's two ANDA products copy. It cannot mean Symbiotec-owned approvals, because the Orange Book shows none (Q5). The RHP's own p.239 says "two developed DCV products".
- The "in-house materials and not imports" claim, together with Rau's sterile lyophilised corticosteroid API block, points to vertical integration: Symbiotec's own (hemi)succinate API from Rau feeding Knovea's DCV fill-finish. This is my inference.

### Gaps
- Partner identity, milestone amounts and profit-share percentage are all undisclosed (confidentiality).
- I found no disclosure of which strengths or pack sizes were filed, of the 505(b)(2) pipeline molecules, or of any EU/RoW DCV filings. The Orange Book lists Solu-Medrol at 40 mg, 125 mg, 500 mg, 1 g and 2 g and Solu-Cortef at 100 mg, 250 mg, 500 mg and 1 g. Which of these come as Act-O-Vial DCVs needs a label check.
- I found no disclosure of Mhow regulatory approvals obtained so far (state FDA manufacturing licence, EU GMP and so on); the RHP says only that approvals are "applied for".

### Inferences
- The FY26 "complex injectables" revenue of ₹330.5 mn (₹33.1 cr, the same figure ICICI shows as FY26 complex-injectable revenue) is non-recurring recognition of a payment received in FY24. It should not be read as DCV product sales.
- The counterparty for the FY24 milestone payment is undisclosed. It may be an earlier DCV partner or development deal that predates the 30 Sep 2026 agreement, since the term sheet for that agreement was only signed around Sep 2026. Treat this link as an open question.
- The US revenue jump in FY26 (₹302 mn to ₹1,140 mn) is large. Whether it includes the milestone income depends on where the counterparty sits; I cannot verify that from the RHP.
- With about 76–80% global volume share in methylprednisolone and hydrocortisone APIs, Symbiotec very likely supplies API to several makers of methylprednisolone sodium succinate and hydrocortisone sodium succinate injections. It holds US DMFs for both hemisuccinate APIs (Q5). No customer is named, however.
- Pfizer being listed as its own methylprednisolone API source weighs against assuming Pfizer buys Symbiotec's methylprednisolone. Two of Symbiotec's top 5 customers are unnamed innovators with 4 products each, which is consistent with, but does not prove, a large steroid innovator such as Pfizer being a customer. This is speculative.

### Gaps
- The RHP does not disclose whether any top customer is Pfizer, Fresenius, Sagent, Hikma, Amneal, Cipla, Apotex or Zydus (the current ANDA holders; see Q5).
- No revenue split by molecule, such as methylprednisolone or hydrocortisone API sales, is disclosed.

## 4. Industry data in the offer document (Frost & Sullivan report)

### Takeaway
The commissioned report is by **Frost & Sullivan (India)**, titled "Independent Market Research on the Overview of the Global API Market and Fermentation Technologies", dated Aug 2026 and paid for by the company. It sizes the **global DCV market at US$1,811 mn in 2025, growing at a 9.5% CAGR to 2030**, and the US methylprednisolone/hydrocortisone sodium succinate opportunity at **US$288 mn** (sourced from Evaluate Pharma). It says innovators hold 65–70% of the DCV market and that DCVs command a 20–50% price premium. ### Inferences

- Two statements look contradictory but are probably consistent:
  - ICICI's "10% spread across more than 5 generic players" most likely refers to single-vial generics;
  - the company's 30 Sep statement that "the innovator is currently the only company with a DCV presentation" refers to the DCV format specifically.

  So Symbiotec's pitch is that a generic DCV would be differentiated from existing single-vial generics.
- The 20–50% DCV price premium is an F&S generalisation drawn mostly from biologics and specialty therapies. It is not a measured premium for Solu-Medrol or Solu-Cortef Act-O-Vial over single-vial generics, and should not be applied directly to those ASPs.

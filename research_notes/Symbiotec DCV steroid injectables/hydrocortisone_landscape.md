## Q1. Who holds NDA/ANDAs, when did they enter, which was the first generic, and did any get first-generic or CGT status?

### Takeaway
The test holds: only **three active ANDAs** exist, all approved in the last ~25 months: Cipla (5 Sep 2024), Apotex (5 Feb 2026) and Zydus (7 May 2026). All three cover **100 mg only**. The 250 mg, 500 mg and 1,000 mg strengths have **zero approved generics** and remain Pfizer-only. About 25 legacy ANDAs from 1978 to 2006 (Abbott/Hospira "A-Hydrocort", APP/Abraxis, Baxter, Watson, IMS) are all discontinued, so Pfizer had sole-source status until Sep 2024. FDA designated Cipla a Competitive Generic Therapy (CGT) and gave it 180-day CGT exclusivity, with first marketing on 26 Sep 2024. FDA did **not** put it on the "First Generic Drug Approvals" list, because generics had been approved before.

### Inferences
- **No generic was marketed just before Cipla.** CGT designation requires "inadequate generic competition" on the reference product, and Cipla also got 180-day CGT exclusivity. Together these imply that no other approved, non-discontinued generic was in the Orange Book's active section in Sep 2024. That fits all legacy ANDAs being discontinued, so Pfizer was effectively sole source for some period before Sep 2024 (the exit date of the last legacy generic is unknown).
- **Cipla's exclusivity window:** 180 days from first commercial marketing on 26 Sep 2024 runs to about **25 Mar 2025** (my calculation; FDA does not list an expiry date). Apotex (Feb 2026) and Zydus (May 2026) were approved well after it lapsed.
- **Rough filing dates from ANDA numbers.** ANDA numbers are assigned sequentially, so they hint at submission timing: 214050 around 2020, 217191 around 2022, 219856 around 2024. That would mean a multi-year review for Cipla and Zydus. This is a heuristic, not a verified date.
- **Strength-specific CGT.** The CGT list is strength-specific (e.g. Theophylline ER "Yes – 300 mg, No – 450 mg" in the same list), and the 250/500/1,000 mg strengths have no ANDA at all. The first ANDA approved for those strengths could therefore plausibly seek CGT designation and 180-day CGT exclusivity. This is unverified for these strengths.

### Gaps
- FDA approval letters for ANDAs 214050, 219856 and 217191 were not retrievable at the standard Drugs@FDA paths (HTTP 404). Submission dates, the RLD presentation referenced and any review history are unverified.
- Exit dates of the legacy generics (Hospira A-Hydrocort, APP, Baxter, Watson) were not found. The Orange Book gives no discontinuation dates.
- No company press releases were found for the Cipla, Apotex or Zydus approvals. That is unusual for Zydus, which normally issues them, but none surfaced in search.

## Q2. Does any generic replicate the Act-O-Vial DCV? Is the DCV a technical or regulatory barrier? Is there an FDA product-specific guidance?

### Takeaway
No US generic replicates the Act-O-Vial. All three approved generics are **"100 mg Plain" lyophilized single vials with no diluent**. That matches Pfizer's own 100 mg plain fliptop vial, the one non-DCV Solu-Cortef presentation. The strengths Pfizer sells **only** as Act-O-Vial (250 mg, 500 mg, 1 g) have no generic at all. That pattern is consistent with the DCV acting as a real practical barrier. FDA has published **no product-specific guidance** for hydrocortisone sodium succinate injection.

### Inferences
- **Why generics stop at 100 mg.** Every entrant chose the 100 mg strength, the only strength where Pfizer sells a plain vial. That points to entrants avoiding the DCV: a plain-vial generic of a 100 mg product matches an existing RLD presentation. A plain-vial generic of the 250/500/1,000 mg strengths would differ in container, user interface and whether diluent is included. That would likely draw FDA scrutiny under the drug-device comparative-analysis framework, or need a DCV the generic firm cannot make. This is inference: no FDA document says plain-vial ANDAs for the higher strengths are barred.
- **Regulatory bar is moderate, not prohibitive.** With no PSG and a Q1/Q2-identical formulation, a 100 mg plain vial is regulatorily simple (a biowaiver-type parenteral solution after reconstitution). The barrier is mainly manufacturing and supply-chain: sterile lyophilization, API and DCV hardware. It is not a bioequivalence barrier.
- **Substitution can extend to 100 mg Act-O-Vial demand.** The Orange Book does not separate the 100 mg plain vial from the 100 mg Act-O-Vial, so pharmacies can substitute AP-rated plain generics for 100 mg orders, subject to workflow (separate diluent). The 100 mg strength is therefore exposed, while 250/500/1,000 mg is protected until a DCV generic arrives.

### Gaps
- No public FDA statement was found on whether a dual-chamber vial is a "device constituent part" for ANDA purposes, or whether a plain-vial ANDA for 250/500/1,000 mg would be approvable.
- Whether any foreign or ex-US generic DCV of hydrocortisone exists (e.g. in EU or India) was not researched (US-only scope).

## Q3. Why has competition been thin (API, sterile lyophilization, price, Pfizer supply)?

### Takeaway
Evidence points to a combination of causes. The market was abandoned for years: all legacy generics exited, leaving Pfizer sole source until Sep 2024. API sources are narrow: only **4 active US Type II DMFs** for hydrocortisone hemisuccinate or sodium succinate, three of them filed 2023–2025, versus 9 for methylprednisolone succinate. Pfizer is captive on API. Most entrants rely on CMO sterile-lyo capacity. **Symbiotec holds the oldest active hemisuccinate DMF (DMF 29221, March 2015)** and also lists hydrocortisone sodium succinate bulk with FDA. Historical pricing was not researched here (covered elsewhere).

### Inferences
- **API supply has only recently widened.** Three of the four active hemisuccinate/sodium succinate DMFs were filed between Jan 2023 and Sep 2025, after a gap of roughly 30 years with no new filings apart from Symbiotec's in 2015. ANDA approvals followed in 2024–2026. Before 2023, Symbiotec was likely the only active third-party US DMF source.
- **Symbiotec may supply the entrants, but this is unverified.** It is plausible that Symbiotec supplies API to one or more of the 2024–26 entrants (Cipla's ANDA 214050 was probably filed around 2020, when Symbiotec's DMF 29221 was the only active one). No source names the API supplier for any ANDA.
- **Captive API removes Pfizer's dependence on third parties.** Pfizer's in-house hemisuccinate bulk listing and its hydrocortisone DMF suggest Pfizer is vertically integrated on API.
- **Generic interest was low for decades.** A sub-scale, low-priced legacy market with all generics exited, plus a DCV-heavy presentation mix, is consistent with that. The 2023 and 2025 shortages and the CGT incentive (180-day exclusivity for the first entrant into a market with inadequate competition) plausibly triggered the 2020s filings.

- Which DMF each ANDA references is not public.
- Why the legacy generics (Hospira, APP, Baxter, Watson) discontinued was not found.

## Q4. Shortage history 2017–2026 (FDA, ASHP, Pfizer)

### Takeaway
The documented US disruptions are all Pfizer-driven and concentrated in 2023–2025:
- **Mar–Jun 2023:** 100 mg Act-O-Vial disruption, with allocation, direct-order only, and an FDA-approved 5-month expiry extension on 32 lots.
- **About Apr 2024:** 100 mg plain vial short, per a secondary source.
- **Apr–3Q 2025:** 100 mg Act-O-Vial 25-pack interruption with an 80% wholesaler allocation.

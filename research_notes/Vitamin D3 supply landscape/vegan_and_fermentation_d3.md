# Non-animal vitamin D3: lichen, microalgae, fermentation (yeast 7-DHC) and phytosterol-derived ("plant-source") routes, as of October 2026

Research date: 21 Oct 2026. Older items are dated inline. Unit conventions used below: 1 µg vitamin D = 40 IU ([NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)), so 1 MIU = 25 mg pure D3; crystalline D3 = 40 MIU/g; a "100,000 IU/g" oil/powder = 0.25% w/w D3; a "500,000 IU/g" feed-grade powder = 1.25% w/w D3. FX assumption for conversions (mine): RMB 7.2/USD.

Framing caveat: the label "vegan / plant-based D3" covers at least four chemically different supply chains: (1) lichen extracts, (2) "algae" D3, (3) phytosterol-to-cholesterol semi-synthesis (pine/tall-oil or soy sterols, then fermentation plus chemistry, then 7-DHC, then UV), and (4) yeast fermentation of sterol precursors. Marketing copy often blurs these. The best-documented industrial non-animal D3 capacity found is route (3), not lichen.

## 1. Lichen-derived D3: who produces it, species and sourcing, process, real capacity, and authenticity

### Takeaway
Lichen D3 is sold by a handful of ingredient brands: Vitashine (ESB Developments, UK, the original since about 2012), Vegadelight (Nutraland USA, the claimed US #1 by 2025 B2B value), Fermenta Biotech's VITADEE Green (India, FSSAI-approved Jul 2026), and a long tail of Chinese and Indian bulk sellers. None of them discloses volumes. Peer-reviewed data show wild lichen holds only about 0.7–2 µg D3/g dry matter. Direct extraction can therefore only supply kg-scale pure D3 unless very large tonnages of lichen are processed, and the "lichen D3" supply chain is opaque. I found no published adulteration investigation.

**Science and capacity constraint**
- Wang, Bengtsson, Kärnefelt & Björn (2001, *J. Photochem. Photobiol. B* 62:118–122) measured *Cladina arbuscula* thalli at vitamin D3 0.67–2.04 µg/g DM and D2 0.22–0.55 µg/g DM. Provitamin D3 (7-DHC) was **below detection (0.01)**, and provitamin D2 was 89–146 µg/g. D3 content correlated with UV-B at the collection site, meaning biosynthesis is light-dependent. — [Lund University LUP](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/f49ca442-2470-4d4e-95d1-1af92d805d5c)
- A 2025 study of epiphytic lichen-forming fungi found that D3 varies with habitat and that olive-oil maceration was the most efficient extraction method. A vendor summary cites ranges of about 0.6 to >20 µg/g; I did not verify this against the paper. — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S187861462500100X); [vendor summary via search](https://shopholistico.com/blogs/news/vitamin-d-from-lichen-vs-lanolin)
- A brand page notes that "given that lichens grow very slowly, scalability and feasibility were key considerations." — [Naternal](https://naternalvitamins.com.au/blogs/womens-health/vitashine-plant-based-vitamin-d3)

**Authenticity / labelling**
- US supplement labels must list vitamin D3 but are not required to specify its source, so "vegan" claims depend on certification. (Source is a blog-level claim.) — [vegancheckr / search summary](https://vegancheckr.com/guides/is-vitamin-d3-vegan)
- Historical precedent: in 2010 Garden of Life's then "Vitamin Code" D3 was found to be "synthesized from animal cholesterol, primarily lanolin," which shows how sourcing claims can mislead. — [VRG blog, 29 Mar 2010](https://www.vrg.org/blog/2010/03/29/garden-of-life-vitamin-d3-derived-from-lanolin/)
- Innobio (China) markets its pine-derived D3 as "verified by stable-isotope analysis to prove plant origin" (supplier claim). This suggests the industry treats origin verification as a differentiator. — [NutraIngredients product page](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Product-innovations/isotope-verified-vegan-d3-from-pine)
- AIDP (2019) said competitor non-animal products "contain a mix of D3 and D2," while its own was 100% D3. — [NutraIngredients, 29 Apr 2019](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2019/04/29/AIDP-There-is-a-significant-market-potential-for-vegan-D3/)

### Inferences
- **Capacity arithmetic (mine):** at 0.67–2.04 µg D3/g dry lichen and 100% recovery, 1 kg of pure D3 needs about 490–1,490 tonnes of dry lichen. One kg of 100,000 IU/g oil (2.5 g D3) needs about 1.2–3.7 t of dry lichen. Direct extraction therefore fits small premium volumes of 100,000 IU/g oils and powders (tens of tonnes of oil a year means only tens of kg of pure D3). It cannot plausibly supply tonne-scale crystalline D3. A "1 t/yr" plant-D3 capacity (Fermenta, unit unknown) or Chundi's "10 t/yr D3 crystal" line (Section 4) cannot come from direct lichen extraction at reported lichen D3 levels. Such volumes imply a semi-synthetic or conversion step (e.g., UV-treating extracted sterols) or a phytosterol-cholesterol route.
- 7-DHC is undetectable in *Cladina*, and one spec sheet describes "UV treated and hydroxylated" extract. Some commercial "lichen D3" therefore probably involves post-extraction UV processing of lichen sterols, which may also co-produce D2 from the abundant provitamin D2. That fits AIDP's 2019 "mix of D3 and D2" jibe. This is plausible but unverified.
- The parent's question "is much of lichen D3 really cholesterol-free semi-synthetic?" cannot be confirmed from public sources. The opacity is a real commercial risk, and an origin-verification advantage (isotopes, chain-of-custody) is being marketed as such.
- Fermenta's own Dec 2025 release says "plant-derived" while trade press says "lichen." The analyst should confirm Fermenta's actual route directly. If it is a phytosterol-based route, Fermenta competes on Symbiotec's home ground.

### Gaps
- I found no verified capacity (kg/t), harvest tonnage or price for Vitashine, Vegadelight or any lichen extractor, and no current (2024–2026) information on ESB Developments' status or partners. "Nutrition Supplies & Services," Mérieux and Lycored links were not found.
- I found no evidence that Pharmavit, Vitae Caps, Dr. Paul Lohmann, Kingdomway, Garden Bio, DSM-Firmenich, BASF, Gnosis by Lesaffre, Stauber, Divi's or Orgenetics sell lichen D3; searches returned nothing. Absence of evidence is not proof.
- I found no peer-reviewed or regulator adulteration study of lichen D3 supplements.

## 2. Microalgae-derived D3: suppliers and commercial status

### Takeaway
Only one named B2B "algae D3" ingredient surfaced: AIDP's VegD3 (US, supplied since 2016, sold as pure crystalline cholecalciferol). Academic work (DTU, Denmark) shows *Nannochloropsis oceanica* makes only about 0.3–1 µg D3/g DM after UV-B, and a DTU-led upscaling project (AlgaeVita) is still at R&D stage in 2025. Algae D3 is niche and not demonstrated at commercial scale beyond AIDP's undisclosed volumes.

### Cited Findings
- AIDP (California) markets VegD3, an "algae-derived cholecalciferol" for vegan infant formula, supplements and foods. It is "produced as pure crystal cholecalciferol rather than as a vitamin D3-rich plant extract," supplied since 2016, self-affirmed GRAS and Vegan Society registered. AIDP declined to give the number of customer products, and no capacity or price was disclosed. AIDP argued that lichen "takes longer to grow than algae." (Apr 2019) — [NutraIngredients, 29 Apr 2019](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2019/04/29/AIDP-There-is-a-significant-market-potential-for-vegan-D3/)
- In a 2020 *Food Chemistry* study, only *N. oceanica* (of four species tested) produced D3, up to 1 ± 0.3 µg/g DM, rising with UV-B dose. — [ScienceDirect 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308814620304891)
- A 2025 *Algal Research* paper reports *N. oceanica* biomass reaching up to 0.260 µg D3/g DM after post-cultivation UV-B exposure, with no negative effect on EPA. — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211926425005752)
- DTU's AlgaeVita project (Jul 2025) aims to upscale *N. oceanica* production for ingredients rich in omega-3 and D3. No industrial partner was named in the summary. — [DTU Food news, Jul 2025](https://www.food.dtu.dk/english/newsarchive/2025/07/researchers-aim-to-upscale-new-source-of-vitamin-d3-and-omega-3-fatty-acids)
- Retail: Naturalis sells vegan D3+K2 listing "Cholecalciferol [D3] from *Nannochloropsis oceanica* microalgae." — [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Naturalis-Plant-Based-Vitamin-Certified-Softgels/dp/B09R2LYZ86)

### Inferences
- Natural algal D3 levels (0.26–1 µg/g DM) are as low as lichen's, so a pure-crystal "algae D3" product like AIDP's almost certainly involves concentration plus chemical or photochemical conversion of algal sterols, or a different feedstock. The "algae" label likely describes the sterol source rather than direct extraction. This is unverified.
- Algae D3 is not a credible near-term mass-market route. Its commercial logic is as a co-product with algal omega-3 (EPA) biomass.

### Gaps
- "Algavit," "VegaVit," Fermentalg and Nordic algae players: no evidence found of commercial algae-D3 ingredients. AIDP's manufacturer, algal species and volumes are undisclosed.

## 3. Fermentation D3: engineered yeast producing 7-DHC (or other provitamins), titers, and commercial status

### Takeaway
Engineered yeasts reached about 3.5–4.3 g/L 7-DHC in 5–50 L fed-batch runs in 2024–2025, mostly at Chinese universities (Jiangnan University among them). That is technically respectable, but I found **no** commercial launch of yeast-fermented 7-DHC-to-D3 (vitamin D3 itself) as of Oct 2026. The one proven commercial yeast-fermentation vitamin D route is dsm-firmenich's Hy-D (25-OH-D3 for feed). It uses GM *S. cerevisiae* CBS 146008 to make the sterol 5,7,24-cholestatrienol, which is then converted chemically, and it is fully EU-authorised. Chinese players such as Ruipu Bio and TIB-CAS are building "calcifediol cell factories."

### Cited Findings
**Academic titers (7-DHC)**
- Diploid *S. cerevisiae* (Jiangnan University, Liu Long team) reached 3.80 g/L 7-DHC in 24 h in a 50 L fermenter, with productivity of 158.3 mg/L/h. The authors claim the highest productivity reported. — [PubMed 39480248 (2024)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39480248); [Chinese summary](http://futurefoods.jiangnan.edu.cn/info/1072/1695.htm)
- Diploid *S. cerevisiae* with sub-organelle engineering reached 640.77 mg/L in shake flask and 4.28 ± 0.11 g/L in a 10 L fermenter. — [Metabolic Engineering 2024, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1096717624000843)
- *Candida tropicalis* ICE2-INO2 reached 3.65 g/L 7-DHC in a 5 L bioreactor. — [ACS Sustainable Chem. Eng., 12 Nov 2025](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acssuschemeng.5c08538)
- *Yarrowia lipolytica* on kitchen waste reached 3.5 g/L in a 5 L bioreactor. — [J. Agric. Food Chem. 2025, PubMed 39699994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39699994/)
- Peroxisome and pathway-refactoring strategies in *S. cerevisiae* reached 517.4 mg/L in shake flask (2025). Secretory production reached 28.2 mg/g DCW at 41.4% secretion. — [PubMed 40575831](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40575831/); [PubMed 37688450](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37688450)
- Chinese commentary says 7-DHC is currently made industrially mainly by chemical synthesis (from cholesterol), which is costly and polluting, and that microbial cell factories are the sustainable alternative. — [cn-ferment.com](https://www.cn-ferment.com/news/show-15886.html)

**Patents (China)**
- CN114213302A: 7-DHC sits in the fermentation broth as a "7-DHC–protein complex." The patent claims purification by ceramic-membrane filtration, ultrasonic disruption and isoelectric precipitation, followed by UV conversion to D3 directly in aqueous solution, avoiding heavy organic-solvent extraction. — [Google Patents CN114213302A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN114213302A/zh)
- CN116790393B: engineered *S. cerevisiae* makes "active VD3" (25-OH-D3) from glucose. The broth is lysed and UV-irradiated directly, reaching 5.02 mg/L 25-OH-VD3 (patent's own claim of industrial suitability). — [Google Patents CN116790393B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN116790393B)
- CN107207429A covers an improved production method for vitamins D2 and D3. — [Google Patents CN107207429A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN107207429A/zh)

**Commercial yeast-fermentation precedent: dsm-firmenich Hy-D (25-OH-D3)**
- The precursor 5,7,24-cholestatrienol "is produced by fermentation with a genetically modified strain of *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*" deposited as CBS 146008 at the Westerdijk Institute, then converted to 25-OH-D3. EFSA calls 25-OH-D3 a semi-synthetic product. No viable cells or recombinant DNA were detected in the product. Spec: >94% 25-OH-D3, with batches averaging 99.7–100% anhydrous; the additive form is about 1.34% 25-OH-D3. The strain shares a parent with ATC 1562, the basis of earlier opinions. Authorisation history: EFSA opinion 2005; Reg. (EC) 887/2009; renewal for poultry and pigs plus extension to ruminants under Implementing Reg. (EU) 2024/1070 (12 Apr 2024); fish and all other species opinion adopted 20 May 2025. Applicant: DSM Nutritional Products Ltd, Kaiseraugst. — [EFSA Journal 2025;23(6):e9479 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12159549/); [EFSA 2023 ruminants (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10394675/); [EUR-Lex 2024/1070](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ:L_202401070)
- EFSA confirmed Hy-D for all animal species including fish (reported Jul 2025), with EU commercial availability for all species expected by early 2026. — [FoodIngredientsFirst, 11 Jul 2025](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/efsa-approves-hy-d-vitamin-d-animal-health.html)
- A Chinese sell-side report on Garden Bio describes the "biofermentation route": 5,7,24-cholestatrienol by fermentation, then epoxidation and reduction chemistry to 25-hydroxy-7-dehydrocholesterol. — [Garden Bio research report (Zhihu repost), via search summary](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/605861455)
- Garden Bio (Apr 2025 IR) disclosed long-term purchase agreements with DSM for cholesterol, 7-DHC and 25-OH-D3. So DSM also buys lanolin-chain intermediates from Garden. — [Garden Bio IR record 2025-001, 7 Apr 2025](http://notice.10jqka.com.cn/api/pdf/5a136c83271a71f8.pdf)
- dsm-firmenich is divesting Animal Nutrition & Health to CVC (about €2.2bn, retaining 20%), and Vitamins sit in a separate "Essential Products Company." — [FoodIngredientsFirst](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/dsm-firmenich-divests-animal-nutrition-cvc.html)

**China commercial moves**
- Ruipu Bio (瑞普生物) acquired Ruihe Bio to strengthen 25-OH-D3 industrialisation. It cites a "high-yield calcifediol cell factory" chosen as a national demonstration scenario, in cooperation with the CAS Tianjin Institute of Industrial Biotechnology. Separately, Ruihe is building **50 t/yr of AD/ADD from phytosterols** by fermentation (construction from Jun 2025, completion Dec 2027) within RMB 570m of synthetic-biology projects. — [Pharnexcloud, May 2025](https://www.pharnexcloud.com/zixun/sx_47012)
- Search found no Chinese company announcing tonne-scale fermentation 7-DHC for D3. Garden Bio's 180 t 7-DHC capacity (Jinxi park) is chemical, from NF-grade lanolin cholesterol. — [search summary of Garden Bio research report](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/605861455)
- Lallemand's Lalmin Vita D is a dried, inactivated whole-cell yeast in which vitamin D is produced from endogenous ergosterol. Ergosterol photolysis normally gives D2, so this is likely a D2 product (my inference). — [Nutraceutical Business Review](https://nutraceuticalbusinessreview.com/lalmin-a-natural-fermentation-based-alternative-to-conventional)

### Inferences
- Titer is no longer the main barrier. At about 4 g/L, one 100 m³ batch would yield about 400 kg of 7-DHC (my arithmetic), which is large against vegan-D3 demand. The constraints are more likely:
  - (a) cost against very cheap lanolin cholesterol. Feed-grade D3 sold at RMB 230–250/kg for 500,000 IU/g in Apr 2025, about USD 0.065/MIU.
  - (b) downstream recovery of intracellular sterols, and selective photoconversion.
  - (c) GMO-labelling and "natural/vegan" positioning issues for supplements.
  - (d) regulation. China does not yet have rules covering plant-derived D3 (Section 5).
- The dsm-firmenich Hy-D precedent shows that yeast sterol fermentation plus chemistry is commercially viable when the product is higher value (25-OH-D3) and goes into a regulated feed channel.
- "Fermentation D3" in marketing more often refers to the phytosterol route (Section 4), where a Mycobacterium fermentation step is used, than to yeast 7-DHC.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence of commercial yeast-7-DHC D3 from Garden Bio, Kingdomway, NHU, Zhejiang Medicine, Bluepha, Cathay or Evolva. The status of 欣贝莱生物 (a Chinese synbio firm discussed in a Zhihu post on 7-DHC, page blocked) is unknown.
- I found no published cost-of-goods comparison (USD/kg) for fermentation 7-DHC against chemical 7-DHC from lanolin cholesterol.
- dsm-firmenich: no human-nutrition vegan D3 launch found. Whether its human calcifediol (ampli-D) uses the yeast route was not confirmed.

## 4. Phytosterol-derived cholesterol to D3 ("plant-source D3"): who does it, and is it chemical or microbial?

### Takeaway
This is the most industrialised non-animal D3 route and the one closest to Symbiotec's platform. China's Kerui Bio (科瑞生物, 832780.NQ), controlled by Zhejiang Ang Likang (昂利康, 002940.SZ) since 2023, makes plant-source cholesterol from phytosterols by **"biofermentation + multi-step chemical synthesis."** It reports 30 t/yr, and its subsidiary Chundi (淳迪) makes plant-source D3, 25-OH-D3 and D2. The route is hybrid: microbial side-chain cleavage of phytosterol to a C22 intermediate (BA, the same Mycobacterium biotransformation family as AD/ADD/9-OH-AD), then chemical rebuilding of the cholesterol side chain, chemical 7-dehydrogenation, and UV photolysis. Evonik (PhytoChol, Germany) and Innobio (pine-oil D3, China) are other plant-sterol players.

### Cited Findings
**Kerui Bio / Ang Likang / Chundi (China)**

- The route is hybrid. The first step (phytosterol → C22 BA by Mycobacterium) is the same industrial biotransformation family Symbiotec runs for AD/ADD/9-OH-AD, but it needs a different strain or knock-outs that stop side-chain degradation at C22. The cholesterol side-chain build, 7-dehydrogenation and UV/thermal isomerisation are classical chemistry and photochemistry.
- Capacity consistency check: 30 t/yr plant cholesterol is small against lanolin cholesterol chains (Garden alone has 180 t 7-DHC). Chundi's reported 10 t D3 crystal plus 10 t 25-OH-D3 crystal lines would need more cholesterol than 30 t at typical multi-step yields. So either those are nameplate figures, or some lines also run lanolin cholesterol. Treat them as unverified.
- Economics: Chundi's FY2023 revenue (about USD 10m) and the 2025 decline in Kerui's specialty intermediates suggest a small, lumpy, customer-concentrated market. Plant cholesterol for cosmetics and LNPs may be worth as much as the D3 itself.

## 5. Market size, price premium, growth, end markets, certification and regulation

### Takeaway
Market-research estimates are mutually inconsistent and low-reliability. They range from about USD 0.1bn (lichen D3, 2025) to USD 1.2–1.5bn ("vegan D3," 2025), against a total D3 market of about USD 1.8bn; the higher figures are almost certainly finished-product retail values. The verifiable price signal is a retail premium of about 1.5–3.5x per dose. At ingredient level, a modeled corridor puts lichen D3 oil (100,000 IU/g) at USD 150–300/kg against USD 10–18/kg for commodity D3 powder at the same potency, roughly 8–30x per MIU. Regulatory gating is favourable in the US (self-GRAS) and India (FSSAI approvals 2025–2026), unclear in the EU, and restrictive in China.

### Inferences
- Per-MIU premium (my arithmetic on the cited figures):
  - Lichen oil at USD 150–300/kg for 100 MIU/kg works out to USD 1.5–3.0/MIU.
  - Commodity 100,000 IU/g D3 powder is USD 0.10–0.18/MIU, an 8–30x premium.
  - Against feed-grade (RMB 240/kg ÷ 500 MIU/kg, about RMB 0.48, or USD 0.067/MIU) the gap is 20–45x, but feed grade is not a like-for-like comparator.
  - Retail premiums are compressed to about 1.5–3.5x because ingredient cost is a tiny share of a capsule's price.
- At USD 1.5–3/MIU, a vegan supplement brand pays only fractions of a US cent more per 1,000–5,000 IU dose. Premium vegan D3 is therefore not price-elastic at the brand level. Volume is limited by supply credibility, certification and the food-fortification channel (plant milks), where cost matters more.
- India is now one of the few markets with explicit regulator approvals for plant D3 (two in 2025–2026), which is relevant for any Indian entrant. China remains closed domestically, which pushes Chinese plant-D3 makers (Kerui/Chundi, Innobio) to export markets.

## 6. Competing non-animal or alternative forms: vitamin D2 (mushroom/yeast UV) and 25-OH-D3 (calcifediol) by fermentation or biocatalysis

### Takeaway
D2 is the incumbent vegan option (UV-treated mushrooms and yeast ergosterol, e.g., Lallemand). It is cheap to make sustainably, but marketers argue it is less effective than D3, which drives demand for vegan D3. 25-OH-D3 is the higher-value adjacent molecule. Fermentation is already commercial there (dsm-firmenich yeast route), China is adding capacity (Garden Bio, Chundi plant-source 25-OH-D3, Ruipu/TIB "calcifediol cell factory"), and the EU extended Hy-D to all animal species in 2025–2026.

### Cited Findings
- Vegans have traditionally used D2 from mushrooms, "which is less bioavailable than D3" (trade press framing). AIDP cites a 2016 *JCEM* study and a 2012 *AJCN* study finding D3 raises serum 25-OH-D more effectively than D2. — [NutraIngredients 2025](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/07/22/pine-derived-vitamin-d3-approved-as-vegan-ingredient-from-fssai); [NutraIngredients 2019](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2019/04/29/AIDP-There-is-a-significant-market-potential-for-vegan-D3/)
- Yeast D2 (USP) is modeled at USD 200–400/kg (unit potency unspecified). — [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/search/vitamin-d-ingredients-price-evidence-world-2026/)
- Lallemand Lalmin Vita D: inactivated whole-cell yeast, with vitamin D made from endogenous ergosterol. — [NBR](https://nutraceuticalbusinessreview.com/lalmin-a-natural-fermentation-based-alternative-to-conventional)
- Garden Bio (Apr 2025) 25-OH-D3 design capacity: crystal 15.6 t/yr, "原" (likely crude/technical) 26 t/yr, powder 1,200 t/yr. Management says 25-OH-D3 "has a trend of replacing ordinary D3," mainly in feed and food additives. — [Garden Bio IR 2025-001](http://notice.10jqka.com.cn/api/pdf/5a136c83271a71f8.pdf)
- Ang Likang: 25-OH-D3 prices are less cyclical than D3 because there are few producers and demand is growing. Chundi supplies plant-source 25-OH-D3 to overseas customers, with a 5-year European offtake. — [Ang Likang 2025 AR summary](https://disc.static.szse.cn/download/disc/disk03/finalpage/2026-04-29/d1db6d43-291e-489d-a3b0-d99559193f23.PDF); [PopCJ 2024](https://www.popcj.com/depth/0642502404363946)
- dsm-firmenich Hy-D (yeast trienol route) is EU-authorised for poultry, pigs and ruminants (Reg. 2024/1070), with an EFSA opinion for fish and all other species (May 2025) and availability expected early 2026. — [EFSA 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12159549/); [FoodIngredientsFirst](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/efsa-approves-hy-d-vitamin-d-animal-health.html)

### Inferences
- For a steroid-fermentation company, 25-OH-D3 and active-vitamin-D APIs (calcifediol, calcitriol, alfacalcidol) may be a more valuable target than vegan D3 itself. They carry higher value per kg, less cyclical pricing and regulated barriers. Kerui is explicitly pursuing them (骨化醇 series in development).

### Gaps
- I found no reliable 2026 price for 25-OH-D3 (USD/kg) or D2.

## 7. Relevance to Symbiotec Pharmalab (Indore): could a phytosterol-fermentation steroid API maker play in vegan or fermentation D3?

### Takeaway
Yes in principle. The proven industrial non-animal D3 route (Kerui/Chundi in China, likely Innobio's pine D3) starts with the same microbial phytosterol side-chain cleavage Symbiotec runs, followed by chemistry Symbiotec already practises. But the addressable vegan-D3 pool appears small (tens of USD millions at ingredient level), customer-concentrated and contested. Kerui/Chundi (China), Fermenta (India, FSSAI-approved Jul 2026, Dahej capex), Innobio, Evonik (plant cholesterol) and lichen brands already occupy it. The more attractive adjacencies are plant-source cholesterol for LNP/cosmetic excipients and 25-OH-D3 or active vitamin D APIs.

### Cited Findings
- Kerui's model: phytosterol → biofermentation → multi-step chemistry → plant-source cholesterol → D3, 25-OH-D3 and D2, sold to overseas pharma, nutrition and feed customers. It is integrated with steroid APIs (progesterone, finasteride, dutasteride) on the same phytosterol platform. — [Ang Likang 2025 AR summary](https://disc.static.szse.cn/download/disc/disk03/finalpage/2026-04-29/d1db6d43-291e-489d-a3b0-d99559193f23.PDF); [PopCJ](https://www.popcj.com/depth/0642502404363946)
- Chundi FY2023 revenue RMB 71.6m at 21.7% net margin; Kerui specialty intermediates fell in 2025 on overseas demand fluctuations. — [PopCJ](https://www.popcj.com/depth/0642502404363946); [Ang Likang 2025 AR](https://disc.static.szse.cn/download/disc/disk03/finalpage/2026-04-29/d1db6d43-291e-489d-a3b0-d99559193f23.PDF)
- Indian competitor Fermenta Biotech (an established lanolin-D3 maker): INR 110 crore Dahej capex (FY26–27) including plant-based D3, an Indian process patent, and FSSAI approval in Jul 2026. — [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/12/11/dcm6-fermenta-biotech-limited.amp.html); [vegconomist](https://vegconomist.com/ingredients/fermenta-biotech-receives-indian-approval-plant-based-vitamin-d3-ingredient/)
- Chinese steroid fermenters are adding phytosterol capacity, e.g., Ruipu/Ruihe at 50 t/yr AD/ADD by Dec 2027, alongside calcifediol cell-factory work. — [Pharnexcloud 2025](https://www.pharnexcloud.com/zixun/sx_47012)
- Plant-derived cholesterol has a separate pharma-excipient market (mRNA-LNP), served by Evonik at Hanau. — [Evonik](https://healthcare.evonik.com/en/drugdelivery/mrna-and-gene-delivery/phytochol)

### Inferences
- **Technical fit:**
  - Symbiotec would need a C22-stopping Mycobacterium strain (BA/4-HBC/HPD) rather than its AD/ADD/9-OH-AD strains.
  - Its own BA-to-cholesterol side-chain chemistry, or a licence, would be required; TIB-CAS and Kerui hold Chinese patents.
  - It would also need 7-DHC chemistry and UV photoreactor / previtamin isomerisation know-how. That is a distinct photochemical competence typically held by D3 specialists (Garden, Fermenta, DSM, Dishman NL).
- **Commercial fit:**
  - Vegan D3 ingredient value is small. Even at USD 1.5–3/MIU, a few hundred kg-equivalent of 100,000 IU/g oil is only USD tens of thousands to low millions.
  - Buyers need origin verification (isotope), vegan, halal and kosher certification, and FSSAI/self-GRAS dossiers.
  - India now has a domestic incumbent (Fermenta).
- **Better angles:**
  - (a) supplying plant-source cholesterol (cosmetics, LNP excipient; Evonik and Kerui as benchmarks);
  - (b) BA/C22 intermediates to D3 and progesterone makers;
  - (c) 25-OH-D3 and active vitamin D APIs, which have higher value per kg and less cyclical pricing.
- **Risk:** Chinese capacity (Kerui BA 800 t project, Ruipu) and dsm-firmenich's yeast route could compress margins in any of these.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether Symbiotec has disclosed any vitamin D, cholesterol or BA programme.
- There is no public cost curve comparing phytosterol-cholesterol-D3 with lanolin-cholesterol-D3. The phytosterol price and route yields (cholesterol → 7-DHC → D3) needed for a margin model were not found.

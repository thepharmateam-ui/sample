## Q1. Symbiotec Pharmalab: business, sites, capacity, financials, exports, customers, IPO

### Takeaway
Symbiotec is a listed (since 1 Sep 2026) Indore-based corticosteroid and steroid-hormone API maker with FY26 revenue of ₹869 cr and EBITDA of ₹238 cr (27.4% margin). Its core skill is fermenting soy phytosterols into steroid KSMs (bisnor alcohol, 9-OH-AD, AD). It holds about 38% global volume share in corticosteroids. It has spent more than ₹1,475 cr of gross capex, mostly on new verticals (biotech CDMO at Ujjain, double-chamber vials at Mhow) that do not yet earn revenue, so earnings were dragged down in Q1FY27.

### Inferences
- Likely route (inference, not stated): "plant path", "phytosterols through a 3-stage bioprocess", a 40 MIU/g Ph.Eur. grade and "photochemistry-based" together point to phytosterol → C22 side-chain intermediate (bisnor alcohol, which Symbiotec already ferments in-house) → chemical rebuild of the cholesterol side chain → 7-DHC → UV photolysis and thermal isomerisation → crystalline D3. Possible alternatives are buying plant-derived cholesterol, or an all-chemical phytosterol→cholesterol route like Fermenta's patent (see Q3). The D2 claim ("yeast-derived") fits UV photolysis of yeast ergosterol, which uses the same photochemistry asset.
- The FY28 commissioning date, and the fact that vitamin D3 is absent from Q1FY27 commentary, imply D3 contributes no revenue in FY27 and is immaterial in ICICI's FY29E model (API ₹1,062 cr in FY29E, mostly legacy steroids).
- The US$600 mn "vegan D3" market size is a management figure. It looks high next to Fermenta's entire D3 revenue (about US$48 mn in FY26) and the small plant-based capacities being built (Fermenta about 1 tpa; Kerey/Chundi lines of about 10 t, see Q3). Analysts should treat it with scepticism until it is reconciled with an independent source.
- No analogue (calcifediol, calcitriol) ambition has been stated. Since Symbiotec already holds 43 US DMFs and 23 CEPs in steroids, the regulatory skill set carries over, but no D-analogue DMF exists to my knowledge.

## Q3. Technical adjacency: phytosterol fermentation → cholesterol / 7-DHC → vitamin D3 → analogues; which steroid players elsewhere also make vitamin D

### Takeaway
Symbiotec's fermentation removes the phytosterol side chain to make C19/C22 KSMs. Cholesterol (C27) needs that side chain kept or rebuilt, so the step from phytosterol to cholesterol is mainly chemical: Fermenta's patented route uses ozonolysis and Grignard on crude phytosterol, and a bisnor-alcohol (C22) handle can also be extended. From cholesterol onward (allylic bromination → 7-DHC → UV photolysis → crystallisation), the chemistry is the kind Symbiotec already runs: bromination/dehydrobromination, photochemistry and steroid purification. Proof that this model works: Hunan Kerey (科瑞生物), a Chinese phytosterol-based steroid intermediate maker, already sells plant-source cholesterol (30 t/yr), plant D3, 25-OH-D3, and is building an analogue pipeline. Analogues (calcitriol, paricalcitol, etc.) are a separate, low-volume, high-value, DMF-driven game led by Dishman/Carbogen Amcis, Formosa Labs and the Chinese.

### Cited Findings
**Standard animal route vs plant routes**
- Commercial D3 traditionally starts from animal cholesterol, mostly from lanolin (wool grease), which is converted to 7-DHC and UV-irradiated. Most plants make little cholesterol, so plant routes need chemical conversion of phytosterols — [search synthesis of patent and literature sources incl. US20110207952A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20110207952A1/en); [PMC11606407](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11606407/)
- **Fermenta patent EP3906247A1** "Synthesis of cholesterol and vitamin D3 from phytosterols". Applicant Fermenta Biotech Ltd; priority 9 Jul 2019 (IN201921027409); PCT filed 2 Jul 2020; status pending. Route from crude phytosterol (stigmasterol/β-sitosterol/campesterol mix): (a) tosylation → (b) methanolysis to the i-steroid methyl ether → (c) ozonolysis at −50 to −90 °C, then NaBH4 → C22 20-hydroxymethyl alcohol → (d) tosylation → (e) Grignard coupling with isopentyl bromide/CuBr·Me2S → i-cholesteryl methyl ether → (f) hydrolysis → cholesterol → acetylation → (g) allylic bromination (DDH) and TBAF elimination → 7-DHC acetate → (h) hydrolysis → 7-DHC → (i) high-pressure Hg lamp photolysis with a bithiophene sensitiser → vitamin D3. Example step yields: cholesterol step 97%; 7-DHC 54% over bromination/elimination/hydrolysis, with unreacted material recycled — [Google Patents EP3906247A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3906247A1/en)
- Fermenta's VITADEE Green is described by the company as "Phytosterol-derived vegan alternative", at 40 MIU/g, with "Higher purity & stability vs lichen-derived options" — [Fermenta Q1FY27 presentation](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Investor-PresentationQ1FY27.pdf). One trade outlet instead calls VITADEE Green "lichen-derived" — [vegconomist](https://vegconomist.com/ingredients/fermenta-biotech-receives-indian-approval-plant-based-vitamin-d3-ingredient/). This conflicts with the company's own description and patent.

**Microbial side of the adjacency (what Symbiotec already does)**
- 22-hydroxy-23,24-bisnorchol-4-en-3-one (4-HBC, "bisnor alcohol", BA) is a C22 steroid made as a side-chain-degradation end product of phytosterol catabolism by engineered Mycobacterium/Mycolicibacterium. It is a key precursor for progestogens and corticoids — [Microb Cell Fact 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12934-017-0705-x); [Microb Cell Fact 2025, M. neoaurum ZS-15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12888175/). Symbiotec names bisnor alcohol as an in-house fermentation building block — [RHP](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf)
- Recent strain work targets C22 side-chain intermediates and 9,22-dihydroxy variants from phytosterols — [IJMS 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11011972/); [PMC12409849](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12409849/)

**Biocatalytic hydroxylation (analogue adjacency)**
- The cytochrome P450 Vdh from Pseudonocardia autotrophica hydroxylates D3 at C25 and then C1α to give calcitriol. ILDONG Pharmaceutical (Korea) reported about 10.4 mg/L calcitriol (10.4% yield) in a 75 L fermenter. There is patent coverage of whole-cell production of calcifediol and calcitriol, but no evidence of commercial scale — [Korean J. ### Inferences
- **Proximity score (inference):** (1) Phytosterol → cholesterol is a moderate step for Symbiotec. Its fermentation removes the side chain rather than keeping it, but its in-house BA (C22 alcohol) is a natural handle for rebuilding the isooctyl side chain. Grignard and tosylation are already in its toolkit (the RHP lists Grignard, bromination, debromination and hydrogenation). (2) Cholesterol → 7-DHC (allylic bromination/dehydrobromination) is close to its existing chemistry. (3) 7-DHC → D3 (UV photolysis, thermal isomerisation, chromatography and crystallisation to 40 MIU/g) uses photochemistry it already claims (photo reactor in R&D). The new parts are scale-up of photoreactors and the handling and stability of an air- and light-sensitive secosteroid.
- **Background chemistry, not sourced in this research:** active analogues (calcitriol, alfacalcidol, paricalcitol, calcipotriol, maxacalcitol, eldecalcitol) are typically made either by 1α/25-functionalisation of D3/D2 or 7-DHC derivatives, or by convergent synthesis joining a CD-ring synthon (often from degradation of vitamin D2 or steroid side chains) with an A-ring fragment. Volumes are kg-scale and need potent-compound handling. The skill transfer from steroid API chemistry is real, but the product set, DMFs and customers are new.
- Kerey is the closest global analogue to a "Symbiotec-in-vitamin-D" case: a phytosterol steroid intermediate player that added plant cholesterol, plant D3 and 25-OH-D3, and is moving into analogues. Its D-business scale (Chundi revenue of about RMB 72 mn in 2023, about ₹85 cr at roughly ₹11.8/RMB, my conversion) is a useful reference point for what a niche plant-D business can earn.
- Plant cholesterol also has a non-vitamin D outlet: injectable-grade excipient for lipid nanoparticles (mRNA). Kerey sells it this way, and it gives optionality beyond D3.

### Gaps
- I could not verify Kerey's exact chemical route (whether it uses BA or another intermediate) or its 2025–26 figures.
- I found no public evidence that Symbiotec produces cholesterol or 7-DHC.
- I did not verify NHU, Kingdomway or Zhejiang Medicine D3 capacities against primary filings. Kingdomway's use of phytosterol is not confirmed.
- I found no evidence of commercial-scale biocatalytic 25-hydroxylation outside DSM's undisclosed route.

## Q4. Indian vitamin D3 landscape: Fermenta, other makers or entrants, import dependence on China, demand, FSSAI, PLI

### Takeaway
Fermenta Biotech is effectively India's only scaled, backward-integrated (cholesterol-to-D3) maker: FY26 D3 revenue was ₹401 cr out of ₹538 cr core revenue. Feed and food premixes drive tonnage; pharma and supplements drive value and regulatory differentiation. Fermenta's roughly 1 tpa plant-based D3 line is large relative to Indian pharma demand and is aimed at global vegan supplement and fortification markets.
- The Chinese "capacity" figures (e.g., 14,740 t global, 3,600 t Garden) are very likely stated in feed-grade product units (e.g., D3 500,000 IU/g, about 1/80th of crystalline potency), not pure cholecalciferol. If so, global crystalline-equivalent capacity is only about 180–200 t. This is my reading and is unverified; analysts should check the basis before comparing with Fermenta's or Symbiotec's figures.
- The FSSAI rule allowing only plant-based vitamin D for fortification is a structural advantage in India for plant-source D3 (Fermenta now; Symbiotec from about FY28 if it executes). It matters most if fortification becomes mandatory. D2 (yeast) also qualifies, which may explain why Symbiotec lists a "developed" D2.
- Fermenta is the domestic incumbent with a 2019-priority patent on the phytosterol→D3 chemical route and a head start on FSSAI approval. A Symbiotec entry would compete directly with it in "Indian plant-source D3". Freedom-to-operate around EP3906247 (and any granted Indian patent) is a diligence item.

## Q6. Economic case and risks for a steroid fermentation player (Symbiotec) entering D3

### Takeaway
Symbiotec appears to be aiming at the right niche: plant-origin, pharma-grade (40 MIU/g, Ph.Eur.) D3 for vegan supplements and FSSAI fortification. Commodity feed-grade D3 is controlled by China, with 83% of capacity, an integrated lanolin cholesterol chain and RMB 50–250/kg price swings. Symbiotec's assets (in-house phytosterol KSMs, photochemistry, a steroid regulatory track record with 43 DMFs and 23 CEPs) give it a credible cost and quality route. However, the opportunity is small relative to its ₹870 cr revenue base. It arrives late (capacity from FY28) against Fermenta and Kerey, and it carries the same cyclicality and qualification risks seen in Fermenta's results.

### Cited Findings
- **China cost leadership:** China holds about 83% of global D3 capacity (2024e). Garden controls more than half of NF cholesterol capacity and has 3,600 t of D3 capacity, with gross margins of about 60–70% — [Tianfeng via Sina](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2024-12-17/doc-inczuerp4854682.shtml). Chinese leaders such as Garden and NHU "compete aggressively on price" (market-research claim) — [GMI Insights](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/vitamin-d3-cholecalciferol-market) (low reliability)
- **Cyclicality:** the D3 price fell from about RMB 110 to below 60–80/kg in 2022, bottomed at about RMB 50/kg in 2023 and rebounded to about RMB 250/kg in Q3 2024. Garden's 2023 net profit fell 49.87% — [Tianfeng via Sina](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2024-12-17/doc-inczuerp4854682.shtml). Fermenta's Q1FY27 animal-nutrition realisation fell 40% YoY and volumes fell 52% QoQ — [Fermenta Q1FY27](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Investor-PresentationQ1FY27.pdf)
- **Niche economics evidence:** Kerey's plant-D subsidiary Chundi earned RMB 71.6 mn revenue at a 21.7% net margin in 2023, with a 5-year European 25-OH-D3 contract — [popcj](https://www.popcj.com/depth/0642502404363946). Fermenta is spending about ₹60 cr for about 1 tpa plant-based D3 plus 50 kg of derivatives — [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/biotech/fermenta-biotech-to-invest-rs-110-crore-for-dahej-plant-expansion-18512). Symbiotec management calls vegan D3 a US$600 mn niche growing about 10% with "few competitors" — [ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)
- **Vegan premium:** the only cost comparisons found are dated or promotional. A 2012 lichen-D3 supplier said lichen D3 is "more expensive than lanolin-source... but not massively so" — [NutraIngredients-USA, 2012](https://nutraingredients-usa.com/Article/2012/12/07/Lichen-based-vegan-vitamin-D3-gains-momentum-as-Nordic-Naturals-introduces-new-product). Symbiotec's Ujjain site holds FSSAI/FSSC — [RHP](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf)
- **Symbiotec's execution context:** about ₹870 cr of capex at Ujjain and Mhow for new businesses whose revenue starts over 6–18 months. Delay risk is flagged as the top risk — [ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf). Q1FY27 consolidated EBITDA margin fell to 21% on pre-operating costs — [Arthneeti](https://www.arthneeti.com/insights/symbiotec-pharma-q1-fy27-earnings)

### Inferences
- **Bull case (inference):** (i) Feedstock: Symbiotec already buys soy phytosterols at scale (e.g., from Cargill) and ferments BA in-house, so a plant cholesterol route would not depend on Chinese lanolin cholesterol, which Garden dominates. (ii) Quality: a 40 MIU/g Ph.Eur. crystalline product can serve pharma (60K IU sachets, drops) and vegan supplements, where provenance claims (non-animal, non-lichen, non-China) can command a premium. (iii) Regulation: FSSAI's plant-only rule for vitamin D fortification gives a domestic pull. (iv) Optionality: plant cholesterol for LNP/mRNA excipients (as Kerey sells), 25-OH-D3/calcifediol, and later analogues using its DMF/CEP skills.
- **Bear case (inference):** (i) The addressable pharma volume is tiny in tonnage terms, so even a premium-priced line (Fermenta-like 1 tpa) is probably a two-digit ₹ cr revenue opportunity, not a needle-mover for a ₹870 cr company.

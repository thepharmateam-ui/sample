## Q1. What does the Concord Biotech RHP (F&S) say about the global pharma, global API, fermentation API (and sub-segments), Indian API and Indian fermentation API markets?

### Takeaway
Concord's RHP is dated 27-Jul-2023. Its industry section draws on F&S, "Overview of the Global Fermentation API and Formulations Industry", 30-Jun-2023. It sizes:
- **Global API market:** $219bn in 2022, rising to $278bn in 2026 (6.1% CAGR).
- **Global small-molecule fermentation API market:** about $11bn in 2022 (the chart shows $12bn), rising to about $14bn in 2026 (3.6% CAGR). By volume it goes from 51.5k MT to 61.7k MT (4.6%).
- **Indian API market:** $17bn in 2022, of which exports are $5bn and domestic consumption $12bn. The domestic part is forecast at an 11.1% CAGR to 2026.

The RHP gives no standalone Indian fermentation API market size. It says China supplied about 70% of small-molecule fermentation API until 2021 and more than 50% today.

### Inferences
- **Fermentation share of global API:** Concord's own numbers put small-molecule fermentation API at only about 5% of the global API market in 2022 ($11–12bn of $219bn). Its growth (3.6%) is well below the overall API market (6.1%). The "growth" story in the RHP rests on immunosuppressants and oncology, not the category.
- **Unsized remainder:** the three sized sub-segments (immunosuppressants about $1bn, oncology about $1.4bn, anti-infectives about $3.2bn) add up to about $5.6bn, roughly half of the $11–12bn total. The rest (statins, hormones/steroids, vitamins and others) is not broken out in the Concord RHP.
- **India's share of global API, 2022 (my arithmetic on Concord numbers):** India's total API market was about 7.8% of the global API market ($17bn / $219bn). API exports alone were about 2.3% ($5bn / $219bn).

### Gaps
- The Concord RHP has no Indian fermentation API market size, no statin or antifungal sub-segment value, and no corticosteroid or steroid-hormone sizing.
- The 2022 value of the fermentation market is internally inconsistent ($11bn in the text vs $12bn in the chart).
- The full standalone F&S report was not reviewed; only the RHP excerpt was.

## Q2. What do other Indian IPO documents (Symbiotec DRHP/RHP, Anthem RHP, others) add on API market sizing?

### Takeaway
Symbiotec's DRHP (Dec-2025) and RHP (Aug-2026) both use F&S, "Independent Market Research on the Overview of the Global API Market and Fermentation Technologies". The RHP figures are:
- **Global API market:** $305.5bn in 2025, rising to $424.6bn in 2030 (6.8% CAGR).
- **Global fermentation API market (small plus large molecule):** $100.4bn in 2025, rising to $162.5bn in 2030 (10.1%). Small molecules are only $18.1bn of this (5.5% CAGR), and the "classical fermentation" basket tied to Symbiotec is just $491.9m.
- **India API exports:** $4.9bn in 2025, rising to $6.4bn in 2030 (5.5%).

Anthem's F&S report (Dec-2024) puts global API at $262.2bn in 2023, rising to $378.3bn in 2028 (7.6%). Across F&S vintages the global API base moves sharply ($219bn for 2022 in Concord vs $262bn for 2023 in Anthem). The small-molecule fermentation base differs by about 30–50% between the Concord and Symbiotec reports, so cross-document comparisons need care.

## Q4. What drivers and restraints are cited (China+1, PLI for fermentation KSMs/APIs, patent expiries, biosimilars vs fermentation)?

### Takeaway
Both F&S reports cite the same tailwinds:
- China+1 de-risking after Chinese environmental shutdowns and COVID.
- India's PLI and bulk-drug-park schemes, including fermentation-specific target segments.
- Generic and biosimilar loss-of-exclusivity.
- Outsourcing to CDMOs.
- The cost and sustainability advantages of fermentation.

Restraints are regulatory and environmental stringency, high capex and scale-up barriers, supply-chain fragility and generic price pressure. For steroids specifically, F&S frames growth as low single digit, driven by autoimmune disease and HRT demand and capped by generic pricing and WHI-era safety concerns.

India's import dependence on China for APIs, KSMs and DIs actually rose to about 74% in FY25 even as PLI capacity came online.

### Cited Findings
- **Concord RHP: API market drivers** — [Concord RHP p.108](https://www.jefferies.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/10/Concord-Biotech-limited-RHP.pdf):
  - Volume: chronic disease, diagnosis rates and emerging markets.
  - Value: complex and high-potency APIs. 227 NCEs and 88 NBEs were approved over 2016–2022, and about 25% of NCEs in development are highly potent.
- **Concord RHP: API market restraints:** stringent cGMP and impurity regulation (for example, carcinogenic impurities), green-chemistry costs, the need for specialization in complex APIs, and supply-chain diversification strain — [Concord RHP pp.108–109](https://www.jefferies.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/10/Concord-Biotech-limited-RHP.pdf).
- **Concord RHP: fermentation drivers and trends** — [Concord RHP pp.119–120](https://www.jefferies.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/10/Concord-Biotech-limited-RHP.pdf):
  - Low-cost agricultural feedstocks and fewer toxic solvents.
  - Loss of exclusivity: 22 fermentation biologics (about $60bn) and about 4 fermentation small molecules (about $280m) in 2023–25.
  - CRISPR and strain improvement.
  - Outsourcing to CDMOs.
  - "China's Dominance Being Replaced by India's Growth".
  - High entry barriers.
- **Symbiotec RHP: fermentation API drivers** — [Symbiotec RHP pp.188–189](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf):
  - Technology: synthetic biology, CRISPR, AI bioprocess.
  - Cost efficiency.
  - "Strategic Shift from China to India", supported by PLI and China+1.
  - A favourable Indian regulatory environment.
  - Demand for complex molecules.
  - CDMO outsourcing.
  - Sustainability.

- **Symbiotec RHP: steroid-specific drivers** — [Symbiotec RHP pp.191–193](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf):
  - Rising autoimmune and inflammatory disease (up to 10% of people in industrialized countries).

  - HRT, menopause and hypogonadism demand, and bioidentical and transdermal products.
- **Symbiotec RHP: steroid-specific restraints:** "price pressure from generics" in hormones. For conjugated estrogens: WHI safety concerns and a shift to estradiol, transdermal and non-hormonal products (SSRIs, NK3 antagonists) — [Symbiotec RHP pp.193, 197–198](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf).

  - Manufacturing cost indexed to the USA: India 30–50%, China 45–55% — [Symbiotec RHP p.211](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf).

### Inferences
- **China+1 is real but slow:** China's share of Indian API/KSM imports rose (68% to 74%) between FY21 and FY25 despite PLI.
- **Where PLI fermentation capacity is going:** the commissioned capacity is mainly high-volume antibiotics (Pen-G, clavulanic acid). Steroid fermentation intermediates (AD and related) are not a named PLI focus.
- **Symbiotec's China exposure:** its in-house phytosterol→AD route is a differentiator vs import-dependent Indian peers. Its own China raw-material imports still swing widely (24–53% of total expenses over FY24–26).
- **Where fermentation growth sits:** the fermentation growth narrative in all three F&S reports is dominated by biologics and biosimilars, not small-molecule or steroid fermentation. For Symbiotec's legacy API business, the relevant growth rates are 1–4%; the 10%+ fermentation CAGRs apply to its new biotech CDMO vertical (insulin, alternative proteins), not its steroid APIs.

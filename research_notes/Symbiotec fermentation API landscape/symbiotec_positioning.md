## 2. Business profile: products, fermentation vs. chemistry steps, capacities, sites, R&D, customers, exports, approvals

### Takeaway
Symbiotec is a backward-integrated corticosteroid and steroidal-hormone API maker: 60+ APIs, 43 US DMFs and 23 CEPs as of 31-Mar-2026. APIs were 96% of FY26 revenue. Fermentation sits upstream only, as microbial biotransformation of soy-derived phytosterols into steroid building blocks (bisnor alcohol, 9-OH-AD, AD). Every step from those building blocks to the APIs is multi-step chemical synthesis. Operating fermentation capacity is 300 KL at Pithampur. A new 400 KL Ujjain plant (700 KL in total) is aimed at non-pharma and biologics CDMO, not at the steroid API business. Exports were 67% of FY26 revenue, led by Europe (29%).

### Cited Findings
**Portfolio and positioning**
- "Over 60 corticosteroid and steroidal-hormone APIs", including hydrocortisones, betamethasones, methylprednisolones, progesterones, estrogens and testosterones. End uses: critical care, respiratory, dermatology, pain, oncology and gynaecology. 43 US DMFs and 23 CEPs as of 31-Mar-2026; 66 DMFs/CEPs in total, vs 64 in FY25 and FY24 — [RHP, Our Business, pp. 218–224](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)

This sits uneasily with the "60+ APIs" claim elsewhere in the RHP — [RHP, Industry Overview, p. 202](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)

**Which steps are fermentation and which are chemistry (RHP case study)**
- "The initial stages of API manufacturing involve bioconversion of phytosterol to the desired starting materials or building blocks like **bisnor alcohol, 9-hydroxy androstenedione or androstenedione**. These building blocks are further converted into APIs through multiple appropriate chemical transformations." The listed transformations include acetylation, Birch reduction, bromination, chlorosilylation, cyanation, epoxidation, fluorination, Grignard, hydrogenation, hydroxylation, oxidation, sulfonylation and thio-acid formation — [RHP, Our Business, Case Studies, p. 240](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)
- **Worked example from the RHP:**
  - A KSM is made from 9-OH-AD by cyanation, chlorosilylation, rearrangement and dehydration.
  - Bromination–debromination gives crude API 1. Saponification gives API 2.
  - Esterification gives API 3. Sodiumization gives API 4.
  - So one fermentation-derived building block feeds a chain of four chemically made APIs — [RHP p. 240](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)
- **Fermentation modalities claimed:**
  - (i) biotransformation of soy-derived sterols into corticosteroid and hormone precursors
  - (ii) classical fermentation for pipeline APIs: anti-infectives, anti-fungals, immunosuppressants, enzymes, anti-parasitics
  - (iii) recombinant expression (GLP-1, insulin)
  - (iv) precision fermentation and synthetic biology for nutraceuticals and alternate proteins
  - Source: [RHP p. 220](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)
- **Phytosterol sourcing:** "to the extent phytosterols are imported from the United States, they are converted into KSMs through bio-transformation processes using fermentation which are further processed using internal organic chemistry platforms". The company also uses "qualified overseas KSM and intermediate suppliers". Principal raw materials are "phytosterols and certain fermentation-based and semi-synthetic intermediates" — [RHP pp. ~233, 243](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)
- **Make-versus-buy:** the company has make-vs-buy flexibility on KSMs for ">80% of our products by revenue". It is "among the few backward-integrated Indian and global manufacturers capable of producing in-house corticosteroid and hormone precursors". F&S says this reduces "dependence on China for intermediates" — [RHP pp. 220, 202](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)
- **Other process technology:** progesterone runs on continuous flow chemistry, which the RHP says makes Symbiotec "the only Indian manufacturer" doing continuous manufacturing for progesterone. A bio-catalysis (enzyme) step replaced a chemical step for one high-value product — [RHP pp. 205, 240–241](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)
- **How F&S tags fermentation use by product:**
  - "Yes" for progesterone, prednisolone, hydrocortisone, betamethasone, testosterone, methylprednisolone, androstenedione and hydroxyprogesterone caproate
  - "Yes – indirectly" for triamcinolone acetonide and clobetasol propionate
  - F&S adds: "The fermentation products mentioned in the report can be manufactured using a combination of chemical synthesis and fermentation technology."
  - Source: [RHP Industry Overview, pp. 205–206](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)
- **Intermediates not in the RHP:** the RHP text does not mention ADD (androstadienedione), 16-DPA or diosgenin. I searched the full text for these terms with no hits — [RHP full text](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)

### Inferences
- In value-chain terms, Symbiotec's fermentation is **biotransformation**: microbial side-chain cleavage of phytosterols into AD, 9-OH-AD and bisnor alcohol. It is not de novo classical fermentation (sugar to secondary metabolite) of the kind Concord Biotech runs for immunosuppressants and statins. Fermentation is one upstream step. Most of the value-add and process complexity is downstream chemistry (fluorination, Grignard, Birch reduction, etc.), which is why chemical capacity is stated in MT (584.67) and fermentation in KL (300 operating).
- Using F&S's own technology table (p. 186), the core steroid business falls under "Bioconversion (Biotransformation) — steroid modification". "Classical fermentation" in that table means antibiotics, amino acids and vitamins. Symbiotec's genuinely classical-fermentation exposure is a pipeline and new-launch item: products launched 2023, a 10-year take-or-pay contract, and mupirocin among the new launches. It is not yet material revenue.
- The Ujjain 400 KL plant (food-grade, FSSAI/FSSC) and its 600 KL expansion are positioned for alternate-protein and nutraceutical CDMO. That is a different market from pharma fermentation APIs. Headline "700 KL" comparisons against fermentation API peers therefore overstate the capacity serving the API business, which is 300 KL at Pithampur.
- The jump in US share (4.0% → 13.1%) and the drop in India share (44.8% → 33.0%) in FY26 suggest a mix shift toward direct regulated-market exports. The RHP notes that "domestic" includes indirect exports to regulated markets.
- Backward integration is partial: the company says "make versus buy" flexibility, not full captive supply. Top-10 supplier share swung from 50.3% of expenses in FY24 to 18.4% in FY25, which suggests material in-sourcing of KSMs, or a change in sourcing, after FY24.

### Gaps
- No revenue split between corticosteroids and steroidal hormones, or by therapy area, was found in the RHP sections I reviewed.
- The RHP does not say whether Symbiotec makes ADD or 16-DPA, or whether it runs any diosgenin-based route. Its route is described as phytosterol-based only.
- Phytosterol volumes and sources, and the split between captive and purchased KSMs, are not quantified beyond ">80% make-vs-buy flexibility".
- The Ujjain-to-Pithampur relationship (whether Ujjain will ever run steroid biotransformation) is not addressed.

## 3. Financials, FY23–FY26 (plus Q1 FY27)

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- **Scale:** Symbiotec's API revenue (₹8.35 bn in FY26) is almost exactly the same as Concord's (₹8.29 bn), which makes Concord a natural size comparator.
- **Profitability:** Concord's EBITDA margins (35–43%) and net-cash balance sheet are structurally better. That fits a de novo classical fermentation franchise (immunosuppressants, with patent-cliff molecule pipelines) versus Symbiotec's high-share but low-growth steroid niche, which is chemistry-heavy and has a 1–3% market CAGR.
- **Valuation and the positioning argument:** the IPO discount to Concord on P/E (about 52–58x vs 61x) is modest given the gap in returns. For a write-up that positions Symbiotec against the classical fermentation API universe:
  - (a) Its fermentation is biotransformation of phytosterols to AD/9-OH-AD/bisnor alcohol, followed by heavy chemistry.
  - (b) Its classical-fermentation exposure (pipeline anti-infectives, immunosuppressants, mupirocin) is nascent.
  - (c) The headline 700 KL capacity is mostly food-grade industrial-biotech CDMO (Ujjain), not pharma fermentation API capacity.
  - (d) The more relevant steroid comparators are Chinese integrated steroid producers and legacy Western makers (Curia, Pfizer CentreOne, Bayer, EUROAPI, Axplora), not Indian classical-fermentation names.
- **Re-rating case:** the bull case for a premium rests on the new verticals (CDMO take-or-pay, DCV licensing, insulin/GLP-1). As of Q1 FY27 those are adding costs, not earnings: consolidated PAT fell 53% YoY.
- **Source bias:** the internal note and Hawkwood both frame the CDMO story positively. Hawkwood is commercially aligned with Symbiotec ("100% in with Symbiotec"), so its market-size and "right to win" claims are advocacy, not independent diligence.

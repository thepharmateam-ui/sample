## A5 Geographies and legal entities {#a5}

Polymed is an Indian exporter first and a multinational second. {{CB-0084}} of FY26 revenue was earned outside India, yet only {{D-WC-057}} came from foreign subsidiaries (our calculation). The rest was shipped from the Indian plants to distributors abroad. FY26 split the two apart: the holding company's own exports fell {{D-WC-045|abs}}, while the subsidiaries' sales rose {{D-WC-060}} as PendraCare and Citieffe were consolidated (our calculation). We therefore treat international revenue as two businesses with different drivers:
1. Indian-made disposables sold through distributors, which move with distributor stocking, tenders, product approvals, freight and Chinese pricing;
2. acquired European device makers that sell partly direct to surgeons and hospitals, which move with clinical relationships, tender calendars and the euro.

### Europe: distributors, a direct foothold in Italy and a regulatory reset

Europe is {{CB-0012}} of group revenue and the market Polymed built deliberately. Europe rose from {{CC-0445}} of exports around FY18 to {{CC-0283}} by FY22 after a Europe CEO was hired in 2017. Six Western European markets (the UK, France, Italy, Germany, Spain and Sweden) produced {{CC-0713}} of European revenue in May-2024. <!-- TR3Q23 p.14; TR4Q22 p.14; TR4Q24 p.13 -->

**How Polymed sells there.** The Indian-made range goes through distributors: {{CB-1101}}+ of them, with {{CB-1102}} products approved and {{CB-1103}} in the pipeline (May-2026). The acquired companies bring their own channels: Citieffe sells direct in Italy plus {{CB-1104}} distributors, PendraCare uses {{CB-1107}} distributors and {{CB-1108}} OEM relationships, and Plan1Health {{CB-1111}}+ distributors. Italy, through Plan1Health and Citieffe, is the only European market where the group sells direct. The MD said in Aug-2026 that he wants to go direct in {{WC-0055|range}} more geographies, starting with new products, because 'our distributors only sell our existing products'. <!-- DK4Q26 p.39; TR1Q27S p.8 -->

**What drives European revenue**, in the order we rank them:
1. **Distributor stocking.** European distributors normally hold {{WC-0068|range}} months of inventory; they cut it to {{WC-0069|range}} months in 2025, which management identifies as the main cause of the FY26 contraction. The 1QFY27 organic growth of {{CB-0324}} came from 'new customer acquisitions' plus restocking. Management guided to {{CA-0293|range}} new customers from Apr-2026. <!-- TR1Q26 p.9; TR3Q26 p.8; TR1Q27S p.3, p.9 -->
2. **Approvals under the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR).** {{CB-2118}} products received MDR certificates, but management admits it 'had to drop many products initially' while waiting. The pipeline is {{CB-0091}}+ products under registration and ~{{CA-0066}} due for CE marking within three to four months of Aug-2026. Under the EU's 2023 extension of the transition, legacy certificates run to 31 Dec 2027 for class III and implantable IIb devices and to 31 Dec 2028 for the rest, which matters most for Citieffe's implants and PendraCare's catheters. <!-- DKAGM30 p.3; TR3Q26 p.8, p.16; DK1Q27 p.11; TR1Q27S p.9; EU_REG2023_607 p.4 -->
3. **Chinese competition, now partly fenced off in public tenders.** Management blamed 1HFY26 on Chinese product redirected from the US. Since 30 Jun 2025 the EU has excluded Chinese bidders from public medical-device tenders of {{F-0238}} or more and capped China-origin content at {{F-0239}}; such tenders are ~{{F-0240}} of EU device procurement. Polymed's standalone business sells little through EU tenders today; the company names the acquisitions as its route into tender markets. <!-- TR2Q26 p.9; EU_IPI2025_1197 p.5, p.12; DK4Q26 p.39 -->
4. **Clinical selling.** Polymed has hired more than {{CA-0276|range}} clinical staff to support distributors, after concluding that distributors 'are not able to do justice' to the clinical case for its products; it plans such teams in {{CA-0310|range}} markets. The MD's reasoning: to win on price alone 'I have to be {{CA-0275|range}} cheaper than China', which Polymed's scale does not allow. <!-- TR3Q26 p.12, p.22 -->

**The UK** deserves separate mention. It was {{CA-0518|range}} of revenue in FY25, the National Health Service does ~{{CA-0460}} of purchasing, and the India-UK trade agreement cut UK duty on most of Polymed's products from {{CA-0459|range}} to zero. Polymed bid for {{CA-0461|range}} new product categories, mostly vascular access, in the NHS's 2026 cycle and formed Poly Health Ltd to sell direct. The offset: NHS contracts slipped {{CA-0290|range}} years after the change of government. <!-- TR4Q25 p.6-7; TR1Q26 p.10-11; TR3Q26 p.16; AR25 p.48 -->

### United States: small revenue, a long approval queue and tariff whiplash

The US is the largest device market but a small Polymed market. Management guided US revenue of {{CA-0636|range}} for FY25, said FY26 was below {{CA-0448|range}}, and expects {{CA-0078|range}} in FY27. The medium-term target of {{WC-0060|range}} has kept its size but not its date: management called it a three-year goal in Jul-2024, three to four years in Oct-2024 and two to three years in Aug-2025. <!-- TR1Q25 p.4; TR2Q25 p.3; TR1Q26 p.7; TR1Q27S p.12 -->

The reasons it is still small, as management describes them:
1. **Approvals.** The FDA database lists {{D-0876}} 510(k) clearances in Poly Medicure's name, all class II infusion devices (safety IV catheters and IV sets), the latest dated 1 Apr 2026. Management said in Nov-2025 that the FDA had started rejecting data from Indian test labs, so products had to be re-tested in the US and 'all work we have done in the past was negated'. It expected the new tests to finish by Apr-May 2026, re-filing to follow and new approvals from mid-2026. <!-- FDA510K_POLY p.1; TR2Q26 p.10 -->
2. **Channel.** Polymed supplies through {{CB-1100}} distributors and GPO contracts ('two large GPOs and distributors'); 'our job is limited to manufacturing in India'. The FDA listing of a suction catheter kit at the Jaipur plant under the name of McKesson, a US distributor, is the only public sign of private-label supply. <!-- DK4Q26 p.38; TR1Q26 p.7; TR1Q25 p.6; FDAREG_POLY p.1 -->
3. **Tariffs.** The US was 'a red flag because of ... tariff situation', which 'delayed our launch to the U.S. market quite a lot'. <!-- TR4Q26 p.10 -->

### West Asia: demand intact, shipments stuck

West Asia (the Gulf states and their neighbours) was {{CA-0128|range}} of revenue before the conflict that began in Feb-Mar 2026, by management's estimate. <!-- HOWDEN_HORMUZ_2603 p.3 --> Management described demand as intact but called shipping 'a logistics nightmare': revenue from the region fell {{CA-0036|abs}} yoy in 1QFY27, with 'all the orders ... still lying at ports or in factories'. That cut organic RoW growth to {{CB-0329}}. <!-- TR4Q26 p.5; TR1Q27S p.3, p.9; DK1Q27 p.26 -->
- **Costs.** Management said logistics costs rose {{CA-0067|range}} times in the six months to Aug-2026. Hapag-Lloyd added a war-risk surcharge of {{F-0255}}, and Indian exporters faced {{F-0256|range}} higher insurance on Gulf-routed cargo. The Drewry container index rose from {{F-0250}} on 2 Apr 2026 to {{F-0252}} on 17 Sep 2026. <!-- TR1Q27S p.8; HOWDEN_HORMUZ_2603 p.11; DREWRY_WCI_260402 p.1; DREWRY_WCI_2609 p.1 -->
- **The acquisitions add exposure.** PendraCare had 'over-indexed exposure' of ~{{CA-0071|range}}% to the region, which management says is why it is not growing. <!-- TR1Q27S p.11 -->
- **Recovery.** Management expects West Asia to grow {{CA-0068|range}} once the situation improves. We would treat that as a rebound from a depressed base, not a trend. <!-- TR1Q27S p.9 -->
- **Access.** Saudi Arabia requires a marketing authorization and a licensed local authorized representative for every device. <!-- SFDA_REQ1 p.4-5 -->

### Africa: a small export market, served partly through Egypt

Africa took {{A-0573}} of the holding company's FY26 exports, {{D-WC-049}} of the total and {{D-WC-043}} yoy (our calculation). ### Latin America: Brazil goes direct

Latin America is Polymed's second-largest export region, with Brazil and Mexico growing and 'a good market share' in Argentina (May-2022). Registration is slow: management said in 2022 that registering a product in Mexico took {{WC-0067}}. <!-- TR3Q24b p.14; TR4Q22 p.5-6 -->

Brazil is where Polymed is changing its model.
- **Market.** Management sizes Brazil's device market at {{CA-0085|range}}, the largest in South America. Brazil was the second-largest destination for India's exports of catheters, cannulas and needles (HS 901839) in 2025, at {{F-0138}}. <!-- TR4Q26 p.3; F_CT_IND_X_901839_2025 p.1 -->
- **Rules.** Under ANVISA's device rules of 2022, a registration must be held by a legal entity in Brazil and is valid for {{F-0246}}. A foreign maker selling through distributors leaves the registrations with them. <!-- ANVISA_RDC751 p.1 -->
- **Moves.** Polymed set up a Brazilian subsidiary in 1QFY26 (pre-operational; FY26 loss {{B-0688|abs}}), then on 29 Apr 2026 bought Medyneo, a distribution company with ANVISA and import licences but no operations, for {{A-1016}} ({{CA-0142}}). Management says the purchase saves {{CA-0143|range}} months. Renato Rocha (ex-Descarpack, B. Braun and GE Healthcare) became CEO of Polymed Brazil in Aug-2026, and the Board has allocated funds for direct sales. ### Company-specific drivers by region, ranked

1. **Europe organic recovery and tender access (management target, partly visible).** Mechanism: new customers, restocking, MDR approvals and the EU's exclusion of Chinese bidders. Evidence: {{CB-0324}} organic growth in 1QFY27. Timing: FY27. Our view: the most important swing factor for export growth.
2. **India private-hospital penetration (management target, visible).** Mechanism: more reps, clinical staff and products per hospital. Evidence: private revenue grew {{CA-0332}} in 2QFY26 and {{CA-0220}} in 3QFY26. Timing: continuous.
3. **West Asia normalization (management expectation).** Mechanism: pent-up orders ship once routes reopen. Evidence: none yet; {{CA-0036}} in 1QFY27.
4. **Direct models in Brazil and new European markets (management plan, early).** Mechanism: own sales teams for new products. Evidence: Medyneo licences and a CEO hire; no revenue yet. Our view: a margin cost before it is a revenue gain.
5. **US (management target, repeatedly deferred).** Mechanism: 510(k) re-filings and GPO contracts, plus Citieffe and PendraCare products. Evidence: {{CA-0078|range}} expected in FY27. Our view: immaterial to FY27-FY28 revenue unless tariffs stay at {{CA-0077}} and the re-filed products clear.

Two margin effects follow. Direct selling raises gross margin but adds fixed sales cost ahead of revenue. The Gulf and container-freight shock raises delivered cost on the export business. Management raised prices at the start of FY27 to offset raw-material inflation, but has not said how much of the freight increase it recovers. <!-- TR1Q27S p.5 -->

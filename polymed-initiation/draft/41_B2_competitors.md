## B2 The competitor universe {#b2}

Polymed competes in three leagues at once:
1. **Global majors** many times its size, which sell whole portfolios into US GPOs and European tenders and also make and sell in India.
2. **Chinese exporters** with similar cost structures and larger scale, now redirecting volume from the US towards Polymed's markets.
3. **Indian makers**, mostly smaller and focused on one or two segments, several of them newly funded by private equity.

Section A8 compares margins and Polymed's competitive position. This section maps who the competitors are, what they make and where they meet Polymed, and the business-line chapters (A4.1-A4.10) go deeper by product.

### How the field is structured

Three reports commissioned for Indian offer documents describe the same structure from different angles:
- **Three kinds of player.** Frost & Sullivan (F&S, 2021) splits the Indian market into multinationals at the premium end, small domestic makers selling on price with variable quality, and a handful of "independent medical device companies" gaining share with multinational-grade quality at lower prices. Multinationals and independents together held {{E-0117}} of the market. <!-- DRHP_HEAL pp.124-125, 127 -->
- **Two layers.** 1Lattice (2025) describes a commodity layer (syringes, IV sets, gloves, gowns), where domestic makers compete on price, scale and reach into smaller cities, and a premium layer, where multinationals and a few certified domestic firms compete on technology, international approvals and relationships with tertiary hospitals. <!-- DRHP_GSURG p.204 -->
- **Few scaled Indian companies.** F&S (2025) says the Indian MedTech industry "has very few scaled companies, such as PolyMed, SMT and Healthium". CRISIL (2024) counts more than {{E-0320}} domestic device makers and describes the consumables market as "largely dominated together by MNC players and imports". <!-- DRHP_SMT p.185; PD24 pp.127, 132 -->

The offer documents also show how peers see Polymed:
- G Surgiwear uses Polymed as its only listed peer, and Integris and SMT name Polymed and Laxmi Dental. <!-- DRHP_GSURG p.144; DRHP_INTEG p.166; DRHP_SMT pp.120, 126 -->
- F&S classifies Polymed as an "Infusion, Vascular Access" company with {{E-0149}} acquisition to Mar-2025 and Class I and II products only, and says "Polymed is most focused on consumables". <!-- DRHP_INTEG p.222; DRHP_SMT p.210 -->

Four points stand out:
1. **Scale and breadth.** BD's medication-delivery business alone is ~{{D-WD-003}} Polymed's revenue (our calculation), and ICU Medical says its rivals win "full-line contracts" because they can supply a hospital's whole infusion range. Breadth is the majors' main weapon in US GPOs and European tenders (Section B1). <!-- BD_10K25 p.1; ICUI_10K25 p.1 -->
2. **They are also in India.** BD, B. Braun, Nipro and Terumo (through Terumo Penpol) make products in India. On CRISIL's FY23 figures BD India had revenue of {{E-0344}} and B. Braun India {{E-0345}}, with export shares of {{D-0498}} and {{D-0496}}. <!-- PD24 pp.162, 165-166 -->
3. **They raise prices where they can.** Terumo's price increases more than offset its US tariff costs in the year to Mar-2026, and B. Braun, Nipro and Baxter also raised prices (Section B1). <!-- TERUMO_FY25 p.7; BBRAUN_AR25 p.44; NIPRO_FY25 p.3; BAX_10K25 p.1 -->
4. **They are reshaping their portfolios.** Teleflex is selling its acute-care and OEM businesses, Baxter sold its kidney-care arm to Carlyle, and BD bought Edwards' critical-care business (Section B5). <!-- TFX_10K25 p.1; BAX_10K25 p.1; BD_10K25 p.1 -->

The distributors are a special case. BD notes that "traditional distributors are also manufacturers of medical devices, providing another source of competition". Medline and Cardinal Health sell own-brand products alongside the brands they distribute, and both were caught up in the FDA's 2023-2024 action against China-made syringes: Medline received a warning letter, and Cardinal's Monoject syringes, made by Jiangsu Caina, were recalled. <!-- BD_10K25 p.1; ICUI_10K25 p.1; FDA_SYR_WL p.1; FDA_WL_CAINA p.1 -->

The Chinese makers matter to Polymed because three forces now push their product out of China and away from the US:
1. **Price cuts at home.** China ran {{E-0285}} volume-based procurement projects for consumables in 2025, and KDL expects the new rules to concentrate the market in the leaders. Weigao says lower selling prices for certain core products under centralized procurement cut its device operating profit in 2025, and its gross margin was {{E-0276}} in 1H26. <!-- KDL_AR25 p.17; WEIGAO_AR25 p.13; WEIGAO_IR26 p.21 -->
2. **Capacity built to win tenders.** Sanxin says the winners of dialysis procurement rounds use the capacity those wins build "to attack overseas markets", where dialysis rates are rising in Southeast Asia and Latin America. Its overseas revenue rose {{WD-0012}} in 2025. <!-- SANXIN_AR25 pp.34, 39 -->
3. **A narrower US market.** The FDA put Chinese syringe makers on import alert in 2024 and told US providers to avoid China-made syringes where possible, and the US raised its Section 301 tariff on Chinese syringes and needles to {{F-0204}} from 27-Sep-2024 (Section B4). <!-- FDA_RU240517 p.1; FDA_SYRINGE_CHINA p.1; FR_301MOD_240918 p.9 -->

The trade data show where the product went. China's device exports to the US fell {{E-0290|abs}} in 2025, while exports to the EU and UK rose {{WD-0006}} and those to Latin America {{WD-0007}}, both core Polymed markets. Weigao's US sales fell in 1H26 while its sales in Europe, West Asia and Africa rose. <!-- KDL_AR25 pp.18-19; WEIGAO_IR26 p.25 -->

### What the competitor map means for Polymed

We see Polymed's competitive position as strongest where it meets the Indian arms of the majors and single-segment Indian makers, and weakest where it meets Chinese exporters on price and the global majors on breadth:
1. **Against the majors,** Polymed wins on cost and loses on range and brand. The MD put its prices at {{CC-0450}} below BD and B. Braun in infusion in 2023, which opens European tenders and Indian private hospitals but not, so far, US GPO volume. <!-- TR3Q23 p.15 -->
2. **Against Chinese makers,** it has no cost edge and sometimes a price disadvantage: the MD said in 2023 that Chinese dialysis products sold ~{{CC-0522|range}} below Polymed's. Its defences are regulatory: the EU's exclusion of Chinese bidders from large tenders, US tariffs and FDA action against Chinese syringes, and the anti-dumping case it has brought in India (Sections B3 and B4). <!-- TR4Q23 p.11 -->
3. **Against Indian makers,** it has the widest range, the most export approvals and, by CRISIL's FY23 figures, the largest revenue among the Indian-owned disposables makers compared. The risk is that private capital narrows that lead, especially in syringes, cannulas and urology, where Romsons and HMD are strong. <!-- PD24 pp.165-167 -->

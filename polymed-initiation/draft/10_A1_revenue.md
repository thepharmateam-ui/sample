# Part A – The business {#part-a}

## A1 What Polymed sells and where the revenue comes from {#a1}

Poly Medicure makes single-use medical devices: the cannulas, infusion sets, fistula needles, bloodlines, blood bags, catheters, tubes and drains that a hospital consumes in very large volumes and throws away after one patient.
- **Scale.** It sells {{A-0001}} products across {{A-0002}} specialities, from {{A-0003}} plants in {{A-0004}} countries with ~{{CB-0082|plain}}bn units of annual capacity. The products reach {{A-0005}} countries through {{A-0011}} distributors. <!-- AR26 p.3; DK1Q27 p.10 -->
- **Anchor product.** The IV cannula: Polymed describes itself as the third-largest maker globally, and the MD put its global share at almost {{CA-0187|range}} on ~{{CA-0188}} a year. <!-- DK1Q27 p.7; TR4Q26 p.17 -->
- **Unit economics.** Most devices sell for tens of rupees: management cites price points of Rs. {{WM-0007|range}} per infusion device. It sold {{CA-0554}} devices in 3QFY25 alone. <!-- TR4Q26 p.18; TR3Q25 p.4 -->
- **Scale of sales.** FY26 consolidated revenue was {{A-0039}}.

The business model in one line: a very high-volume, low-price consumables business, built on in-house automation, that earned a consolidated operating EBITDA margin of {{A-0028}} in FY26 ({{A-0030}} in FY25; Section A2). It is now adding higher-priced devices in cardiology and orthopaedics, where management says a single unit sells for thousands of rupees, sometimes above {{WM-0008}}, and in oncology through Plan1Health. <!-- AR26 p.5; TR4Q26 p.18 -->

### Five therapy lines are disclosed, two of them new

Polymed reports revenue in five buckets:
1. **Infusion therapy:** IV cannulas, infusion sets and accessories.
2. **Renal:** dialysis consumables and machines.
3. **Cardiology:** reported separately since the PendraCare acquisition.
4. **Orthopaedics:** Citieffe, reported separately since its acquisition.
5. **Others.**

"Others" pools every other speciality: blood management, anaesthesia and respiratory, critical care, diagnostics, urology, gastroenterology, surgery and wound drainage, and the vascular-access and oncology products of Plan1Health. On calls, management has declined to break it out further. The MD said on the 4QFY26 call that the new lines are 'very new businesses' and 'we don't call out those numbers specifically because confidentially, we don't want to share what company is doing'. <!-- TR4Q26 p.17 -->

The company's statutory sustainability report (BRSR) nonetheless gives a finer split of standalone turnover, which we use below and in Section A4. We do not estimate anything beyond what that report prints.

### The sustainability report gives the only finer product split

The BRSR lists Polymed's main products with each one's share of turnover. It does this for the standalone company, which excludes the foreign subsidiaries, on a product classification broader than the one in its investor presentations. <!-- BRSR26 p.12; BRSR25 p.5 -->

It gives the following for FY26:
- **Blood management and blood collection:** {{A-0848}} of standalone turnover, or about {{D-M-037}} (our calculation). That makes it the third-largest line after infusion and dialysis.
- **Surgery and wound drainage:** {{A-0849}}.
- **Urology:** {{A-0850}}.
- **Anaesthesia and respiratory:** a separate line at {{A-0899}} in FY25. It has no separate line in FY26; we infer it was reclassified into another line.

A separate BRSR table on product life-cycle assessment shows that prefilled saline flush syringes (3 mL, 5 mL and 10 mL) alone were {{A-0870}} of FY26 standalone turnover, about {{D-M-041}} (our calculation). That makes flush syringes one of the largest single product families after IV cannulas. <!-- BRSR26 p.27 -->

### Two-thirds of revenue is earned outside India

Polymed has always been an exporter: {{CB-0079|approx}} of FY20 revenue came from outside India. International revenue was still {{CB-0084|approx}} of FY26 revenue (and {{CB-0332}} in 1QFY27, lifted by the acquisitions). India has been the fastest-growing region since FY20 and the rest of the world the slowest, on the company's CAGRs ([[EXREF:front_cagr]]); the shares moved accordingly:
- **Domestic:** India's share rose from {{CB-0008}} to {{CB-0011}}.
- **Europe:** its share rose from {{CB-0009}} to {{CB-0012}}.
- **RoW:** Africa, West Asia, the Americas and the rest of Asia; their combined share fell from {{CB-0010}} to {{CB-0013}}.

The MD's stated long-run aim is a mix of {{CA-0161|plain}}, roughly today's balance. <!-- TR4Q26 p.11 -->

### What the disclosure does and does not tell us

Company disclosure is good on:
- consolidated and standalone P&L by quarter;
- domestic versus international revenue, with Europe and RoW;
- the five therapy buckets;
- acquisition contributions.

It is thin on four things:
1. **Product-level revenue** within "others", beyond the standalone percentages in the BRSR. The BRSR excludes the subsidiaries and uses a broader definition of infusion than the presentations.
2. **Customer concentration and the OEM/private-label share.** Section A2 sets out what the placement documents and rating reports disclose.
3. **Country-level export revenue.** The US is the only country for which management gives a number: it expects exports there of {{CA-0078|range}} in FY27. <!-- TR1Q27S p.12 -->
4. **Volume and price split by product.** Management has occasionally given total devices sold ([[EXREF:vol_price]]) but not by line. <!-- TR3Q25 p.4, p.10 -->

These gaps shape the question bank in Part C.

## A6 Manufacturing, capacity and R&D {#a6}

Polymed makes almost everything it sells, and it makes it in India. That is what lets it sell a cannula at a few rupees and still earn a mid-to-high-twenties operating margin. The process is integrated from mould-making to sterile pack: moulds, dies and much of the assembly machinery are built in-house, parts are moulded and extruded on site, and products are assembled on automated lines. Sterilization moved in-house in 2025. The next two years are about filling capacity rather than adding it:
- capacity grew faster than output after FY22, and management puts utilization of the post-2021 capacity at {{CA-0173|range}};
- two new Indian plants (Palwal and the YEIDA medical-device park) and the second phase of the captive gamma sterilization plant come on stream between end-2026 and 1QFY28;
- wages in Haryana, where most of the plants are, rose sharply from Apr-2026.

R&D spend is low by global standards at {{A-0414}} of turnover but has grown {{D-WC-079}} a year since FY19 (our calculation). It now supports {{CB-1083}} launches a year and a pipeline of more than {{CB-1084}} products. <!-- TR4Q26 p.14; AR26 p.51; DK4Q26 p.30 -->

### Sterilization: from outsourced EtO to a captive gamma plant

Until 2025 Polymed sterilized mainly with EtO gas through third-party vendors. The catalogues label some products gamma-sterilized (the PolyFlush syringe, Polyswab, the ABG syringe) and others EtO-sterilized (transducer protectors, peritoneal-dialysis sets). <!-- TR1Q25 p.3; CATDOM25 p.9, p.28; CATEXP24 p.22, p.54 -->

The captive plant has been a long project:
1. **May-2022.** Management added {{CC-0270}} to the capex plan for 'a controlled radiation plant', because many new products are gamma-sterilized, there was no facility near the plants, and outside vendors sometimes compromised quality. It was due in FY24. <!-- TR4Q22 p.8, p.15 -->
2. **Jul-2024.** Still under construction; management called gamma 'more safer' than the EtO 'which we use today' and planned to move 'most of our sterilization' to it. <!-- TR1Q25 p.3 -->
3. **Aug-2025.** Phase I started, licensed for Class A and B devices, with Class C and D licences awaited; source capacity to rise {{CA-0452}}. <!-- TR1Q26 p.7 -->
4. **Sep-2026.** 'Operational; one of the few such captive facilities in India', with Phase II due by end-2026. <!-- DKAGM31 p.3; TR2Q26 p.16 -->

Management places the plant variously at 'Palwal' and at 'Faridabad-Mitral'; the annual report says Mitrol. Why it matters: EtO is under regulatory pressure. About {{E-0381}} of US sterile devices are EtO-sterilized, and the FDA is encouraging alternatives including radiation. In-house sterilization should also shorten the time from moulding to shipment (our inference). Neither the cost of the plant nor the savings, nor the share of output now sterilized in-house, is disclosed. Citieffe outsources its sterilization and plans to bring it in-house. <!-- FDA_ETO p.1; DKCIT p.16 -->

### What drives plant economics, ranked

1. **Filling the capacity already built (visible).** At {{CA-0173|range}} utilization on the post-2021 plants against a practical peak of {{CC-0046|range}}, volume growth can come with little new capex, which is why FY27 capex guidance is lower. Mechanism: fixed cost absorption. Timing: FY27-FY28.
2. **Wages against automation (headwind now).** A {{CA-0024}} minimum-wage rise in the state that hosts most capacity is only partly offset by automation; the net effect showed up as {{CA-0023}} employee-cost growth in 1QFY27.
3. **Captive sterilization (management claim, unquantified).** Management's stated aims are control of quality and turnaround and a safer process than EtO; we would add the vendor margin and transport it removes. The benefit is undisclosed and depends on the Class C/D licences.
4. **New plants for new businesses (cost before revenue).** Palwal and YEIDA add depreciation and fixed cost from end-FY27, ahead of the orthopaedics and cardiology revenue they are built for.
5. **Moving acquired manufacturing processes to India (management plan, early).** Citieffe's and PendraCare's plants run at {{CA-0244|range}}. At the Citieffe deal management had 'no plans' to move its manufacturing, only to outsource parts of the process to India; by Aug-2026 some process transfers for both acquisitions were 'at the regulatory approval stage'. Our view: a margin lever for FY28 onwards, gated by approvals. <!-- TRCIT p.6, p.8; TR1Q27S p.6 -->

## B5 Structural trends {#b5}

Nine structural trends will shape disposables over the rest of the decade. Most of them favour a low-cost maker that already has safety-engineered and PVC-free products, captive gamma sterilization and European and US approvals, which describes Polymed. Two cut the other way: Chinese makers redirecting volume from the US into Polymed's markets, and consolidation that gives rivals more scale and capital.

### Safety-engineered devices

The EU's sharps directive (Directive 2010/32/EU), transposed by {{F-0244}}, requires employers to provide devices with safety-engineered protection where a risk assessment calls for it, and bans recapping. CRISIL lists the safety devices now in use in India: safety IV cannulas and scalp-vein sets in infusion, safety blood-collection sets and fistula needles in dialysis, and safety Huber needles and closed IV catheter systems in critical care. <!-- EU_DIR2010_32 pp.2, 6; PD24 p.137 -->

How far conversion has gone is uncertain. Two market-research vendors put the safety share of the IV-catheter market at {{E-0358}} (Persistence, CY25) and {{E-0361}} (Future Market Insights, CY26); both are vendor estimates, and neither splits the figure by region. <!-- VND_PMR_PIVC p.1; VND_FMI_PIVC p.1 -->

**What it means for Polymed.** Each hospital that converts moves from a conventional to a higher-priced safety cannula, and Polymed has FDA 510(k) clearances for its safety and closed IV catheters as well as EU approvals (Section A4.1). The share of its cannula volume that is already safety-engineered is [not disclosed], which is the number that would show how much conversion is left. <!-- FDA510K_POLY p.1 -->

### PVC and DEHP

DEHP is the plasticizer that makes PVC flexible in blood bags, bloodlines and infusion tubing. Under Regulation (EU) 2023/2482, its use in medical devices has a latest application date of {{F-0243}} and a sunset date of {{F-0242}}, after which it needs an authorization. ICU Medical lists demand for "more environmentally friendly products" and "materials of concern" among the factors it competes on, and Fresenius introduced a PVC-free foil for its dialysate bags in 2025 and cut the material used in its bloodlines. HLL, which claims a dominant share of India's blood-bag market, sells PVC bags. <!-- EU_REACH2023_2482 pp.1, 4; ICUI_10K25 p.1; FMC_20F25 p.1; HLL_BISPR p.1 -->

Polymed sold {{A-0803}} of PVC-free products in FY25, {{WD-0025}} of the finished goods it sold, and aims to develop PVC-free versions for at least {{WD-0016}} of its portfolio by 2035. Its catalogue already includes blood bags with TOTM plasticizer for platelet storage, a DEHP- and PVC-free burette set, PVC-free extension lines and a DEHP-free Ryle's tube. <!-- AR25 p.65; BRSR26 p.20; CATDOM25 pp.13, 16, 25, 32 -->

**What it means for Polymed.** We infer that the unit share is high because many of its products, such as cannulas, contain no PVC in the first place; the harder part is the PVC-based lines in blood management, renal and infusion tubing that it sells into Europe. We see the EU deadline as a moderate positive: it raises the cost of staying in the EU market for PVC-based rivals, but alternative plasticizers cost more and must be revalidated product by product.

### Sterilization: ethylene oxide under pressure, gamma in-house

About {{E-0381}} of sterile devices in the US are sterilized with ethylene oxide (EtO), per the FDA, which supports alternatives and treats radiation as an "established" method. The pressure on EtO is regulatory and legal:
- the US EPA's emissions rule for sterilization facilities took effect on 5-Apr-2024 with two years to comply, and a presidential proclamation of Jul-2025 gave certain facilities two more years;
- the EPA's interim registration decision of Jan-2025 tightens exposure limits, which BD warns could reduce sterilization capacity;
- contract sterilizers have faced lawsuits. <!-- FDA_ETO p.1; BD_10K25 p.1; TFX_10K25 p.1 -->

Polymed commissioned a captive gamma-sterilization plant in 2025, which management expects to expand {{CA-0452}}. Before it, sterilization was outsourced, and management called gamma a "more safer process compared to the ETO gas which we use today". Citieffe still outsources sterilization but plans to bring it in-house. <!-- DK2Q26 p.24; TR1Q26 p.7; TR1Q25 p.3; DKCIT p.16 -->

**What it means for Polymed.** Captive gamma shortens lead times, removes a dependence on third-party EtO capacity that tighter rules may shrink, and lowers cost at scale; management cites gamma savings among its offsets to raw-material inflation. The share of output that has moved to gamma, and what still needs EtO, is [not disclosed]. <!-- TR4Q26 p.6 -->

### Closed systems and needle-free access

The leading infusion suppliers now sell the catheter as part of a system: BD's range includes flush syringes, disinfecting caps, needle-free connectors and closed-system transfer devices, and ICU Medical offers "a one-stop shop" of catheters, connectors, sets and pumps. CRISIL counts closed IV catheter systems among the safety devices in use in India. <!-- BD_10K25 p.1; ICUI_10K25 p.1; PD24 p.137 -->

**What it means for Polymed.** It has most of the parts: a closed IV catheter system cleared in the US (Nouvo), needle-free connectors, disinfecting caps and prefilled flush syringes. It lacks infusion pumps, which limits it in full-line US contracts (Section A4.1). We see this trend as raising the value per line for Polymed in Europe and India, where it sells without pumps, and as a barrier in the US. <!-- FDA510K_POLY p.1; CATDOM25 pp.6, 9-10 -->

### Home dialysis and single-use dialysers

Estimates of how many patients dialyse at home depend on who counts:
- **Fresenius (worldwide, CY25):** {{E-0227}} of patients received haemodialysis in a clinic and {{E-0228}} peritoneal dialysis (PD), mostly at home.
- **F&S (its study markets, 2024):** PD below {{E-0015}} of patients and home haemodialysis below {{E-0016}}; it expects PD's share to stay minimal. In India, clinic haemodialysis is {{WD-0026}} of dialysis therapies. <!-- FMC_20F25 p.1; RHP_NEPHRO pp.267, 269, 286 -->

Policy may push the other way in India. The parliamentary health committee's Aug-2026 report on kidney disease recommends promoting home-based PD and mandatory insurance for kidney patients. PM-JAY already pays a PD package of {{WD-0028}} for catheter insertion and {{WD-0029}} a day for fluid and disposables, and Assam's health mission funds PD at Rs. {{F-0126|num}} per patient per month. <!-- PIB_PSC176_2608 p.1; PMJAY_HBP2022 p.11; PMNDP_ASSAM_ROP p.6 -->

The other shift is to single use. Nipro expects Asian dialyser sales to grow on "the shift to single use", while Indian centres reuse dialysers several times (Section A4.3). <!-- NIPRO_FY25 p.15 -->

**What it means for Polymed.** Its renal range is built around haemodialysis: dialysers, bloodlines, fistula needles and machines, with PD kits among its PLI products. A move to home PD would shift demand towards PD fluids and kits, where Vantive and Fresenius lead; a move to single use would multiply dialyser volumes. We see single use as the larger and nearer effect for Polymed, and PD as a slow policy risk. <!-- PLIMD_APPR23 p.2 -->

### Automation and labour cost

F&S puts India's average hourly manufacturing wage below {{E-0184}}, against {{E-0185}} in China, {{WD-0017}} in the US and {{WD-0018}} in Europe, and India's manufacturing and R&D costs about {{E-0186|plain}}% below those of the US and Europe. <!-- DRHP_SMT p.185 -->

Polymed pairs that labour-cost edge with automation: {{CB-1077}} automatic assembly machines and {{CB-1078}} robots in May-2026, against {{D-0166}} assembly machines in Jun-2024. We read its workforce data as one reason: permanent-worker attrition was {{A-0862}} in FY26, and {{A-0874}} of permanent workers were paid the minimum wage. Chinese rivals are automating too: KDL reports pushing scale expansion, automation and intelligent upgrading at each of its four manufacturing bases. <!-- DK4Q26 p.29; PD24 p.179; BRSR26 pp.14, 41; KDL_AR25 p.19 -->

**What it means for Polymed.** Against Western makers the labour gap is decisive; against China it is smaller, and against both it matters less as automation cuts labour content. We read Polymed's durable edge as automation plus in-house tooling and machine building (Section A8), with low wages as a diminishing bonus. Minimum-wage revisions and high attrition are the pressure points.

### China+1, tariffs and supply security

US policy has pushed buyers away from Chinese syringes. The FDA advised providers in 2024 to use syringes "not manufactured in China, if possible", and the US raised its Section 301 tariff on Chinese syringes and needles to {{F-0204}} from Sep-2024 (Section B4). US syringe imports show the result. <!-- FDA_SYRINGE_CHINA p.1; FR_301MOD_240918 p.9 -->

Other evidence points the same way, but slowly:
- **Buyers.** 1Lattice reports that international buyers are turning to Indian suppliers for cost, flexible specifications and faster turnaround, and that "many companies are moving manufacturing from China to India". <!-- DRHP_GSURG pp.176, 178 -->
- **Polymed.** The MD said in Feb-2024 that "there is no shift from China today" for Polymed's products, and in Nov-2025 that US buyers had so far favoured Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia. <!-- TR3Q24b p.15; TR2Q26 p.14 -->
- **Chinese response.** Chinese makers are localizing abroad: Weigao's Indonesian joint venture starts production in 2H26. China's drug regulator has also issued rules on export sales certificates, effective 1-May-2026, which KDL says will support device exports. <!-- WEIGAO_IR26 p.34; KDL_AR25 p.17 -->
- **Supply security.** Hurricane Helene's damage to a competitor's US IV-solutions plant in 2024 caused a national IV-fluid shortage, and B. Braun raised its inventory coverage to {{WD-0027|num}} weeks in 2025 "to ensure supply security". We would expect buyers that were short of product to be more willing to qualify a second source. <!-- ICUI_10K25 p.1; BBRAUN_AR25 p.43 -->

**What it means for Polymed.** China+1 is real in syringes and slow in cannulas. We see it as a multi-year opening in the US and with OEMs that source from Asia, which depends on US tariffs on India staying below those on China (Section B4) and on Polymed winning GPO volume.

### EU MDR and supplier attrition

The MDR raised the cost of staying in Europe. F&S says it adds complexity, delays launches and raises entry barriers, and that Europe's funding squeeze favours overseas makers with a cost advantage. The EU now has {{F-0236}} notified bodies designated under the MDR, and legacy certificates run out on {{F-0230}} for higher-risk devices and {{F-0231}} for the rest. <!-- DRHP_INTEG p.226; DRHP_HEAL p.125; EU_COM2025_1023 p.1; EU_REG2023_607 p.4 -->

Attrition is visible in the evidence:
- Polymed "had to drop many products initially" during the transition, and management said in May-2022 that a CE mark now took {{CC-0264}} months. <!-- TR3Q26 p.8; TR4Q22 pp.5-6 -->
- Translumina's German unit had its CE certificate suspended by its notified body for five months from Oct-2024. <!-- DRHP_INTEG p.42 -->

Polymed has paid the entry cost: all its plants are certified under the MDR for their full range, and it had ~{{CA-0377|num}} CE-certified products in Nov-2025, with ~{{CA-0376|num}} more expected to be CE-marked in CY26. The Dec-2025 proposal to simplify the MDR would remove the five-year limit on certificates and, by the Commission's estimate, save {{F-0234}} a year (Section B4). <!-- AR26 p.35; TR2Q26 p.10; EU_COM2025_1023 p.1 -->

**What it means for Polymed.** We read the MDR as having thinned the field of small suppliers in European tenders, and the acquisitions of PendraCare and Citieffe bought MDR-certified European manufacturing. The simplification lowers costs for all incumbents, including smaller rivals, so we see the moat widening less from here than it did in 2021-2025.

Two patterns stand out. The US majors are selling low-growth consumables and contract-manufacturing units (Teleflex's acute care and OEM businesses, Baxter's kidney care) to specialists and private equity, while buying higher-growth technology (BD's critical care, Teleflex's vascular intervention). In India, F&S counts {{E-0148}} MedTech private-equity deals since 2017, and the latest have funded Polymed's closest domestic rivals. <!-- TFX_10K25 p.1; BAX_10K25 p.1; BD_10K25 p.1; DRHP_INTEG p.216 -->

**What it means for Polymed.** The carve-outs create owners of consumables lines who are under pressure to cut costs, and Teleflex's OEM unit, which makes catheters, extrusions and introducers for other device makers, shows that OEM supply is a business in its own right; CRISIL notes that OEMs have been seeking cost-competitive sources since Covid. That supports Polymed's contract-manufacturing push (Section A4.10). The offset is that its Indian rivals now have capital for capacity, acquisitions and exports. <!-- TFX_10K25 p.1; PD24 p.141 -->

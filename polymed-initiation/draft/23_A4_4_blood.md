## A4.4 Transfusion and blood management {#a4-4}

Blood management, entered in 2000, is Polymed's third-largest product family on the BRSR and its least transparent: the BRSR puts it at ~{{D-WF-011}} the size the placement document showed a year earlier (our calculation), and every consistent series shows it growing more slowly than the group. Demand comes from a public blood system that buys largely by tender, against two larger incumbents. The levers are a doubling of capacity at Palwal from Mar-2027 and a shift to multi-bag, filtered systems as India separates more of its blood into components. <!-- AR26 p.11; BRSR25 p.5; BRSR26 p.12; PD24 p.173; TR1Q26 p.18; DKAGM31 p.4 -->

### What it is and how it is used: one bag system per donation

A blood bag system is a sterile set of PVC bags, pre-filled with anticoagulant, into which a donor's blood is drawn through an attached 16G needle; the MD calls it a "device plus drug" combination. CPDA keeps whole blood or packed red cells for {{D-0859}}, and SAGM additive solution extends red cells to {{D-0860}}. <!-- CATDOM25 p.31; TR3Q25 p.19 -->

The unit of demand is the donation: each unit collected uses one bag system, and what varies is its complexity. <!-- D_product_glossary A4.4 -->
- **Components.** Double to quintuple systems carry satellite bags so that a blood centre can centrifuge a unit into red cells, plasma and platelets, letting one donation "meet the needs of more than one patient" (WHO). <!-- CATDOM25 p.31; WHO_BLOOD p.1 -->
- **Premium configurations** add a TOTM platelet bag (about {{D-0861|range}} days' storage), top-and-bottom bags for automated extraction, an in-line leuko-depletion filter, or a needle guard with a pouch that diverts the first, skin-contaminated blood. <!-- CATDOM25 pp.32-33 -->
- **Smaller items** include paediatric and Penta bags (one unit split among up to five paediatric recipients), cord-blood bags and the ACD anticoagulant bag used in apheresis. <!-- CATDOM25 p.31; CATEXP24 pp.55, 58 -->

Each transfusion then uses a filtered administration set. Collection tubes and needles, reported in the same line, are covered in Section A4.5. <!-- CATDOM25 p.31; PD24 p.173; D_product_glossary A4.4 -->

### Polymed's portfolio and position

Polymed entered transfusion in 2000, three years after infusion, and by FY16 sold a quadruple bag with an in-line red-cell filter. Its domestic and export catalogues cover every configuration above, with main bags from 250 ml to 500 ml, plus Haemofusor and Transvol administration sets and a pressure pump set; the latest decks add a bedside filter and a lab-side leukocyte filter. It does not make IV fluid bags. <!-- AR26 p.11; AR16 p.6; CATDOM25 pp.31-33; CATEXP24 pp.55-60; DK1Q27 p.4; DKAGM31 p.5; TR3Q23 p.17 -->

Management sells transfusion as a basket: it pitched blood banking as a "concept" at Arab Health in 2024 and promised to "keep on adding more and more products" in transfusion and vascular access. The blood-banking equipment the MD floated in Feb-2022 has not appeared: the catalogues list no collection monitors, mixers or other blood-bank machines. Market share is [not disclosed]; the MD has said only that Polymed gained share in blood transfusion (May-2022) and that new capacity would raise its domestic and export share (Feb-2024). <!-- TR3Q24b pp.5-6; TR1Q25 p.4; TR3Q22 pp.7-8; CATDOM25 pp.31-33; CATEXP24 pp.55-60; TR4Q22 p.13 -->

**Capacity and supply chain.**
- **Expansion.** Capacity was added in FY24, with new lines due by May-2024 "for exports and for the local market". In Aug-2025 the MD said transfusion capacity would double at Palwal in Haryana, a plant he now describes as focused on "orthopedics and transfusion" plus extra infusion capacity. <!-- TR3Q24b p.5; TR4Q24 p.3; TR1Q26 p.18; TR1Q27S p.15 -->
- **Palwal.** Budgeted at {{D-0356}} in the 2024 placement document and billed as the company's largest plant, it is due for Phase-1 by Mar-2027 and Phase-2 by Dec-2027. Current capacity, and which plant makes blood bags today, are [not disclosed]. <!-- PD24 p.74; DK3Q25 p.13; DKAGM31 p.4 -->
- **Inputs.** The placement document lists "blood bag sheets" among raw materials, which we read as the PVC film being bought in rather than extruded. <!-- PD21 p.86 -->

**Mix.** Policy has promoted component separation since the 2003 Action Plan for Blood Safety, and for bag makers it is a second lever besides volume: lower-middle-income countries separate {{WF-0008}} of their blood, against {{WF-0009}} in high-income ones (WHO), and each separated unit needs a multi-bag system. India's separation rate is [not disclosed] in our sources. <!-- DGHS_BTS p.1; WHO_BLOOD p.1; D_product_glossary A4.4 -->

**Structure.** A 1996 Supreme Court judgment that ended paid donation led to the National Blood Transfusion Council and state councils; blood banks have been licensed since 1997, and the DGHS took over from NACO in 2021. India has {{WF-0001}} licensed blood centres, and the DGHS supports {{WF-0002}} public and charitable ones with staff, training and the procurement of blood bags and test kits. <!-- DGHS_BTS p.1 -->

**Value.** There is no current Indian market size. A 2018 Business Today report on Terumo BCT put India's {{E-0313|plain}}mn annual collections at a {{E-0314}} market, ~{{D-WF-013}} per collection (our calculation). 360iResearch puts the global market at {{E-0362}} in 2026, growing {{WF-0012}} a year to {{WF-0013}} by 2032 (vendor estimates), ~{{D-WF-014}} per donation on WHO's {{E-0400|plain}}mn (our calculation, different vintages). Lower-middle-income export markets collect only {{E-0401|plain}} donations per thousand people, against {{WF-0011|plain}} in high-income countries. <!-- BT_TERUMOBCT p.1; VND_RNM_BLOODBAG p.1; WHO_BLOOD p.1 -->

### Customers, channel and pricing

In India the customers are blood banks, which the BRSR names among Polymed's customers. <!-- BRSR26 p.13 -->
- **Public and charitable blood banks** get bags through government procurement, which the DGHS support above covers. HS 90189032 "blood transfusion apparatus" is one of {{F-0092}} device lines reserved since Mar-2021 for Class-I local suppliers (at least {{F-0089}} local content), and the DoP's illustrative list of such suppliers names Polymed. <!-- DGHS_BTS p.1; PPP_MD_GUIDE21 p.1; PPP_MD_LC21B p.3, p.10 -->
- **Private blood banks** buy through distributors, which for Polymed are blood-banking specialists. <!-- TR3Q22 p.11 -->
- **Exports** go through overseas distributors, who sell from the full export catalogue. <!-- CATEXP24 pp.55-60 -->

Prices and the channel split are [not disclosed]. We read the market as price-led: value per collection is low, incumbent capacity exceeds domestic demand, and blood centres' own charges for blood follow national rates, last revised in Jun-2022. <!-- DGHS_BTS p.1 -->

### Regulation and trends

- **Indian licensing.** Blood bags were on the short list of devices already licensed before the 2020 amendment to the Medical Devices Rules, so we read the Oct-2023 licensing of Class C and D devices, which helped renal, as having changed little here. HLL was the first maker certified by BIS to the ISO 3826 blood-bag standard (Aug-2023); Polymed's status is [not disclosed]. <!-- GSR102E_2020 p.7; TR1Q25 p.10; HLL_BISPR p.1 -->
- **DEHP.** DEHP typically makes up {{WF-0021}} of plasticized PVC by weight and helps preserve stored red cells, which we read as making red-cell bags harder to convert than tubing. The EU requires authorization for its use in devices after {{F-0242}}, with applications due by {{F-0243}} (Section B5). Polymed's catalogues call its bags "medical grade PVC sheet" without naming the plasticizer; only the TOTM platelet bag and the DEHP-free administration sets are specified. HLL's bags are also PVC. <!-- GMED_DEHP pp.1-2; EU_REACH2023_2482 p.4; CATDOM25 pp.31-32; CATEXP24 pp.55-60; HLL_BISPR p.1 -->

### Company-specific growth drivers, ranked

1. **Mix shift to component systems** (structural; our view). Higher separation rates favour triple, quadruple, SAGM and filtered bags without extra donations; Polymed's range covers them, so the lever is its share of the upgrade. <!-- WHO_BLOOD p.1; CATDOM25 pp.31-33 -->
2. **Palwal capacity** (management plan; Phase-1 by Mar-2027). Doubling capacity pays off only if tenders and exports absorb it. <!-- TR1Q26 p.18; DKAGM31 p.4 -->
3. **Collection growth** (structural but slowing). Collections already exceed the requirement estimate, and rising discards may make blood centres collect more carefully rather than more. <!-- FACTLY_BLOOD p.1 -->
4. **Exports and DEHP-free bags** (our view; 2029-2030). Low donation rates abroad and the EU deadline favour makers with validated alternatives; Polymed's readiness is [not disclosed].
5. **Adjacent products** (management ambition): filters, the Haemodraw segment device and, possibly, blood-bank equipment. <!-- DKAGM31 p.5; CATDOM25 p.33; TR3Q22 pp.7-8 -->

### Company-specific margin drivers, ranked

1. **Tender pricing against surplus capacity** (our view): public buying and two large incumbents cap prices on standard bags.
2. **Mix:** multi-bag, SAGM and filtered systems carry more value per donation; realizations by configuration are [not disclosed].
3. **Utilization:** doubling capacity ahead of demand would weigh on absorption from FY28 until volumes follow (our inference).
4. **Materials:** bought-in PVC film and the cost of any move away from DEHP drive the cost of goods. <!-- PD21 p.86 -->

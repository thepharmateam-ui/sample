## B4 Export-market regimes {#b4}

Polymed earns {{CB-0084|approx}} of its revenue outside India, so the rules of its export markets matter as much as India's (Section A1). <!-- DK1Q27 p.10 --> Three regimes set the terms: the EU's Medical Device Regulation (MDR) and procurement rules, which govern its largest export region; US clearance and tariff policy, which govern the largest market it barely serves; and a patchwork of registration rules, procurement systems and, since early 2026, war-related shipping costs in its other markets.

**MDR.** The MDR has applied since May-2021, and its certificates, issued by notified bodies, are valid for at most five years. It raised the cost and time of keeping each product on the market: management said in May-2022 that a CE mark now took {{CC-0264}} months, against six to twelve before, and in Feb-2026 that Polymed "had to drop many products initially". The EU has only {{F-0236}} notified bodies designated under the MDR, for a medical-technology market the Commission puts at {{F-0235}}. <!-- PD24 p.42; EU_COM2025_1023 p.1; TR4Q22 pp.5-6; TR3Q26 p.8 -->

Polymed's position is strong: every plant is certified under the MDR for its full range, {{CB-2118}} products held MDR certificates in FY25, and management put its CE-certified range at ~{{CA-0377|num}} products in Nov-2025, with ~{{CA-0376|num}} more expected to be CE-marked in CY26; the two counts are on different bases, which the company does not reconcile (Appendix). The acquisitions also bought MDR-certified European manufacturing. The simplification proposal would lower costs for everyone and so narrow the advantage Polymed gained by paying the entry cost early (Section B5). <!-- AR26 p.35; DKAGM30 p.3; TR2Q26 p.10 -->

**The procurement shield.** The International Procurement Instrument measure is the EU's answer to China's closure of its own hospital market: an EU investigation found that {{WD-0019}} of Chinese public device tenders restrict imported products. Since 30-Jun-2025 Chinese bidders have been excluded from EU device tenders of {{WD-0022}} or more, and winning bidders may source no more than {{F-0239}} of the contract value from China. China responded by restricting European device makers in its own public procurement, per B. Braun. <!-- EU_IPI2025_1197 pp.5-6, 12; BBRAUN_AR25 p.42 -->

We see the measure as a direct, if partial, benefit to Polymed. It removes Chinese makers from the largest European tenders just as Chinese pricing was hurting Polymed's European sales (Section A3), but it does not cover tenders below the threshold, private buyers or distributors' own sourcing, and the content cap still allows some Chinese components.

**The UK.** Management put the UK at about {{CA-0518|range}} of Polymed's revenue in FY25. Management says the India-UK trade agreement cuts UK import duty on most of its products from ~{{CA-0459|range}} to near zero, and that NHS contracts were delayed by {{CA-0290|range}} years after the change of government. <!-- TR4Q25 pp.6-7; TR1Q26 p.10; TR3Q26 p.16 -->

### United States: clearance, quality rules and a tariff path that keeps changing

**Clearance and quality.** Polymed's own FDA 510(k) clearances are all in infusion; its subsidiaries hold clearances for a pain-management catheter (Plan1Health), guiding catheters (PendraCare) and trauma implants (Citieffe) (Sections A4.1 and A8). The FDA's Quality Management System Regulation, which incorporates ISO 13485:2016, took effect on 2-Feb-2026, and management says the FDA now expects testing in US laboratories. The FDA has also turned against Chinese syringes: it advised US providers in 2024 to use syringes "not manufactured in China, if possible", and put several Chinese makers on import alert. <!-- FDA510K_POLY p.1; FDA510K_CIT p.1; FDA510K_PEN p.1; FDA510K_P1H p.1; FR_QMSR_240202 pp.1-2; TR2Q26 p.11; FDA_SYRINGE_CHINA p.1; FDA_RU240517 p.1 -->

**Tariffs.** Medical instruments enter the US free of ordinary (MFN) duty, so every tariff Polymed has faced since 2025 has come from emergency or trade-remedy measures that were imposed, raised, struck down and replaced in quick succession.

Four points follow from the table:
1. **India's rate is back to where management can sell.** The MD called the Aug-2025 rate of {{CA-0379}} "completely untenable" and said customers were waiting; by Aug-2026 he said "the duty is only {{CA-0077}}" and that the US pathway was still open. <!-- TR2Q26 p.11; TR1Q27S p.12 -->
2. **India now pays less than China on the products that matter.** On syringes and needles China carries the {{F-0204}} Section 301 duty; on all medical instruments the forced-labour action charges China {{F-0223}} against India's {{F-0221}}. The forced-labour action came only in Jul-2026, so we infer that it was the Section 301 duty on Chinese syringes and needles from Sep-2024, with the FDA's stance on Chinese syringes, that opened the US syringe market to Indian suppliers in 2025 (Section B5). <!-- FR_301MOD_240918 p.9; FR_301PRES_260728 p.4; FR_301ACT_260728 p.7 -->
3. **Section 232 is the next risk for everyone.** The Commerce Department opened a national-security investigation into imports of PPE, medical consumables and medical equipment on 2-Sep-2025. The statute gave Commerce {{F-0260}} from initiation to report to the President, a period that ended in late May-2026, and a finding could add tariffs on top of country rates whatever the origin. BD and ICU Medical both flag it as a risk; we found no published report or action as of 24-Sep-2026. <!-- FR_232PPE_250926 p.1; BIS_232_LIST p.1; BD_10K25 p.1; ICUI_10K25 p.1 -->
4. **India is also a target.** A Section 301 investigation opened in Mar-2026 into excess capacity lists health among India's surplus sectors and cites a bilateral goods surplus of {{F-0225}} in 2025; no action has followed yet. In the Feb-2026 joint statement India agreed to "address long-standing barriers to the trade in U.S. medical devices". <!-- FR_301INIT_260317b p.5; WH_USIND_JS_2602 p.1 -->

For Polymed the direct exposure is small: management expects US exports of {{CA-0078|range}} in FY27. The indirect effects are larger. US tariffs on China push Chinese product into Polymed's other markets (Section B2), and US tariffs on EU-made goods also apply to the European subsidiaries' US sales; B. Braun reported a {{E-0250}} US tariff on EU-made medical technology in 2025, before the IEEPA duties were struck down. <!-- TR1Q27S p.12; BBRAUN_AR25 p.41 -->

**Freight.** The Drewry World Container Index roughly doubled between April and September 2026, and the Shanghai-Rotterdam lane rose by about two-fifths. In September Drewry linked the latest rise to Transpacific demand ahead of China's Golden Week and carrier capacity management, and noted a gradual return of Suez services but renewed risk in the Red Sea; war-risk surcharges and insurance add to the cost of Gulf cargo. Polymed sells ex-factory in many markets, so customers bear much of the freight, but management says logistics costs have risen {{CA-0067|range}} times in six months and that longer delivery times have slowed collections. <!-- DREWRY_WCI_260402 p.1; DREWRY_WCI_2609 p.1; HOWDEN_HORMUZ_2603 p.11; TR3Q24b p.15; TR1Q27S pp.8, 14 -->

We draw two conclusions for Polymed. First, in the regimes that matter most Polymed has already paid the entry cost where it sells today: it holds MDR certificates for its range in Europe and US clearances for its safety catheters and IV sets, which makes these regimes barriers to others more than to Polymed in those products. Second, the risks are now mostly in trade policy and logistics rather than regulation: Section 232 in the US, the West Asia war, and whether the EU's shield against Chinese bidders holds.

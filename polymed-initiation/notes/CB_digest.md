## A1 Revenue architecture

### Key facts
- FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 1,875.3 cr (+12.3%): organic Rs 1,759.5 cr plus acquisition impact Rs 115.3 cr. PendraCare is consolidated from 23-Sep-2025 and Citieffe from 07-Nov-2025 [DK4Q26 p.5, p.10]
- 1QFY27 revenue was Rs 525.4 cr (+30.3%): organic 453.1 (+12.4%) plus acquisitions 72.3. Domestic grew 16.2% and international 36.7% (10.4% organic) [DK1Q27 p.21, p.26]
- 1QFY27 was the first five-way therapy split: infusion 259.2 (+11.1%), orthopaedics 49.2 (Citieffe), renal 43.2 (-3.8%), cardiology 28.6 (vs 2.9), others 141.9, export incentive 3.3. Infusion is now 50% of revenue [DK1Q27 p.27]
- Mix shift FY20 to FY26: infusion fell from 68% to 53% of revenue, renal rose from 4% to 10% and others from 28% to 37% [DK1Q27 p.6]. Revenue CAGR FY20–26 was 14% for infusion, 39% for renal, 23% for others and 18% in total [DK1Q27 p.6]
- Geography FY20 to FY26: India rose from 27% to 31%, Europe from 29% to 32%, and RoW fell from 46% to 37% [DK1Q27 p.4]. International was ~73% in FY20 and ~69% in FY26 [DK1Q27 p.9, p.10]
- Standalone (India entity) FY26 revenue was only +3.8% (1,662.5), against +22.5% in FY25 (1,601.8) [DK4Q26 p.9; DK4Q25 p.14]
- Standalone volumes: 143.6 crore devices sold in FY25 (+24.7%) and 37.2 crore in 4QFY25 (+24.6%) [DK4Q25 p.10, p.11]. "Top 5 products" revenue was Rs 959.4 cr in FY25 (+22.3%); the products are not named [DK4Q25 p.11]
- Acquired entities (EUR): Plan1Health revenue grew from €3,239K (2020) to €10,514K (2025), with EBITDA margin rising from 3.7% to 13.4% [DK1Q27 p.12]. PendraCare CY24 revenue was €9.9 mn with EBITDA €1.4 mn; CY25 was ~€8.2 mn with EBITDA ~€84K [DKPEN p.11; DK1Q27 p.13]. Citieffe CY24 revenue was €17.3 mn with EBITDA €3.1 mn; CY25 was ~€17.7 mn with ~€3.15 mn [DKCIT p.12; DK1Q27 p.14]

FY25 others is 506.7, against 468.3 for the quarters. FY26 infusion is 997.3 [DK4Q26 p.12], against 1,055.7 for the quarters as first reported (1,037.2 using the restated 1QFY26). FY26 others is 690.4, against 630.5. The same gap exists in the standalone FY25 data: 9M 800.3 + 4Q 252.8 against a full year of 1,013.6 [DK3Q25 p.17; DK4Q25 p.16; DKAGM30 p.22]. Revenue of roughly Rs 37–60 cr moves from infusion into "others" in the annual tables. The reported FY26 infusion decline of -1.5% is therefore on the annual classification; summed quarters are roughly flat [CB check]
- **1QFY26 therapy split restated** in the 1QFY27 deck. As first reported: infusion 251.8, renal 43.5, others 107.9. Restated: infusion 233.3, renal 44.9, cardiology 2.9, others 119.7, export incentive 2.4 [DK1Q26 p.17; DK2Q26 p.17; DK1Q27 p.27]

The full-year Europe/RoW figures also differ from 9M + 4Q by about ±3–5 cr [CB check: FY26 Europe 423.5 + 170.1 = 593.6 against 597.0; FY25 552.6 against 557.4] [DK3Q26 p.12; DK4Q26 p.11]

## A2 Business model
- Integrated "flywheel": in-house tool design and tool manufacturing, moulding, automated assembly, packaging and captive sterilization, plus in-house R&D, regulatory affairs, clinical support and distribution [DK2Q26 p.27; DK1Q27 p.5]
- Scale indicators changed over time. Products 200+ became 225+ [DK1Q26 p.22; DK1Q27 p.4]. Specialities rose from 12 to 13 with orthopaedics added [DK1Q26 p.22; DK4Q26 p.18]. Employees were 3,000+ (May-25), 3,500+ (Sep/Nov-25) and 3,300+ (May-26) [DK4Q25 p.6; DKAGM30 p.11; DK2Q26 p.23; DK4Q26 p.18]. Distributors 900+ became 1,000+ [DK2Q26 p.23; DK1Q27 p.4]. Countries served: 125+ [DK1Q27 p.4]
- Sales force: 475+ associates (Feb-25), 510+ (May-25), 530+ (Aug/Sep-25), 540+ (Nov-25), 570+ (May-26) and 590+ direct-sales reps in India, US, Italy and Mexico (Aug-26) [DK3Q25 p.6; DK4Q25 p.5; DK1Q26 p.22; DK2Q26 p.23; DK4Q26 p.18; DK1Q27 p.5]. Net additions were 78 in FY25, 22 in 1QFY26, 62 in 1HFY26 and 97 in 9MFY26 [DK4Q25 p.11; DK1Q26 p.5; DK2Q26 p.4; DK3Q26 p.4]
- India channel: 630+ distributors (FY25) and 650+ (May-26); 260 overseas distributors (FY25) [DK4Q25 p.5; DK4Q26 p.40]
- Clinical engagement is a stated go-to-market lever:
  - 60+ clinical experts and 4,000+ customer engagement programmes a year [DK1Q27 p.5]
  - PACE Academy (400+ nurses trained from 52 hospitals; 4–5 more academies planned) [DK4Q26 p.40]
  - IV Talks with INS (6 sessions reaching 6,000+ nurses in 1QFY26; 518 nursing engagements in 2QFY26) [DK1Q26 p.7; DK2Q26 p.11]
  - SARATHII AI-based sales training (3,000+ sessions for 265 people; international roll-out in FY27) [DK4Q26 p.40; DKAGM31 p.3]
- Channel mix is distributor-led in export markets. Direct presence exists in India, the US (PolyHealth Medical, set up 2021), Italy and Mexico (through Citieffe), plus new subsidiaries in the UK and Brazil [DK2Q26 p.24; DK4Q26 p.37; DK1Q27 p.5]
- Product catalogue by speciality covers vascular access, infusion, oncology, dialysis, transfusion, diagnostics, surgery and wound drainage, urology, gastroenterology, anaesthesia and respiratory, critical care, cardiology and orthopaedics [DK2Q26 p.29, p.30]
- Strategy phases: 2020–24 innovation and global expansion; 2025–27 build cardiology, critical care, oncology and renal, and go direct; 2028+ premium markets and developed-market growth [DK2Q26 p.26]. Later reframed as Polymed 1.0 (FY1996–2020), 2.0 (FY2020–26) and 3.0 (FY2027+) [DK1Q27 p.8]

## A3 FY26 slowdown and guidance record
- FY26 growth was weak. Consolidated revenue rose 12.3%, but standalone rose only 3.8%. Consolidated operating EBITDA was 457.7 against 458.6 in FY25 (-0.2%), with the margin down 305 bps to 24.4%. PAT fell 5.3% to 320.7 [DK4Q26 p.9, p.10]
- The slowdown was concentrated in the first half:
  - Standalone quarterly revenue ran 384, 417, 419, 443 [DK4Q26 p.6]
  - 1QFY26 international revenue fell 0.9% and Europe 6.7% [DK1Q26 p.16]
  - 2QFY26 international rose only 1.4% while Europe fell 9.6% [DK2Q26 p.16]
  - Consolidated H2 (1,029) was 21% above H1 (847), "in line with the guidance provided" [DK4Q26 p.6]
- FY26 one-offs:
  - Labour Code provision of Rs 6.8 cr, booked as an extraordinary item [DK4Q26 p.9, p.10]

  - An undisclosed one-time provision for regulatory and employee costs in a subsidiary, which hit 4QFY26 (margin 21.0%) [DK4Q26 p.5]
- Definition changes affect comparability:
  - From 2QFY26 decks, operating EBITDA includes the share of associate profit. FY25 EBITDA was 452.8 in DK4Q25 and 458.6 in DK4Q26; 4QFY25 was 119.5 and 121.9 [DK4Q25 p.13; DK4Q26 p.10]

- Working capital stretched (standalone). Debtor days rose from 78 to 98, inventory days from 136 to 152, and the cash conversion cycle from 183 to 215 days (FY25 to FY26) [DK4Q26 p.13]

### A4.1 Infusion therapy
- The company claims to be the 3rd largest IV cannula maker globally [DK1Q27 p.7, p.9]; DKAGM31 softens this to "amongst the largest" [DKAGM31 p.12]. Infusion was the entry segment in 1997 [DK4Q26 p.21]
- Infusion revenue as reported: FY25 1,012.4 and FY26 997.3 (-1.5%) [DK4Q26 p.12]; 1QFY27 259.2 (+11.1%) [DK1Q27 p.27]. See the A1 flag on annual versus quarterly classification [DK4Q26 p.12; DK3Q26 p.13]
- 1QFY27 drivers: higher-value products and price hikes in India; international recovery despite Middle East sales falling 32% [DK1Q27 p.27]. Company-wide average price increase of ~4–5% in 1QFY27 versus 4QFY26 [DK1Q27 p.22]
- Product examples: safety IV cannula, prefilled saline syringe, needle-free connectors, three-way stopcock, extension lines and disinfecting cap [DK1Q27 p.4, p.9]. The Panama catalogue shows the IV cannula range (14G–26G), the Nouvo safety closed IV catheter system (power-injector rated to 300 PSI) and a stopcock of Indian origin [DKNOV25 p.2, p.6, p.7, p.9]

### A4.2 Vascular access, oncology and Plan1Health
- Plan1Health (Amaro, Italy; founded 1995) was acquired 100% for EV ~€4.2 mn. The acquisition year is stated as 2019 [DK1Q27 p.12; DK2Q26 p.24] but as 2018 on DK1Q27 p.9. Its business covers long-term infusion devices, oncology products and an innovative pain-control device. It is a Class III implantables specialist and sole approved supplier to one of the largest OEMs for Class III implants [DK1Q27 p.12; DKAGM31 p.17]
- Since acquisition, revenue has grown 3.2x and EBITDA 11.8x, with margin up 970 bps (2020–25) [DK1Q27 p.12]. Other decks state "20%+ CAGR" with margins up "~670 bp" [DKAGM31 p.17] or "~600 bps" [DK4Q26 p.22]
- The "P1H playbook" is to be replicated for PendraCare and Citieffe: faster launches, India manufacturing to cut cost, direct sales, wider distribution and joint R&D/marketing [DK1Q27 p.11, p.12]
- Launches: 1 Fr and 2 Fr neonatal PICC lines ("one of the first" to make them in India), antimicrobial CVC, tear-away introducer, midlines, PICC ports and safety Huber needle [DKAGM31 p.3, p.5; DK4Q26 p.30]. Two contract-manufacturing deals were signed with US and Hong Kong companies in vascular access and pain management [DK1Q26 p.7]
- P1H in Europe: 20+ distributors, whole portfolio (4 categories) approved, 1 approval in the pipeline. In the US: 1 approval for an OEM, FDA submissions under evaluation [DK4Q26 p.38, p.39]

### A4.3 Renal

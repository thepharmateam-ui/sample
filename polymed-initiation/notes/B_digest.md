## A1 Revenue architecture
- In FY10 the business reported one segment, "Medical Devices", with two market domains: domestic and export [AR10 p.11].
- Standalone product revenue was disclosed only under the old Schedule VI. IV cannula sales were Rs 9,145 lakh in FY12, Rs 10,864 lakh in FY13, Rs 15,378 lakh in FY15 and Rs 14,603 lakh in FY16, so the category fell in FY16. Blood bags were Rs 1,790, 2,205, 2,231 and 3,185 lakh in the same years. [AR13 p.49; AR16 p.94]
- CRISIL's figures on dependence on the core franchise:
  - IV cannula was 56–57% of FY22 revenue [CRR2212 p.1].
  - Infusion therapy was about 67% of FY23 sales [CRR2402 p.1] and about 67% of FY24 sales [CRR2503a p.1]. The June 2026 rationale gives 69% for FY23 and about 53% for FY26 [CRR2606 p.1].
- Own-brand sales were about 70–72% of FY22 revenue and about 70% "in recent fiscals" (Feb-2024). The rest is by implication OEM or contract business; the split is not stated. [CRR2212 p.1; CRR2402 p.1]
- Exports have been about two-thirds of sales throughout:
  - 65.57% of revenue in FY16 [AR16 p.48]
  - about 70% in FY19, FY20 and FY22 [AR19 p.67; AR20 p.70; AR22 p.47]
  - 69% in FY21 [AR21 p.59]
  - 69.63% in FY24 and 72.14% in 1QFY25 [AR24 p.70]
- Europe was about 40% of FY22 sales [CR23 p.7].
- Ind AS geographic split of standalone product sales: domestic grew from Rs 12,638 lakh in FY18 to Rs 40,772 lakh in FY24; exports grew from Rs 37,025 lakh to Rs 88,943 lakh [AR19 p.142; AR24 p.118].
- Consolidated revenue in FY21–FY23 includes "sales related to foreign subsidiaries" of Rs 3.9–4.7k lakh [AR21 p.176; AR23 p.147].
- FY15 PAT includes a one-time Rs 1,957.80 lakh exceptional income "from one of its customer towards settlement of a contract". The customer is not named. [AR16 p.104]

## A2 Business model (cost structure, pricing, channel, working capital)
- Vitromed does much of the contract manufacturing. Job-work payments to Vitromed Healthcare make up the bulk of total job-work charges: Rs 1,044 lakh of Rs 1,119 lakh in FY10, Rs 3,004 lakh of Rs 3,198 lakh in FY16 and Rs 7,653 lakh of Rs 9,101 lakh in FY24. [AR10 p.36, p.41; AR16 p.95, p.99; AR24 p.121, p.139]
- Raw materials were largely imported:
  - The imported share of raw materials consumed was 61.89% in FY09, 67.55% in FY10, 69.48% in FY12, 71.17% in FY13, 81.59% in FY15 and 79.58% in FY16 [AR10 p.47; AR13 p.58; AR16 p.103].
  - The FY19 AR says "the majority of raw materials are being imported" [AR19 p.131].
  - CRISIL gives imports of 50–60% of requirements, which acts as a natural hedge against export receivables [CR23 p.9].
- Pricing: raw-material price increases are "passed on to customers, but with a lag". The company keeps 3–4 months of raw-material inventory, mainly plastics. [CR23 p.8]
- Working capital, on CRISIL-adjusted consolidated figures:

  | | FY20 | FY21 | FY22 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Gross current asset days | 160 | 167 | 175 |
  | Debtor days | 74 | 77 | 85 |
  | Inventory days | 80 | 82 | 88 |
  | Creditor days | 105 | 85 | 89 |

  Cash flow from operations was only Rs 58 cr in FY22 [CR23 p.10].
- Bank-limit utilisation stayed moderate:

  | Period | Utilisation |
  |---|---|
  | 12 months to Aug-2022 | 40–50% |
  | 12 months to Oct-2023 | 46% |
  | 12 months to Feb-2025 | about 58% |
  | 12 months to Mar-2026 | 56.8% |

  [CRR2212 p.1; CRR2402 p.1; CRR2503a p.1; CRR2606 p.1]
- Customer concentration is low. No customer has been 10% or more of sales in any year from FY19 to FY24. Within exports, the largest customer was at most 4% of sales in FY21. [AR19 p.142; AR24 p.145; CR23 p.7]
- Domestic channel:
  - FY16: more than 40 distributors, more than 1,300 dealers and more than 225 sales staff [AR16 p.50]
  - FY19: more than 25 super-distributors, more than 10 agents and more than 1,300 dealers [AR19 p.66]
  - 30-Jun-2024: 506 distributors, 440 sales staff and 8,000 hospitals [AR24 p.70-71]
- Export channel: more than 150 distributors (FY16), more than 200 (FY19), more than 220 (FY21), more than 250 (FY22–23) and 260 (1QFY25). Many have local or regional exclusivity. [AR16 p.49; AR19 p.67; AR21 p.60; AR22 p.48; AR24 p.71]
- CRISIL says the company supplies "other big players, such as Atroge and Asept" (spelling as printed). It also sells overseas through distributors under the PolyMed brand and deals with hospital purchasing groups. CRISIL adds that India's low-cost image "limits the company's ability to command a premium". [CR23 p.7]
- Export incentives peaked at Rs 1,665 lakh in FY19 and fell to Rs 420 lakh in FY22 before recovering to Rs 786 lakh in FY24 [AR19 p.113; AR22 p.89; AR24 p.119].
- Freight nearly doubled in FY24, from Rs 642 lakh to Rs 1,129 lakh. The AR gives no reason. [AR24 p.122]

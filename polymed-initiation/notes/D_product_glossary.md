## A4.1 Infusion therapy

### IV cannula, standard (peripheral intravenous catheter)
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 pp.7-8; CATEXP24 pp.6-8; WEB_VASCULAR_ACCESS p.1; WEB_CARDIOLOGY p.1; CAT25 p.2]:
  - Ported and winged: Polyflon, Ultra I.P. (domestic) and Polyflex (port, snap-fit cap, perforated wings).
  - Without a port: Polycan (perforated wings; also a "long I.V. cannula"), Polywin and Polywin Plus.
  - Pen type, with no port and no wings: Polypen and Polyon.
  - Neonatal: Polyneo (24G) and Neonovo (26G).
  - Polycath, which has an integrated three-way stopcock.
  - Quick-flashback versions with "Adva" needle technology, including Nouvo Flex Adva.
  - Sizes 14G-26G.
  - Typically packed 50 or 100 per duplex box.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A short, flexible plastic catheter (FEP/PTFE or polyurethane) mounted over a steel needle.
  - After the vein is punctured, the needle is withdrawn and the catheter stays in a peripheral vein, usually in the hand or forearm. It is used to give fluids, drugs, blood and contrast.
  - Gauges are colour-coded, from orange at 14G to violet at 26G.
  - Ported designs have a top injection port and are common in India, Europe and emerging markets. Non-ported designs dominate in the US.
  - Quick-flashback designs show blood as soon as the tip enters the vein, which improves first-attempt success.
- **Unit of demand [general description].**
  - One cannula per insertion attempt, so failed attempts use extra units.
  - Cannulas are resited when they fail (phlebitis, infiltration, dislodgement) or at an interval set by hospital protocol, so one admission often uses several.
  - Most inpatients receive at least one. Demand tracks admissions, day-care procedures and emergency visits.
- **Users and buyers [general description].**
  - Users: nurses, junior doctors, anaesthetists and emergency staff.
  - Buyers: hospital purchase departments, group purchasing organizations and government tenders, usually through distributors.
  - It is a high-volume, price-sensitive item.

### Safety IV cannula (passive and active)
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 pp.6-7; CATEXP24 pp.9-12; WEB_VASCULAR_ACCESS p.1]:
  - Passive, self-activating needle guard: Polysafety (snap-fit cap), Polysafety Pro, Poly Safety Adva, Polywin Safety, Polycan Safety and Polywin Plus Safety.
  - Active, user-activated: Polyguard+ and Polyshield (push-off tab).
  - US 510(k)s [FDA510K_POLY p.1]:
    - K230616: Polyguard and Polyshield.
    - K231401: Polysafety BC, Polywin Safety and their Adva versions.
    - K252677: Polyshield.
    - K252513: Polywin Safety size range.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A peripheral IV cannula whose needle tip is covered by a shield as it is withdrawn.
  - Passive guards engage automatically. Active designs need the user to press a button or tab.
  - The purpose is to prevent needlestick injuries and the blood-borne infections (HIV, hepatitis B and C) they transmit to staff.
  - Safety-engineered sharps are required in the US (Needlestick Safety and Prevention Act) and the EU (sharps directive 2010/32/EU). Adoption is rising in Indian private hospitals.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** As for a standard cannula: one per insertion attempt, at a price premium.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** As for a standard cannula. In regulated markets the safety version is the default purchase.

### Blood-control safety IV cannula
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.7; CATEXP24 p.13; WEB_VASCULAR_ACCESS p.1]:
  - Polysafety BC (non-ported) and Polysafety-BC Ported.
  - Adva quick-flashback needle.
  - Sizes 18G-24G.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Adds a valve inside the catheter hub that stops blood flowing back out after the needle is removed, until a luer device is connected.
  - This reduces blood spillage and staff exposure. US demand has shifted towards blood-control designs.
- **Unit of demand, users and buyers [general description].** As for a safety cannula.

### Closed IV catheter system
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.6; CAT25 pp.4-5; FDA510K_POLY p.1]:
  - Nouvo Safety Set: polyurethane catheter with an integrated extension, Micro PolySyte needle-free connector and passive needle guard.
  - Power-injectable to {{D-0857}}; also suitable for subcutaneous infusion.
  - US 510(k) K232234.
  - Packed Duplex 20, Carton 200.
  - Management named "nouvo safety" among FY24 growth products at the foreign subsidiaries [PD24 p.107].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A peripheral cannula with a pre-attached extension set and connector, so the line is never open to air during insertion, plus a stabilizing platform.
  - This reduces contamination, blood exposure and dislodgement.
  - It suits longer peripheral therapy and, when power-rated, CT contrast injection.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per insertion, at a higher price point than a standard cannula.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Hospital nursing and vascular-access teams.

### Winged infusion (scalp-vein) set, including the safety version
- **Polymed range** [CATEXP24 p.21; WEB_VASCULAR_ACCESS p.1; PD24 p.173; PD21 p.86]:
  - Safety winged infusion set: short, sharp needle with a safety shield.
  - "Safety scalp vein sets" are named in the infusion vertical.
  - PD21 described safety scalp-vein sets as stock-in-trade made by contract manufacturer Vitromed Healthcare.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A short steel needle with flexible plastic "butterfly" wings and a length of tubing ending in a luer connector.
  - It is used for short infusions, single injections, blood sampling and small veins (children, the elderly, scalp veins in neonates).
  - The steel needle stays in the vein, so the set suits short use only.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per procedure or short infusion.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Nurses and phlebotomists; bought with other IV disposables.

### IV infusion sets (gravity administration sets)
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 pp.14-15; CATEXP24 pp.25-29; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1; CAT25 p.3]:
  - Standard sets:
    - Polyfusion, vented and non-vented.
    - Polyfusion W (winged spike) and Polyfusion Plus.
    - Novofusion and Novofusion BCV (back-check valve).
    - Ventrifusion.
  - Auto-air-stop sets with priming filter: Autofusion and Autofusion Nuovo.
  - Ultrafusion: stopcock plus priming filter.
  - Microfusion: paediatric micro-drip, about 60 drops/ml. Standard sets deliver about 20 drops/ml [CATDOM25 p.15].
  - Photofusion: light-protective for photosensitive drugs.
  - Oncofusion: 0.2 micron in-line filter.
  - PVC-free versions.
  - US 510(k) K220312 covers Polyfusion IV administration sets [FDA510K_POLY p.1].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** The tubing that connects an IV bottle or bag to the patient's cannula.
  - Parts: a spike (vented for rigid bottles), a drip chamber, a roller clamp for rate control, a Y-injection site and a luer connector.
  - Specialized sets add:
    - Air-stop filters, which stop air entering when the container empties.
    - In-line filters, which remove particles and microbes (used for oncology drugs).
    - Opaque or amber tubing, which protects light-sensitive drugs.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One set per IV line.
  - Sets are replaced at protocol intervals, which are shorter when the set carries blood, lipids or propofol, and whenever the line is re-established.
  - Demand scales with IV-therapy days.
- **Users and buyers [general description].**
  - Users: nurses.
  - Buyers: hospitals, largely on price, through tenders and distributors.

### Flow-regulator and measured-volume (burette) sets
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.16; CATEXP24 pp.30-31; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1]:
  - Polyflo Novo IV flow-regulator extension set.
  - Polytrol Micro set: micro-drip with flow regulator.
  - Polyvol and Polyvol Pro burette sets (110 ml and 150 ml chambers).
  - Polypedia: DEHP-free, PVC-free burette.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].**
  - A flow regulator is a dial marked in ml per hour that sets a gravity infusion rate more precisely than a roller clamp. It is a low-cost alternative to an infusion pump.
  - A burette set has a graduated chamber between the container and the patient, so only a set volume can run in. It is used in paediatrics and for diluting intermittent drugs, to avoid fluid overload.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per line, replaced with the administration set.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Paediatric wards and wards without infusion pumps.

### Extension lines and T-extension sets
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.13; CATEXP24 pp.23-24,35; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1; WEB_CRITICAL_CARE p.1]:
  - Low-pressure lines (to 4.5 bar).
  - High-pressure lines (to 55 bar), including a version for photosensitive drugs.
  - PVC-free high-pressure lines, straight and coiled.
  - Extension lines with needle-free connectors.
  - T-type extension sets.
  - Lengths 10-300 cm.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Small-bore tubing with luer ends, used to:
  - lengthen an IV line;
  - let the patient move;
  - keep repeated connections away from the cannula hub;
  - connect syringe pumps.
  - High-pressure lines are used with syringe pumps, invasive pressure monitoring and contrast injectors.
  - A T-extension is a short piece fixed at the cannula, with a side port.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One or more per IV line or syringe-pump infusion, changed with the line. ICU patients use many.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** ICU, operating-theatre and ward nurses; hospital purchasing.

### Three-way stopcocks
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.13; CATEXP24 pp.35-36; CATCOMP24 p.15; CAT25 p.6; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1]:
  - Polyway.
  - Polyway Pro: lipid-resistant, to 5.5 bar.
  - Polyway Pro Click: 45° click positions.
  - Stopcock with extension tube and the Nouvo Click extension.
  - Also sold as components.
  - Management named "three way stop cock" among FY24 growth products at the foreign subsidiaries [PD24 p.107].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A small valve with three luer ports and a rotating tap.
  - It lets two infusions share one cannula and allows bolus drugs or samples without disconnecting the line.
  - Lipid-resistant versions tolerate propofol and lipid emulsions, which crack standard materials.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One or more per IV line, replaced with the line. Use is heavy in ICU, operating theatres and anaesthesia.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Nurses and anaesthetists; hospital purchasing; also bought by other device makers as a component.

### Manifolds (infusion)
- **Polymed range** [CATEXP24 p.32; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1]:
  - 2- to 6-way manifolds, lipid-resistant, also with needle-free connectors.
  - Described as "suitable for intervention radiology and cardiology procedures" and for IV delivery.
  - The Neofold Pro cardiology manifold is covered under A4.8.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A bank of stopcocks on one body.
  - In the ICU, several infusions share one access.
  - In the cath lab, the operator switches between contrast, saline flush and pressure measurement.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per multi-infusion set-up or per interventional procedure.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** ICUs and cath labs.

### Needle-free connectors (NFCs)
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 pp.9-10; CATEXP24 pp.24,33; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1]:
  - Safe Conekt: negative displacement; mono, duo and trio.
  - Micro PolySyte: neutral displacement, split septum, dead space {{D-0858}}; stand-alone or with extension tubing.
  - PolySyte access devices: IV-bag access, vented bag spike, multi-dose vial access cap and spike, and a red ring for arterial lines.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A valve fitted to a catheter hub or extension line.
  - It opens only when a luer syringe or line is connected, so drugs are given without needles and the line stays closed between uses.
  - Designs are classed by the fluid movement on disconnection (negative, positive or neutral displacement). This affects blood reflux into the catheter and occlusion.
  - The hub must be disinfected before each access to limit catheter-related bloodstream infection (CRBSI).
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per catheter lumen or line.
  - Replaced at protocol intervals, commonly with the administration set, or when damaged.
  - Demand scales with central and peripheral line-days, especially in ICUs.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Nurses; bought by hospitals, most heavily in the US and Europe.

### Prefilled saline flush syringes
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.9; CATEXP24 p.34; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1]:
  - PolyFlush: 0.9% sodium chloride in 3, 5 and 10 ml sizes.
  - All sizes share a 10 ml barrel diameter.
  - Single-sterile and double-sterile (sterile-field) versions.
  - Gamma sterilized.
  - Management named prefilled syringes as a driver of FY24 product-sales and export growth [PD24 pp.106-107].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Ready-to-use syringes of sterile saline.
  - They flush IV catheters before and after drug doses and keep lines open.
  - They replace syringes filled by hand from ampoules or bags, which saves nursing time and cuts contamination and errors.
  - The 10 ml barrel keeps injection pressure low enough not to damage catheters.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** Several per catheter per day: one before and after each intermittent dose, plus scheduled flushes of idle lines.
- **Users and buyers [general description].**
  - Heaviest use is in US and European hospitals and home-infusion services. Indian adoption is lower.

### Disinfecting caps
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.34; CATEXP24 p.22; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1]:
  - Polyswab disinfecting cap, compatible with needle-free and luer access systems.
  - Gamma sterile.
  - Protection period per catalogue: {{D-0866}}.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A cap containing an alcohol-soaked sponge that screws onto a needle-free connector between uses.
  - It disinfects the connector passively and keeps it covered, replacing or backing up the manual "scrub the hub" step.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per connector, each time the line is accessed and the cap is removed.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Mainly US and European acute-care and home-infusion providers.

### Vial-access and transfer spikes
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.12; CATEXP24 pp.37,90; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1; WEB_CRITICAL_CARE p.1]:
  - Polyspike: vented vial-access spike; the red-coded version is compatible with cytotoxic drugs.
  - Polyspike V-Plus: with an integrated valve.
  - Transfer spike.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].**
  - A vial-access spike pierces the vial stopper and provides a luer port with an air filter. Several doses can then be drawn by syringe without needles and without creating a vacuum in the vial.
  - A transfer spike links two containers (for example vial to bag) for reconstitution.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per vial, or per multi-dose vial for its in-use life.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Nurses and hospital pharmacies, including chemotherapy compounding units.

### Obturators, stoppers, caps and injection sites
- **Polymed range** [CATEXP24 pp.35,89-90; WEB_INFUSION_THERAPY p.1; WEB_VASCULAR_ACCESS p.1]:
  - Obturator: stylet or mandrin with luer lock, 14G-22G.
  - Injection stopper.
  - Luer lock.
  - Luer-lock injection site.
  - Universal cap.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Small closures for open hubs.
  - An obturator fills the lumen of an idle cannula to keep it closed.
  - Stoppers and caps close luer hubs. Injection sites add a pierceable septum for injections.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One or more per idle cannula or line. Unit value is very low and volume very high.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Nurses; hospital purchasing.

### IV-site fixation dressing
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.33; CATEXP24 p.89; WEB_VASCULAR_ACCESS p.1]:
  - Novoguard cannula fixator: transparent, water-resistant film with a recording label.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A transparent adhesive dressing that secures the cannula and covers the insertion site while leaving it visible.
  - It reduces dislodgement and site infection.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per cannula insertion, changed when loose or soiled.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Nurses; bought with cannulas.

### CVP manometer
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.34; PD24 p.173]:
  - Graduated manometer tube with a three-way stopcock and extension tube.
  - Named in the infusion vertical.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A graduated fluid column connected through a stopcock to a central venous catheter.
  - It measures central venous pressure, a guide to fluid status, without electronic monitors.
  - It is used where transducer-based monitoring is not available.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per patient monitoring episode.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** ICUs and wards in lower-resource settings.

## A4.2 Vascular access and oncology

### Central venous catheter (CVC) kits
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.17; CATEXP24 pp.16-17; WEBPROD_CARDIO p.13; OTNOV pp.1-4; WEB_VASCULAR_ACCESS p.1; WEB_CRITICAL_CARE p.1]:
  - Domestic: "Polymed CVC" Mono, Duo, Trio and Quadruple, with or without a needle-free connector.
  - Export: Novocent and Novocent Protekt (adds an NFC and a lipid-resistant stopcock).
  - Novocent Pro kit.
  - Kit contents include:
    - nitinol J-tip guidewire;
    - Y-introducer needle with valve;
    - dilator;
    - syringe;
    - scalpel;
    - catheter holder.
  - Single-, double- and triple-lumen CVCs make up the "vascular access" vertical [PD24 p.174].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A longer polyurethane catheter placed in a large central vein (internal jugular, subclavian or femoral).
  - It is inserted by the Seldinger technique (needle, guidewire, dilator), with the tip near the heart.
  - Multiple lumens allow, at the same time:
    - incompatible drugs;
    - vasopressors;
    - parenteral nutrition;
    - irritant infusions;
    - central venous pressure measurement.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One kit per insertion, left in place from days to a few weeks.
  - Demand tracks ICU admissions, major surgery and resuscitation.
- **Users and buyers [general description].**
  - Users: anaesthetists, intensivists and emergency physicians.
  - Buyers: ICUs and operating theatres through hospital purchasing.
  - The kit price is well above peripheral devices.

### PICC (peripherally inserted central catheter) and PICC-port
- **Polymed range**:
  - PICC kits [CATDOM25 p.18]:
    - power-injectable;
    - compatible with ECG tip confirmation;
    - kit includes a 21G echogenic needle, nitinol guidewire, introducer, dilator, NFC and sutureless securement.
  - Plan1 Health's HealthPICC range [CATEXP24 pp.19-20].
  - PICC-Port [CATEXP24 p.46].
  - PICC lines and "health – peripherally inserted central catheter" are named in the oncology vertical [PD24 p.173].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A long catheter inserted through an upper-arm vein under ultrasound, with the tip advanced to the superior vena cava.
  - It is used for therapy lasting weeks to months: chemotherapy, long antibiotic courses, parenteral nutrition and frequent blood sampling.
  - Power-injectable PICCs allow CT contrast.
  - A PICC-port is an implantable port placed in the arm rather than the chest.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One kit per treatment course, plus maintenance items (NFCs, flushes, dressings, securement).
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Placed by vascular-access nurses, interventional radiologists and anaesthetists; bought by oncology and tertiary hospitals.

### Midlines and mini-midlines
- **Polymed range**:
  - Poly Lead (Polylead) mini-midlines, over-the-needle (OTN) and over-the-wire (OTW), power-injectable to 300 psi [CATDOM25 p.11; CATEXP24 p.18; WEB_VASCULAR_ACCESS p.1; WEB_CRITICAL_CARE p.1].
  - HealthMID midline (Plan1 Health) [CATEXP24 p.20].
  - Midline and mini-midline catheters also sold as components [CATCOMP24 pp.6-7].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Peripheral catheters longer than a standard cannula.
  - They are placed in an upper-arm vein, with the tip below the armpit (not central).
  - They fill the gap between a short cannula and a PICC, for therapy lasting days to a few weeks with drugs that are safe to give peripherally.
  - They reduce repeated cannulation.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per patient course.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Vascular-access nurses; ICUs and wards.

### Arterial cannulas and catheters
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.12; CATEXP24 pp.14-15; WEB_VASCULAR_ACCESS p.1; WEB_CRITICAL_CARE p.1]:
  - Art-Can Standard and Art-Can Safety arterial cannulas: 20G, ON/OFF switch.
  - Art-Cath over-the-wire arterial catheter: straight or J nitinol guidewire; 3-5 Fr.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A catheter placed in the radial or femoral artery.
  - It connects through a high-pressure line to a pressure transducer for beat-to-beat blood-pressure monitoring.
  - It also allows repeated arterial blood sampling in ICU and major surgery.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per monitored patient episode.
  - It also pulls through high-pressure lines, transducers and ABG syringes.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Anaesthetists and intensivists; ICUs and operating theatres.

### Introducer needles, sheaths and dilators
- **Polymed range**:
  - Safety introducer needle (18G/21G, echogenic) and safety winged set [CATEXP24 p.21].
  - As components [CATCOMP24 pp.13-14,16]:
    - introducer needle;
    - safety introducer needle;
    - Y-introducer needle with valve;
    - tear-away introducer, 3-9 Fr;
    - dilators.
  - Sylera introducer needle on the cardiology page [WEBPROD_CARDIO p.12].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** The needles, peel-away sheaths and dilators used to enter a vessel and pass a guidewire or catheter (Seldinger technique).
  - Echogenic tips show up better on ultrasound.
  - Tear-away sheaths split apart after the catheter is placed.
  - Safety versions shield the needle.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per insertion, often bundled in kits.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Clinicians who build their own insertion sets, and device makers (OEMs) buying them as components.

### Implantable ports (chemoports)
- **Polymed range** (Plan1 Health, Italy) [CATEXP24 pp.38-45]:
  - Healthport and Secureport ports, titanium or plastic:
    - ETI (easy to implant);
    - LP (low profile);
    - PLP (plastic low profile);
    - Power TI;
    - Power LPT/PLP (power-injectable);
    - miniMAX.
  - Supplied as standard kits or all-in-one kits with Huber needle, peel-away introducer, J-guidewire and tunneller.
  - Septum rated for up to {{D-0867}} punctures with a 22G Huber needle; power ports rated to {{D-0868}} [CATEXP24 pp.39,41].
  - PD24 lists "secureport PLP used in chemotherapy" among purchased stock-in-trade [PD24 p.102].
  - The website oncology page lists only "Plan1Health", Huber needles and safety Huber needles [WEB_ONCOLOGY p.1].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A small chamber with a self-sealing silicone septum, implanted under the skin of the chest or arm.
  - It connects to a catheter whose tip sits in a central vein.
  - Nurses access it through the skin with a non-coring Huber needle.
  - It is used for chemotherapy given over months, and for long-term antibiotics, nutrition and blood sampling. It lowers infection risk and lets the patient live normally between treatments.
  - Power ports tolerate CT contrast injection.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One port per patient for the length of treatment (months to years).
  - Ports are implanted by surgeons or interventional radiologists.
  - Demand tracks new chemotherapy starts.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Surgical and medical oncology teams; cancer hospitals.

### Huber needles and safety Huber needles
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.10; CATEXP24 p.47; WEB_ONCOLOGY p.1; WEB_CRITICAL_CARE p.1]:
  - Polygrip Huber needle: non-coring, PVC-free extension, soft wings, clamp and Y-site; 20G and 22G.
  - Safety version.
  - PD24 lists safety Huber needles among its critical-care products [PD24 p.168].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A needle with a deflected tip that pierces the port septum without coring it, attached to a short extension line for infusion or blood draws.
  - Safety versions cover the needle on removal.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per port access.
  - During continuous infusion the needle is replaced at protocol intervals.
  - Demand scales with chemotherapy cycles and the installed base of ports, making it a recurring consumable.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Oncology day-care nurses; cancer hospitals.

### Pain-management catheters (Painfusor)
- **Polymed range**:

- **Unit of demand [general description].** One set per drain, usually one per operation, left in place for a few days.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Surgeons and theatre staff.

### Chest (thoracic) drainage

- **Unit of demand [general description].** One catheter and one drainage system per chest-drain insertion.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Cardiothoracic and general surgeons, emergency and ICU staff.

### Surgical suction and abdominal drainage

- **Users and buyers [general description].** Operating theatres.

### Surgical scrub brush

## A4.8 Cardiology (interventional; entered FY23)

All items are listed at [WEB_CARDIOLOGY p.1], with device specifications in the D digest's A4.8 table. No manufacturer name is printed on any of them.

The devices are used in catheterization labs by interventional cardiologists [general description]:
- **Diagnostic coronary angiography** maps the arteries with contrast.
- **Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI, angioplasty)** opens narrowed arteries with balloons and stents.

### Drug-eluting coronary stents (DES)
- **Polymed range**:
  - RisoR: everolimus-eluting.
  - Serolin Pro: sirolimus-eluting; cobalt-chromium platform; {{D-0872}} struts; biodegradable polymer.
  - Lengths {{D-0874}} and {{D-0875}} [WEBPROD_CARDIO pp.1-2; OTGEN2 p.1; OTGEN3 p.1].
  - A cobalt-chromium stent with biodegradable polymer, "available in both sirolimus and everolimus drugs", also appears in the components catalogue [CATCOMP24 p.8].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A small expandable metal mesh tube, crimped onto a balloon catheter.
  - It is expanded inside a narrowed coronary artery during angioplasty and left in place to keep the artery open.
  - Its polymer coating releases an anti-proliferative drug (sirolimus, everolimus) over weeks to months to prevent re-narrowing.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One or more stents per PCI.
  - Demand tracks PCI volumes.
  - In India, stent prices are capped by NPPA ceiling prices; the 2017 cap is in the ledger as {{D-0472}} [PD24 p.152].
- **Users and buyers [general description].**
  - Users: interventional cardiologists in hospital cath labs.
  - Buyers: hospitals, through distributors and rate contracts.
  - The operator often chooses the brand within the price ceiling.

### PTCA balloon dilatation catheters
- **Polymed range** [WEBPROD_CARDIO pp.3-4; OTEXSC p.1; OTEXNC p.1; OTGEN1 p.1]:
  - Excedo SC: semi-compliant, Pebax; nominal pressure {{D-0869}}; rated burst {{D-0870}}; usable length {{D-0871}}.
  - Excedo NC: non-compliant, nylon; for post-dilatation.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A catheter with a small balloon at its tip, run over a guidewire to the narrowing and inflated to widen it.
  - Semi-compliant balloons grow with pressure and are used to pre-dilate tight lesions before stenting.
  - Non-compliant balloons hold their size at high pressure and are used to fully expand a stent after placement.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** Typically one or more balloons per PCI.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** As for stents.

### Guiding catheters
- **Polymed range**:
  - Vasoguide: 6F and 7F; multiple coronary curves [WEBPROD_CARDIO p.5; OTVAS p.1].
  - PendraCare Convey (6-8F) and Primum hydrophilic guiding catheters; US 510(k)s K120585, K132197 and K250972 [FDA510K_PEN p.1].
  - Guiding catheters were {{CB-2499}} of PendraCare's CY24 core business [DKPEN p.12].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** A wide-lumen, pre-shaped catheter that sits in the opening of a coronary artery.
  - It gives a supported channel through which guidewires, balloons and stents are delivered during PCI.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per PCI, occasionally more if the curve must be changed.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** As for stents. PendraCare also sells to OEMs (see A4.10).

### Diagnostic (angiographic) catheters
- **Polymed range**:
  - Concath: 5F and 6F; Judkins, Amplatz, IM, MPA, Radialis, TIG and pigtail curves [WEBPROD_CARDIO p.6; OTCON p.1].
  - Diagnostic catheters as components [CATCOMP24 p.5].
  - PendraCare diagnostic catheters ({{CB-2500}} of its CY24 core business) [DKPEN pp.12-13].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Pre-shaped catheters used to inject X-ray contrast into the coronary arteries or heart chambers for diagnostic angiography.
  - Different curves engage the left or right coronary artery.
  - A pigtail catheter is used for the left ventricle and the aorta.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** Typically one to three per diagnostic angiogram, fewer with universal radial curves.
  - Diagnostic angiograms outnumber PCIs.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** As for stents.

### Guidewires (angiographic and access)
- **Polymed range**:
  - Filoflex: PTFE-coated.
  - Aquareach: hydrophilic.
  - Both are listed in 0.032-0.038 inch sizes on the ordering tables [WEBPROD_CARDIO pp.7-8; OTFIL p.1 and OTAQU p.1, visual reads].
  - As components [CATCOMP24 pp.9-12]: nitinol, stainless-steel, PTFE and hydrophilic guidewires; a nasogastric-tube guidewire; a guidewire advancer.
  - No 0.014-inch coronary guidewire is listed [WEB_CARDIOLOGY p.1], although the Excedo SC balloon ordering table specifies a 0.014-inch wire [OTEXSC p.1].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Thin wires over which catheters are advanced through vessels.
  - PTFE coating lowers friction. Hydrophilic coatings become very slippery when wet, for crossing tortuous vessels.
  - Wires in the 0.035-inch class are used to place sheaths and angiographic catheters.
  - PCI itself runs balloons and stents over separate, finer coronary wires.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One or more per procedure. Guidewires are also in every CVC, PICC, midline and dialysis-catheter kit (A4.2, A4.3).
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Cath labs; device makers buying components.

### PCI accessories: inflation devices, Y-connectors and manifolds
- **Polymed range** [WEBPROD_CARDIO pp.9-11; OTTER p.1; OTZIN p.1; OTNEO p.1]:
  - Teravox inflation device: plunger and gun types.
  - Zinera PTCA Y-connector haemostatic-valve kit: push-click and push-pull.
  - Neofold Pro manifold: 2- or 3-port; pressure rating {{D-0873}}.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].**
  - An inflation device is a large locking syringe with a pressure gauge, used to inflate balloons and stents to a precise pressure.
  - A Y-connector haemostatic valve attaches to the guiding catheter, letting wires and balloons pass while limiting blood back-flow.
  - A manifold switches between contrast injection, saline flush and pressure monitoring.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One of each per procedure, often sold as a "PTCA kit".
  - These are low-price accessories that hospitals may buy bundled with balloons and stents.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Cath labs.

### Arterial access needles and catheter introducers
- **Polymed range**:
  - Sylera introducer needle [WEBPROD_CARDIO p.12].
  - PendraCare SimplGuide (product code DYB, catheter introducer; K261242) [FDA510K_PEN p.1].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].**
  - Needles puncture the radial or femoral artery.
  - Introducer sheaths let catheters be exchanged during a procedure without re-puncturing.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One per procedure.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Cath labs.

### ECG electrodes
- **Polymed range** [CATDOM25 p.34; CATEXP24 pp.87-88; WEB_CARDIOLOGY p.1]:
  - Electrocardiograph electrodes: adult and paediatric; tear-drop design; latex- and PVC-free; liquid-gel option.
  - Listed under "Others" in the catalogues and on the cardiology web page.
  - ECG electrodes appear among traded goods and were named as a driver of FY24 domestic growth [PD24 p.107].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Disposable adhesive gel pads stuck to the skin and clipped to leads, for recording an ECG and for continuous heart monitoring.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** Several per recording or monitoring set-up.
  - During continuous monitoring they are replaced as they lose adhesion.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** ECG technicians, ICUs, operating theatres, emergency departments, cath labs and diagnostic centres.

## A4.9 Orthopaedics (Citieffe, Italy)

The CA digest records the acquisition as closed on 6-Nov-2025 [TR2Q26 p.3]. Orthopaedics is absent from Polymed's website, catalogues and placement documents [WEB_HOME p.1]. The product types below come from Citieffe's US 510(k)s [FDA510K_CIT p.1] and the acquisition deck [DKCIT pp.12,15]. Citieffe describes itself as a maker of "Trauma and Extremity fixation systems" [DKCIT p.12].

### Intramedullary nails
- **Citieffe range**:
  - Estremo nails: tibial and femoral; Estremo fibular nail K250197.
  - EBA2 and EBA One femoral nailing systems.
  - DT Humerus.
  - Titanium nailing system.
  - US 510(k)s K113387, K131005, K181540 and K183666 [FDA510K_CIT p.1; DKCIT p.12].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Metal rods inserted along the hollow centre of a long bone (femur, tibia, humerus, fibula) to hold a fracture in line while it heals.
  - They are locked with screws at each end.
  - This is the standard treatment for many shaft fractures.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One nail plus locking screws per fractured bone; the implant is usually left in place.
- **Users and buyers [general description].**
  - Users: orthopaedic trauma surgeons.
  - Buyers: hospitals.
  - Implant sets often reach hospitals on consignment with instrument trays, at prices far above consumables.

### External fixators and pins
- **Citieffe range** [FDA510K_CIT p.1; DKCIT p.12]:
  - Dolphix external fixation system, including an MR-conditional version.
  - ST.A.R. 90 F4 external fixator and F4 screws with hydroxyapatite.
  - F4 elbow and knee.
  - Rekrea and Xframe.
  - US 510(k)s K113384, K132363, K150661 and K163323.
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Frames outside the limb, attached to bone by pins or screws through the skin.
  - They are used for open or complex fractures, temporary "damage-control" stabilization, and limb reconstruction or lengthening.
  - A hydroxyapatite coating improves the grip of pins in bone.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** One frame per treated limb.
  - Frame parts are re-used in some markets; pins and screws are single use.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Trauma and limb-reconstruction surgeons; hospitals.

### Bone screws and plates
- **Citieffe range** [FDA510K_CIT p.1; DKCIT p.12]:
  - Titanium cannulated screws (K113388).
  - Rondo screws.
  - PL8 wrist reconstruction plate (K180150).
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].**
  - Cannulated screws are inserted over a guide wire to fix fractures such as the femoral neck or the scaphoid.
  - Plates screwed onto bone hold fractures such as those of the distal radius (wrist).
- **Unit of demand [general description].** Per fracture; several screws per plate.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Orthopaedic surgeons; hospitals.

### Navigation system for nailing
- **Citieffe range**:
  - Navigation system, described as "Unique navigation software for nailing systems" [DKCIT pp.12,15].
- **What it is and clinical use [general description].** Equipment that guides the placement of distal locking screws through the holes of an intramedullary nail, reducing X-ray exposure and operating time.
- **Unit of demand [general description].** A capital or instrument item placed with hospitals to support nail sales.
- **Users and buyers [general description].** Hospitals using Citieffe nails.

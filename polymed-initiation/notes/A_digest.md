## A1 Revenue architecture
- Consolidated FY26 revenue from operations Rs 1,87,525.92 lakh (+12.3%), FY25 Rs 1,66,983.16 lakh; total income Rs 1,99,534.14 lakh [AR26 p.98; AR26 p.8]
- Management therapy split (consolidated, MD&A): infusion therapy Rs 99,728.0 lakh (FY25 1,01,237.7, -1.5%, "broadly stable"); renal care Rs 18,762.8 lakh (+24.5%); "Others" (cardiology, critical care, blood management, surgery etc., incl. acquisitions) Rs 69,041.4 lakh (+36.3%) [AR26 p.47]
- Standalone split by product family (BRSR, % of standalone turnover, FY26 vs FY25): infusion therapy 69.87% vs 67.14%; dialysis 11.23% vs 9.49%; blood management & collection 9.54% vs 9.96%; surgery & wound drainage 5.35% vs 5.67%; urology 2.99% vs 3.29%; anaesthesia & respiratory not listed in FY26 (2.88% in FY25); other 1.02% vs 1.57% [BRSR26 p.12; BRSR25 p.5]
- Prefilled syringes = 10.18% of standalone turnover (BRSR LCA table) [BRSR26 p.27]
- Geography (MD&A, consolidated): India Rs 58,173.1 lakh (+19.6%), Europe Rs 59,697.6 lakh (+7.1%), RoW Rs 68,321.1 lakh (+11.4%), export incentive Rs 1,334.2 lakh; international total Rs 1,28,018.6 lakh (+9.3%) [AR26 p.47]
- Ind AS 115 (consolidated): domestic Rs 57,929.65 lakh; exports from India Rs 1,06,739.20 lakh (FY25 1,10,286.10); sales of foreign subsidiaries Rs 21,279.44 lakh (FY25 6,803.19) [AR26 p.113]
- Export share of standalone turnover 65.01% in FY26 vs 69.51% in FY25 (BRSR); AR25 MD&A called it 70% / "nearly 69%" [BRSR26 p.13; BRSR25 p.6; AR25 p.60; AR25 p.66]
- Ind AS 108: single segment (medical devices); no customer ≥10% of revenue, standalone or consolidated [AR26 p.91; AR26 p.92; AR26 p.130; AR26 p.131]
- Self-description in MD&A: "operates predominantly in the consumables segment – IV catheters, infusion sets, dialysis products, and related disposables" [AR26 p.43]. Portfolio 225+ products across 13 specialities (infusion, vascular access, dialysis & renal, critical care, cardiology, oncology, transfusion, diagnostics, gastro, anaesthesia & respiratory, urology, surgery & wound management, orthopaedics) [AR26 p.3]; 200+ in FY25 [AR25 p.4]
- Entry timeline: 1997 infusion & vascular access; 2000 transfusion; 2018 renal; 2019 critical care; 2024 cardiology (DES launched in India 2025); 2025 orthopaedics (Citieffe) [AR26 p.11]

## A2 How Polymed makes money
### Unit of demand and channel
- International sales go entirely through distributors ("all international sales managed via distributor partnerships"), many with local or regional exclusivity; India sales team covers private and government hospitals and runs CME programmes; 8,000+ hospitals and nursing homes served in India [AR25 p.61]
- 1,000+ distributors (900+ in FY25), 590+ direct-sales reps in India, US, Italy and Mexico (510+ in FY25), 60+ clinical experts, 4,000+ customer engagement programmes a year [AR26 p.3; AR25 p.65; SR25 p.8]
- Customers per BRSR: distributors abroad (100+ countries); in India private and government hospitals, laboratories, blood banks, dialysis centres, nursing homes [BRSR26 p.13; BRSR25 p.6]
- India clinical engagement: 4,000+ CNE programmes, 50+ hospitals, 80,000+ healthcare professionals reached; PACE academy [AR26 p.19]
- UCMPMD FY25 (India marketing code return): domestic sales Rs 486.36 cr; free samples between Rs 4,20,565 and Rs 14,87,876 a month (September printed ambiguously as '182,3,212'); 115–229 CNE/CME events a month costing Rs 0.58–8.39 lakh a month; third-party events include World Congress of Nephrology (Rs 8.0 lakh), NephroPlus (Rs 7.5 lakh), Infusion Nurses Society (Rs 7.49 lakh), a CDSCO event (Rs 6.3 lakh). No annual totals printed [UCMPMD25 p.1; UCMPMD25 p.2; UCMPMD25 p.3; UCMPMD25 p.4]
- Order book (unsatisfied performance obligations) Rs 13,605.96 lakh consolidated, Rs 10,577.34 lakh standalone at 31-Mar-2026 [AR26 p.114; AR26 p.77]

### Pricing and FX
- Finished-goods prices "generally reviewed every year"; drastic raw-material increases passed on "after due discussions with the customers" [AR26 p.85]
- Rebates and discounts (standalone) rose to Rs 1,158.43 lakh from Rs 731.42 lakh (FY24 526.46) [AR26 p.77; AR25 p.119]
- MD&A: renal care and other segments faced "pricing pressure from low-cost imports, including dumping from China", met with "calibrated price increases" [AR26 p.47]
- FX: exposure mostly USD/EUR/GBP/JPY; natural hedge via imports; **no forward contracts outstanding at 31-Mar-2026** (FY25: EUR sell and USD buy forwards); net unhedged USD receivable Rs 23,586.86 lakh and EUR Rs 5,518.56 lakh; JPY net payable (machinery/mould imports implied) [AR26 p.86]. FX gains of Rs 3,812.97 lakh (standalone) sit in other income, below operating EBITDA [AR26 p.78]
- Directors' Report: "it may not always be possible to fully pass on the impact of adverse currency movements" [AR26 p.36]

## A3 FY26 slowdown and guidance record
- Stated reasons (Chairman): "geopolitical uncertainties, evolving trade dynamics, heightened competitive pressures, and the ongoing conflict in West Asia, which impacted supply chains and increased raw material costs" [AR26 p.7]
- MD&A reasons for margin fall: part-year consolidation of lower-margin PendraCare and Citieffe, one-time acquisition expenses of Rs 970.0 lakh, and a Labour Codes provision of Rs 680.4 lakh (exceptional) [AR26 p.48]
- Acquisitions' contribution: Citieffe revenue Rs 7,257.13 lakh with a PBT loss of Rs 9.79 lakh; PendraCare revenue Rs 4,415.14 lakh with a PBT loss of Rs 1,409.67 lakh (both unaudited, management-certified) [AR26 p.121; AR26 p.122]
- **Organic export weakness**: standalone export sales fell to Rs 1,06,739.20 lakh from Rs 1,10,286.10 lakh; standalone Europe exports fell to Rs 49,272.82 lakh from Rs 53,831.54 lakh; Americas slightly down (28,247.16 vs 28,704.37); Asia up (23,352.56 vs 20,154.87). Standalone domestic sales rose to Rs 57,929.65 lakh from Rs 48,412.99 lakh; the consolidated "Europe +7.1%" in the MD&A therefore includes acquisitions [AR26 p.92; AR26 p.77; AR26 p.47]
- Standalone revenue by quarter (Rs lakh): 1QFY26 38,404.67; 2QFY26 41,654.17; 3QFY26 41,886.15; 4QFY26 44,301.49; 1QFY27 43,109.95. Consolidated: 40,321.07; 44,388.16; 49,365.55; 53,451.14; 52,537.55 [RS1Q26 p.3; RS2Q26 p.2; RS3Q26 p.3; RS4Q26 p.4; RS1Q27 p.3]
- Other income was a large profit prop: standalone Rs 11,991.77 lakh, incl. MF gains of Rs 4,024.30 lakh realised and Rs 1,258.96 lakh unrealised, and FX gain of Rs 3,812.97 lakh [AR26 p.78]. 1QFY27 standalone other income Rs 2,864.96 lakh [RS1Q27 p.3]
- Guidance record in these documents: standalone EBITDA margin guidance "25%–27%" (FY26 delivered 26.8%) [AR26 p.48]; Vision 2030 = "2.0x Revenue, industry-leading margins, globalization"; double R&D spend in 3-4 years; 2 new plants; 25-30 launches a year; 100+ sales associates a year [AR26 p.9; AR26 p.10]. ## B1 Procurement & pricing economics
- Main raw materials are plastic granules (PP, LDPE, HDPE, PC, PA, SAN, ABS, K-resin), crude-linked; "The majority of Raw materials are being imported by the Company and the few are procured indigenously" [AR26 p.85]. AR25 also cites PVC compound [AR25 p.62]
- Inputs sourced directly from MSMEs fell to 18.47% of input value (26.35% FY25); from within the district/neighbouring districts 37.88% (41.69%) [BRSR26 p.52]
- Raw material inventory was deliberately built up in FY26 to absorb West Asia supply shocks [AR26 p.48]
- Import share of India consumables demand fell from 80-85% (2022) to 50-55% (2025), per BCG-KIHT as cited in the MD&A [AR26 p.43]

## B2 Competitors
- AR25: key Indian players are mainly MNCs, plus fragmented local players in disposables; some competitors are larger and better funded [AR25 p.67]. AR26: low-cost Chinese imports and dumping, especially in renal [AR26 p.47; AR26 p.49]

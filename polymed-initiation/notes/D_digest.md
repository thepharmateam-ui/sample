## A1 Company overview

- Polymed describes itself as "among the top five companies in the medical devices industry in India" by operating income, and fifth by PAT in FY23. The source is the CRISIL report it commissioned (D-0001, D-0002) [PD24 p.167]. PD21 made the same top-five claim for FY19 [PD21 p.124].
- In PD24 the portfolio was over 123 categories and 6,745 SKUs at 30-Jun-2024 (D-0003, D-0004) [PD24 p.167]. PD21 gave "over 130 SKUs" at 31-Dec-2020 (D-0538) [PD21 p.124]. The two documents probably count on different bases, so the two figures are not comparable.
- PD24 lists these verticals: infusion therapy, oncology, anaesthesia & respiratory care, urology, gastroenterology, vascular access, surgery & wound drainage, dialysis & renal care, diagnostics, transfusion system, veterinary, cardiology (entered FY23) and critical care (division launched FY23) [PD24 p.167]. The document calls these "11 product verticals" on p.168 and "13 product verticals" on p.173 (D-0023, D-0024) [PD24 p.168; PD24 p.173].
- In the PD24 product table, "Others" includes insulin syringes, umbilical cord clamps, sputum collectors and dry brushes [PD24 p.174]. The veterinary range was five SKUs: large-bore infusion sets with coiled tubing, stomach tubes, endoscope flushing catheters and vet IV infusion sets (D-0073) [PD24 p.174].
- The strengths PD24 claims are: 1) one of the leading Indian disposable-device makers with a diversified portfolio; 2) R&D capability; 3) global manufacturing with automation; 4) wide distribution reach; 5) an experienced management team [PD24 pp.168-170].
- The strategies PD24 lists are: 1) move to a "solution provider" model, starting with infection-control and fluid-management products, ICU/OT audits and onboarding clinicians; 2) expand manufacturing and the portfolio, including oncology, nephrology, cardiology (structural heart and complex procedures) and critical care; 3) R&D using AI/IoT and biodegradable materials; 4) gain share in India (Tier 2/3 cities) and abroad (US branch office, UK representative office); 5) acquisitions [PD24 pp.170-172].
- Awards PD24 cites: India Medical Device Leader of the Year 2022 (Department of Pharmaceuticals), PLEXCONCIL top exporter of plastic medical disposables for 2021-22 and 2022-23, ET Edge Best Healthcare Brands 2024, Fortune Next 500 (2023) and CII Top 50 Innovative (2022) [PD24 p.181]. The domestic catalogue says Polymed has been the "largest exporter of consumable medical devices from India for 10 years" (D-0849) [CATDOM25 p.3].
- Catalogue claims:
  - CATDOM25 (PML-Domestic 03/2025): 125+ countries; 330+ patents; over 200 devices; more than 1.8 billion devices made a year in 12 plants in 4 countries; 350+ moulding machines; 200+ automatic assembly machines; about 5,000 employees, including 530+ sales associates and 50+ clinical specialists (D-0840 to D-0848) [CATDOM25 p.3].
  - CATCOMP24 (Nov-2024): 12 specialities; 325+ patents granted and 40+ pending; 400+ sales associates; 200+ devices; capacity of 1.5 billion+ devices a year; 4,000+ employees (D-0850 to D-0855) [CATCOMP24 p.3].
- Corporate history: incorporated in Delhi on 30-Mar-1995; listed on BSE on 3-Jun-1996 and on NSE on 7-Dec-2011 [PD24 p.194].
- Group structure in PD24:
  - Wholly-owned subsidiaries: Poly Medicure (Laiyang) Co. Ltd (China, incorporated 25-Jun-2007), Plan1 Health India Pvt Ltd (25-Feb-2020) and Poly Medicure B.V. (Netherlands, 15-Mar-2018).
  - Step-down subsidiaries: Plan1 Health s.r.l. (Italy, incorporated 1990) and PolyHealth Medical Inc. (Delaware, 19-Jul-2021).
  - Associate: Ultra for Medical Products (Egypt, 23%).
  - No joint venture and no holding company [PD24 pp.194-195].
  - PD21 had three wholly-owned subsidiaries, one step-down subsidiary and the same associate [PD21 p.146].

## A2 Channel, customers, concentration, pricing, raw materials

- **Customer concentration (PD24 risk factor 14).** Top-10 customers were 27.60% of revenue from operations in FY22, 30.67% in FY23, 28.99% in FY24 and 31.51% in 1QFY25 (D-0222 to D-0225). The largest customer was 4.92%, 6.65%, 5.66% and 7.33% over the same periods (D-0230 to D-0233). Rupee amounts are D-0218 to D-0221 and D-0226 to D-0229 [PD24 p.50].
  - PD21 disclosed top-5 customers at 18.22% (9MFY20) and 17.15% (9MFY21), and top-10 at 32.55% and 29.10% (D-0768 to D-0771) [PD21 p.47].
  - The FY24 financial statements state that no customer individually accounts for 10% or more of sales [PD24 p.323].
  - Polymed has no long-term agreements with customers, and customers "often undertake vendor rationalisation" [PD24 p.50].
  - Customers are not named. Given the all-distributor export model, the top customers are presumably distributors, but PD24 does not say so [PD24 p.46].
- **Distributors.** All sales outside India go through distributors [PD24 p.170].
  - Overseas distributors: 215 (FY22), 230 (FY23), 240 (FY24) and 260 (1QFY25) (D-0035, D-0034, D-0033, D-0007) [PD24 p.170]. Risk factor 8 gives 240 for 1QFY25 instead (D-0204, conflict) [PD24 p.46].
  - India distributors: 425 (FY22), 463 (FY23) and 506 (FY24 and 30-Jun-2024) (D-0201 to D-0203) [PD24 p.46].
  - Many distributors have local or regional exclusivity "subject to certain conditions". Agreements typically run 2 years (D-0200). Polymed limits dependence on any one distributor by periodically redefining territories and product scope [PD24 p.46].
  - Distributors order on purchase orders and there is no significant order backlog. Demand is forecast from rolling distributor projections and hospital procurement plans. Occasional "unpredictably large tender sales orders" occur [PD24 p.52].
  - PD21 gave "over 260" India distributors on p.124 and "over 200 third-party distributors" on p.135 (D-0539, D-0750, internal conflict) [PD21 pp.124,135].
- **Sales force and hospital reach.** The sales division had 440 employees at 30-Jun-2024, promoting products in 8,000 private and government hospitals and nursing homes and running CME programmes (D-0036, D-0006) [PD24 p.170]. This was up from 225 employees and over 5,000 hospitals at 31-Dec-2020 (D-0565, D-0540) [PD21 p.127]. The domestic catalogue claims 530+ sales associates and 50+ clinical specialists (D-0847, D-0848) [CATDOM25 p.3].
- **Pricing.**
  - Raw-material cost changes "are generally passed through" to customers, but only at periodic intervals, so there is a lag [PD24 p.88].
  - FX changes are also passed through only periodically [PD24 p.51].
  - Declining ASPs over a product's life have been "partially offset" by volume and cost reductions [PD24 p.47].
  - DPCO: all notified medical devices outside Annexure A of the Eighth Schedule of MDR 2017 are "non-scheduled formulations", and their prices cannot rise more than 10% in any 12-month period (D-0185) [PD24 p.41].
  - Government contracts can blacklist a product, or Polymed itself, after repeated quality defaults [PD24 p.42].
  - NPPA alleged that Polymed overcharged 20% above MRP on its spinal needle in 2014, with a demand of Rs 9.34 mn, of which Rs 1.83 mn was paid under protest (D-0794 to D-0796) [PD21 p.205]. The NPPA demand appears as a contingent liability of Rs 66.88 lakh at 31-Mar-2024 [PD24 p.58].
- **Marketing spend** was 1.27% of revenue in FY22, 1.84% in FY23, 1.97% in FY24 and 1.75% in 1QFY25 (D-0234 to D-0237) [PD24 p.50]. Rebates and discounts were Rs 526.46 lakh in FY24 and Rs 454.31 lakh in FY23 (D-0299, D-0300) [PD24 p.295].
- **Raw materials.**
  - Types: plastic granules, PVC compounds, fibre and stainless-steel tubes [PD24 p.179]. PD21 also lists PVC sheets, boxes, medical paper, film and FEP compound [PD21 pp.44-45].
  - Buying: spot purchase orders with no long-term contracts, although Polymed says it has long relationships with its major suppliers. For some materials "there may be no alternate vendors" [PD24 pp.48,180].
  - Imported share of raw-material requirement: 66.35% in FY22, 73.97% in FY23, 71.75% in FY24 and 73.97% in 1QFY25 (D-0242 to D-0245) [PD24 p.88]. PD21 gave 62.67%, 61.04% and 75.44% for FY18-FY20 and 62.79% for 9MFY21 (D-0789 to D-0792) [PD21 p.77].
  - Materials are bought from the US, Germany, China, Denmark, Portugal, Japan, Korea and Taiwan [PD24 p.51].
  - Raw-material cost was 33.78% of total revenue in FY24, down from 38.05% in FY23 and 38.17% in FY22 (D-0172 to D-0175) [PD24 p.88]. It was 42.53% of total expenses in FY24 (D-0210) [PD24 p.47].
  - Supplier names and top-supplier concentration are not disclosed (see gaps).
- **Receivables.** Trade receivables were Rs 26,993.90 lakh at 31-Mar-2024. Receivable days were 80 (FY22), 76 (FY23) and 73 (FY24) (D-0248 to D-0254) [PD24 p.54]. At 31-Mar-2024 receivables by region were India Rs 7,050.35 lakh, Europe Rs 8,398.00 lakh, USA Rs 376.56 lakh and Others Rs 11,168.97 lakh (D-0291 to D-0294) [PD24 p.312].
- **FX.**
  - Unhedged FX receivables were Rs 14,015.00 lakh against forwards of Rs 1,943 lakh at 31-Mar-2024 (D-0238, D-0239) [PD24 p.51].
  - At 30-Jun-2024, total unhedged exposure was Rs 15,791.85 lakh against forwards of Rs 6,390.49 lakh (D-0240, D-0241) [PD24 p.88].
  - Borrowings are "primarily U.S. Dollar denominated" [PD24 p.51].
  - Imports provide a natural hedge "to a limited extent" [PD24 p.51].
- **Freight** was 0.87% of revenue in FY24 and 1.06% in 1QFY25 (D-0256, D-0255) [PD24 p.55].

- **B1 Imports and exports.**
  - Import dependence is quoted in three ways in the same chapter: about 70% of demand [PD24 p.128], about 80% of the market [PD24 p.134] and 70-75% of demand [PD24 p.139].
  - Consumables are 30-40% imported [PD24 pp.134,139]. Import dependence is lower for bandages, needles and syringes and higher for catheters and cannulas [PD24 p.139].
  - Imports grew from Rs 399 bn in FY19 to Rs 650 bn in FY24, and exports from Rs 143 bn to Rs 280 bn (14% CAGR). Exports are 50-55% of production (D-0445 to D-0450) [PD24 pp.139-140].
  - Imports come from Europe 33%, China 16% and the US 15%. Exports go to Europe 22% and the US 16% [PD24 pp.128,140].

The CAGR is quoted as 5.5-7.5% in the text but 5.5-6.5% in the heading (D-0473 to D-0479) [PD24 p.153]. North America had a 49% share in 2023 and Europe plus the Middle East 22% [PD24 p.154]. PD21 put the global market at $459 bn in FY19 and projected $575-585 bn in 2024 [PD21 pp.119-120].
- **B2 Demand drivers.**
  - India's current health expenditure was 3.3% of GDP in CY21 [PD24 p.121], with per-capita spend of $74 (PPP) and out-of-pocket spending near 50% [PD24 p.122].
  - Non-communicable diseases caused 66% of deaths in 2019 [PD24 p.125].
  - About 550 mn people had health insurance in FY23 [PD24 p.142].
  - There were 146 mn people aged 60+ in 2021, expected to reach 171 mn by 2026 [PD24 p.142].
  - The government targets public health spend of 2.5% of GDP [PD24 p.142].
  - Medical tourism grew at a 52% CAGR over 2020-23 [PD24 p.142].
- **B3 Consumables specifics.** Syringes and needles are "the majority" of consumables sales by frequency of use [PD24 p.126].
  - Polymed's market share is 1.9% of the Indian consumables market in FY23, on domestic revenue of Rs 3,440 mn. This is the only share figure in either document (D-0535, D-0536) [PD24 p.166].
  - PD21 said MNCs hold 30-40% of consumables and that "Hindustan Syringes and Poly Medicure hold a higher share of the consumables market than MNCs" [PD21 pp.109,112].
  - Neither document sizes the markets for IV cannula, infusion sets, dialysis, blood bags or CVCs (see gaps).
- **B4 Industry structure and competition.**
  - India has more than 800 domestic manufacturers [PD24 p.127]. Of the players, 56-58% had turnover below Rs 100 mn in 2024 and 85-87% below Rs 500 mn [PD24 p.129]. About 65% of manufacturers make consumables [PD24 p.128].
  - Clusters: Haryana and Delhi make low-end consumables (Polymed, Hindustan Syringes, BD, BL Lifesciences, Hollister) [PD24 p.133].
  - CRISIL's FY23 peer table (operating income, Rs mn): Philips India 57,340; Siemens Healthcare 43,251; BD India 13,014; Transasia 13,028 (FY22); Poly Medicure 11,185; Hindustan Syringes 8,453; Sahajanand 7,959; Allengers 7,556; Healthium 7,085; B. Braun India 6,106 (D-0489 onwards) [PD24 pp.164-165].
  - Polymed's operating margin is given as 26.0% in the ratio table but 25.8% in the key observations (conflict), and CRISIL ranks it third among peers on margin. PD24 lists Polymed's FY24 figures as operating income Rs 13,758 mn and OPBDIT Rs 3,582 mn [PD24 pp.165-166].
  - MNC lists of competitors appear at [PD24 pp.130,181; PD21 p.136].
- **B5 Regulation and policy.**
  - Medical Devices Rules 2017 took effect on 1-Jan-2018 [PD21 p.119]. The Feb-2020 amendment brought all devices under the MDR and under DPCO's 10% annual price-increase cap for non-scheduled devices [PD24 p.41; PD21 p.51]. The sector was separated from pharma in 2014 with 100% FDI under the automatic route [PD24 p.128].
  - The PLI scheme for medical devices was announced in Mar-2020; Polymed was approved for renal devices [PD24 p.87].
  - The medical-device parks scheme has an outlay of Rs 400 cr over FY21-FY25 and grants of up to Rs 1,000 mn per park (HP, TN, MP, UP) [PD24 p.127].
  - The National Medical Devices Policy was issued in 2023 [PD24 pp.41,127].
  - GST ranges from 5% to 18% and customs duty from 0% to 7.5%. An inverted duty structure is flagged [PD24 p.152]. In 2021 there was 7.5% customs duty plus a 5% health cess on select devices [PD21 p.119].
  - Stents and knee implants were price-capped in 2017, cutting prices by 60-80% [PD24 p.152]. The AIMED, QCI and NABCB voluntary certification scheme is described at [PD24 p.151].
  - In the EU, MDR applies from May 2021, with a transition for legacy MDD certificates described in PD21 [PD24 p.42; PD21 p.38].
  - Environmental rules: the Plastic Waste Management Rules ban PVC banners under 100 micron [PD24 p.54]. PD21 warned of a possible mandatory switch away from PVC, its "primary raw material" [PD21 p.49].

## C Risk factors (PD24, 23-Aug-2024) – business-specific list with numbers

1. **Extensive and changing regulation.** Covers MDR 2017, the D&C Act and environmental laws; the PLI incentive (5% of incremental sales, FY23-27); the National Medical Devices Policy 2023; and the DPCO 10% annual price cap. Cites the pending Raigad drug-inspector case [PD24 pp.40-41].
2. **Export approvals.** Exports are 69.63% of revenue. The US needs 510(k)s, of which only two product categories are cleared (safety IV cannula, IV set). The EU needs MDR and CE marks, and re-certification is due every 3-5 years [PD24 pp.41-42].

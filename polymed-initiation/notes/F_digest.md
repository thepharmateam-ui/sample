### B3.2 PLI and other schemes: where Polymed stands
- Polymed was approved under PLI **Target Segment 3, Category A** for seven renal products: dialyzer, dialysis machine, PD kits, fistula, blood line, haemodialysis catheter and transducer protector. The site is in Faridabad, Haryana [PLIMD_APPR23 p.2], [PLIMD_APPR24 p.3].
  - The Lok Sabha reply of 24-Jul-2026 shows the project "Commissioned", with committed investment of Rs 72.00 crore and actual investment of Rs 55.61 crore [LSQ_AU1001_260724 p.7].
- **Polymed has received no PLI incentive to date.** Incentives up to FY25 totalled Rs 266.64 crore (FY23 36.85, FY24 98.70, FY25 131.09). They went only to Wipro GE, Siemens, Philips, Nipro, Meril Life, Meril Healthcare and Panacea [LSQ_AU1001_260724 p.2], [LSQ_AU1001_260724 p.3].
  - Nipro, the other domestic dialyzer maker, received Rs 34.89 crore [LSQ_AU1001_260724 p.3].
- Polymed is also a beneficiary under **SMDI / MIS-RID**. The project covers "polymer tubing components for infusion therapy, bloodline and dialysis accessories, central venous access/catheters", with a project cost of Rs 100.00 crore and a grant of Rs 10.00 crore [LSQ_AU1001_260724 p.9].
- Scheme progress at the national level:

  - They reached Rs 13,624.52 crore by Dec-2025, with exports of Rs 6,425.48 crore [PIB_MDM_26 p.1].
  - Commissioned projects rose from 19 (44 products) to 22 (55) to 24 (57) [PIB_PLI_2412 p.1], [PIB_PLI_2512 p.1], [PIB_MDM_26 p.1].
- **Conflicting official figures** (all recorded in the ledger):

### B3.3 Regulation: MDR 2017 and the draft devices bill
- G.S.R. 102(E) says the registration exemption ends 30 months (A/B) or 42 months (C/D) "from the date of this notification" (11-Feb-2020). Read literally, that gives Aug-2022 and Aug-2023. The regime was actually implemented from 1-Oct-2022 for A/B [GSR102E_2020 p.7], [PIB_MDRAB_2209 p.1]. This conflict is recorded in the ledger.
- The Annexure of devices already notified before the 2020 amendment covers most of Polymed's portfolio: syringes, needles, perfusion sets, catheters, IV cannulae, scalp-vein sets, blood bags, orthopaedic implants and, from 1-Apr-2021, dialysis machines [GSR102E_2020 p.7], [GSR102E_2020 p.8].
- Draft G.S.R. 515(E) of 23-Jun-2026 makes a notified-body QMS audit a precondition for a licence (new Rule 20(5A)) and shortens licensing clocks (table above) [GSR515E_2026 p.1].
- The draft Drugs, Medical Devices and Cosmetics Bill 2026 would rename the DCGI as "Drugs and Medical Devices Controller General". It would add a Medical Devices Technical Advisory Board and extend criminal penalties for substandard or misbranded products to devices [MB_DMDCB_2026 p.1] [press].
  - An AiMeD-led coalition wrote to the Health Minister on 11-Aug-2026 asking for a standalone Medical Devices Act and an autonomous regulator, and objecting to jail terms of 1–7 years [ANI_DMDCB_2608 p.1] [press].
- On reuse of single-use devices, CDSCO says it received no complaints in five years. Rule 44 labelling applies [LSQ_AU1086_260724 p.2].

### B3.4 Pricing: DPCO, trade margins, the 20% recommendation and the Maharashtra survey
- Today, non-scheduled devices face only Para 20: at most a 10% MRP increase in 12 months [PIB_MDDRUG_2004 p.1], [PIB_NPPA_2408 p.1].
- Devices have been priced three ways so far:
  - Ceiling prices on stents (Feb-2017) and knee implants (Aug-2017) [PIB_TMR_2107 p.1]. Knee caps are estimated to save about ₹1,500 crore a year [PIB_PLI_2512 p.1].
  - TMR on six devices in 2021, saving about ₹1,000 crore a year [PIB_PLI_2512 p.1].
- **PSC 176th report (7-Aug-2026).** It "recommends that the gap should not be more than 20% between landing price and MRP for medical devices and drugs". It cites Tenecteplase (landing cost Rs 18,000, MRP Rs 50,000) and the 2017 stent cap (Rs 25,000–30,000, down from about Rs 1,90,000) [PIB_PSC176_2608 p.1].
  - Trade press notes NPPA has no blanket power to cap margins. A cap would need a DPCO amendment or TMR category by category. 82% of the pharma market by value is non-scheduled [MB_20PCT_2608 p.1] [press].
- **Maharashtra FDA survey (15/16-Sep-2026).** It covered IV sets, syringes and needles, nebulisers and oxygen masks, and miscellaneous consumables [FPJ_MAHFDA_2609 p.1] [press]:
  - IV set: Rs 11.05 against MRP Rs 325 (2,841.17%).
  - **Polymed IV set: Rs 10.50 against MRP Rs 187 (1,680.95%)**. Lyvofusion IV set: Rs 11.50 against Rs 252 (2,091%).
  - 10-ml syringe: Rs 6.75 against Rs 57.20 (747.4%).
  - Catheter: Rs 29.41 against Rs 310 (955%).
  - Nebuliser: Rs 40 against Rs 715 (1,687.5%).
  - The FDA asked for TMR or margin caps, guidelines on permissible price differentials, and structured price monitoring or DPCO inclusion.
  - FDA commissioners of Punjab, Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu sent similar inputs [MB_MRP_2609 p.1] [press].
- **Health Minister's direction (18-Sep-2026) and after.** DoP met at least six private hospital chains and FICCI on 17-Sep. J.P. Nadda directed DoP/NPPA to hold consultations and review the 2004, 2008 and 2019 TMR exercises. "No public deadline has been set" [MB_MRP_2609 p.1] [press], [ANI_MRP_260919 p.1] [press], [TRIB_MRP_2609 p.1] [press].
  - Date conflict: Medical Buyer says the directive was issued at a review meeting "on September 18". The Tribune (19-Sep) says Nadda "on Saturday" (19-Sep) directed DoP/NPPA [MB_MRP_2609 p.1] [press], [TRIB_MRP_2609 p.1] [press].
  - AHPI (about 30,000 hospitals) argues that MRPs are fixed upstream by manufacturers and distributors. AiMeD and MTaI back TMR [MB_MRP_2609 p.1] [press], [MB_20PCT_2608 p.1] [press].
  - Nothing official was found after 21-Sep-2026 (see gaps).

### B3.6 Trade remedy: DGTR anti-dumping case on dialyzers (applicant: Poly Medicure)
- Case F.No. 6/19/2026-DGTR, Case No. AD (OI) (17/2026), SETU AD/OI/019/2026. Initiated 25-Jun-2026 [DGTR_DIAL_INIT p.1], [DGTR_DIAL_PCN p.1].
- Scope: disposable haemodialysis dialyzers from China PR and Malaysia, tariff line 90189031. Fresenius brands F6/F7 HPS, FX5/8/10/60/80 and HF60/80 are excluded [DGTR_DIAL_INIT p.1], [DGTR_DIAL_INIT p.2].
- PCN: surface area (at most 1.3 m², 1.3–1.8, at least 1.8) and flux (high / other). Housing material (polycarbonate or polypropylene) was added on 11-Aug-2026. The PUC is unchanged [DGTR_DIAL_INIT p.2], [DGTR_DIAL_PCN p.2].
- POI 1-Jan-2025 to 31-Dec-2025. The injury period covers FY23–FY25 and the POI [DGTR_DIAL_INIT p.3].
- Standing: Polymed is "the major domestic producer". The only other producer is Nipro Medical India, a 100% EOU whose domestic sales count as domestic production [DGTR_DIAL_INIT p.3].
- Normal value was constructed from the applicant's own costs for both countries (China treated as a non-market economy). The export price comes from DG Systems CIF data. The dumping margin is "above de-minimis and significant". No figures were published [DGTR_DIAL_INIT p.3], [DGTR_DIAL_INIT p.4].
- Injury claimed:
  - Subject imports rose "significantly in absolute terms".
  - There was price undercutting and price depression.
  - The domestic industry "has incurred financial losses, cash losses and negative return on capital employed during the period of investigation".
  - This is disclosure of Polymed's dialyser economics [DGTR_DIAL_INIT p.4].
- Procedure: questionnaire responses extended to 9-Sep-2026 [DGTR_DIAL_EXT p.1]. 11 registered parties, including Shandong Weigao Blood Purification, Wego Healthcare (Shenzhen), Bain Medical (Guangzhou), Vital Healthcare Sdn Bhd (Malaysia), Chengdu OCI, Jiangxi Sanxin, and importers Dora Medical and LepuCare [DGTR_DIAL_IP p.2], [DGTR_DIAL_IP p.3]. The notice is dated "15th May, 2026" on its face but was published on 15-Sep-2026 [DGTR_DIAL_IP p.1], [DGTR_DIAL_CASE p.1]. **There were no preliminary findings as of 24-Sep-2026.** The case page lists only four documents, the last on 15/09/2026 [DGTR_DIAL_CASE p.1].

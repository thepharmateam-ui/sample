Industry, regulation and policy

Q90Must-askRegulation and policy

What share of domestic revenue is sold through the trade channel at MRP, what trade margin do your products typically carry today, and how would a cap on trade margins change your realizations?

Why it matters
India is weighing a cap on trade margins for medical devices; the exposure depends on the channel mix and MRP headroom (Section B3).
Our context
A press report of a Maharashtra FDA survey named a Polymed IV set bought at Rs. 10.5 against an MRP of Rs. 187, a mark-up of 1,681%. The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Health recommended capping the gap between landing price and MRP at 20% in Aug-2026, and the Health Minister ordered a review. Domestic revenue was Rs. 5.8bn in FY26.
Listen for
Reassuring: modest trade margins and hospital-led pricing. Concerning: MRPs set well above realized prices.
Follow-up
What share of domestic revenue is billed to patients at MRP, as against package rates, chain contracts or tenders – and would you reset MRPs ahead of a cap?

Q91Must-askRegulation and policy

With the US tariff on Indian devices at 10% and higher US duties on Chinese syringes and needles, which of your products become competitive in the US, and what is your exposure to the Section 232 investigation?

Why it matters
US trade policy is the largest external swing factor for the export thesis (Section B4).
Our context
The duty on Indian HS 9018 goods rose to 25% plus a further 25% in Aug-2025, fell to nil after the Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling of 20-Feb-2026, and is now a 10% Section 301 duty from 24-Jul-2026. Chinese syringes and needles pay 100% under Section 301. US syringe imports from India (all makers) rose from $930k in 2024 to $14.8mn in 2025, while imports from China fell.
Listen for
Reassuring: product-by-product US plans. Concerning: US ambitions contingent on tariff outcomes.
Follow-up
Commerce had 270 days to report on Section 232. Are you considering US-side packaging, assembly or sourcing to qualify for any exemption?

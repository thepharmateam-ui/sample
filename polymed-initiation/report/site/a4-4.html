A4.4 Transfusion and blood management

Blood management, entered in 2000, is Polymed's third-largest product family on the BRSR and its least transparent: the BRSR puts it at ~2.1x the size the placement document showed a year earlier (our calculation), and every consistent series shows it growing more slowly than the group. Demand comes from a public blood system that buys largely by tender, against two larger incumbents. The levers are a doubling of capacity at Palwal from Mar-2027 and a shift to multi-bag, filtered systems as India separates more of its blood into components.

What it is and how it is used: one bag system per donation

A blood bag system is a sterile set of PVC bags, pre-filled with anticoagulant, into which a donor's blood is drawn through an attached 16G needle; the MD calls it a "device plus drug" combination. CPDA keeps whole blood or packed red cells for 35 days, and SAGM additive solution extends red cells to 42 days.

The unit of demand is the donation: each unit collected uses one bag system, and what varies is its complexity.

  • Components. Double to quintuple systems carry satellite bags so that a blood centre can centrifuge a unit into red cells, plasma and platelets, letting one donation "meet the needs of more than one patient" (WHO).
  • Premium configurations add a TOTM platelet bag (about 5 days' storage), top-and-bottom bags for automated extraction, an in-line leuko-depletion filter, or a needle guard with a pouch that diverts the first, skin-contaminated blood.
  • Smaller items include paediatric and Penta bags (one unit split among up to five paediatric recipients), cord-blood bags and the ACD anticoagulant bag used in apheresis.

Each transfusion then uses a filtered administration set. Collection tubes and needles, reported in the same line, are covered in Section A4.5.

Polymed's portfolio and position

Polymed entered transfusion in 2000, three years after infusion, and by FY16 sold a quadruple bag with an in-line red-cell filter. Its domestic and export catalogues cover every configuration above, with main bags from 250 ml to 500 ml, plus Haemofusor and Transvol administration sets and a pressure pump set; the latest decks add a bedside filter and a lab-side leukocyte filter. It does not make IV fluid bags.

Management sells transfusion as a basket: it pitched blood banking as a "concept" at Arab Health in 2024 and promised to "keep on adding more and more products" in transfusion and vascular access. The blood-banking equipment the MD floated in Feb-2022 has not appeared: the catalogues list no collection monitors, mixers or other blood-bank machines. Market share is [not disclosed]; the MD has said only that Polymed gained share in blood transfusion (May-2022) and that new capacity would raise its domestic and export share (Feb-2024).

Capacity and supply chain.

  • Expansion. Capacity was added in FY24, with new lines due by May-2024 "for exports and for the local market". In Aug-2025 the MD said transfusion capacity would double at Palwal in Haryana, a plant he now describes as focused on "orthopedics and transfusion" plus extra infusion capacity.
  • Palwal. Budgeted at Rs. 2.7bn in the 2024 placement document and billed as the company's largest plant, it is due for Phase-1 by Mar-2027 and Phase-2 by Dec-2027. Current capacity, and which plant makes blood bags today, are [not disclosed].
  • Inputs. The placement document lists "blood bag sheets" among raw materials, which we read as the PVC film being bought in rather than extruded.

Revenue: four disclosures, four bases

In the annual reports we have, blood bags are the only manufactured product besides IV cannulas ever shown separately, and only for FY12-FY16. Every later figure is broader or inconsistent (Exhibit 47).

Blood bags, FY12-FY16. Sales rose from Rs. 179mn to Rs. 319mn, a 15.5% CAGR against 18.5% for all finished goods (our calculation), in steps: +23% in FY13, +1% over the next two years and +43% in FY16. We read the lumpiness as consistent with tender-driven demand.

Blood management and collection, FY18-FY24. The placement documents report a broader line (bags, administration sets, collection tubes and needles) on consolidated product sales. It grew from Rs. 449mn to Rs. 765mn, a 9.3% CAGR against 17.0% for all product sales, so its share fell from 8.8% to 5.9% (our calculation). Two points jar:

  1. the line fell 16% in FY20 (our calculation), yet the same document names the blood bag among the products behind that year's 14.6% rise in manufactured sales;
  2. the May-2022 deck showed "blood transfusion" at 9% of sales, presumably for FY22, against 6.3% in the placement document.

The BRSR, FY25-FY26. The BRSR puts "Blood Management & Blood Collection" at 10.0% of FY25 and 9.5% of FY26 standalone turnover, ~Rs. 1.6bn and ~Rs. 1.6bn, an implied -0.6% change (our calculation). No filing or call explains the step up from FY24; we read it as reclassification, not growth, though the company has not said so. The flat FY26 also sits awkwardly with the annual report, which names blood bags first among FY26 growth products, and with the ~18% transfusion growth the MD cited for 1QFY26; weaker collection tubes could hide growth in bags. Management declines to split the line: the MD called each category outside infusion and renal "not significant under 5%" (Feb-2025) and the transfusion numbers "confidential" (May-2025).

Market: India collects more blood, and discards more of it

Volumes. Collections reported on e-Raktkosh rose 24% over 2023-2025 to 15.7mn units, above the government's requirement estimate of 14.6mn units. Growth slowed to 8% in 2025, and discards rose to 11% of collections, with expiry the largest cause (our calculation; Exhibit 48). Reporting is patchy: in one study ~22% of blood banks rarely or never updated the portal.

Mix. Policy has promoted component separation since the 2003 Action Plan for Blood Safety, and for bag makers it is a second lever besides volume: the share of blood separated into components rises with national income (Exhibit 49), and each separated unit needs a multi-bag system. India's separation rate is [not disclosed] in our sources.

Structure. A 1996 Supreme Court judgment that ended paid donation led to the National Blood Transfusion Council and state councils; blood banks have been licensed since 1997, and the DGHS took over from NACO in 2021. India has 4,263 licensed blood centres, and the DGHS supports 1,131 public and charitable ones with staff, training and the procurement of blood bags and test kits.

Value. There is no current Indian market size. A 2018 Business Today report on Terumo BCT put India's 11mn annual collections at a $50mn market, ~$4.5 per collection (our calculation). 360iResearch puts the global market at $621.42mn in 2026, growing 7.3% a year to $968.19mn by 2032 (vendor estimates), ~$5.2 per donation on WHO's 120.4mn (our calculation, different vintages). Lower-middle-income export markets collect only 8.5 donations per thousand people, against 28.9 in high-income countries.

Customers, channel and pricing

In India the customers are blood banks, which the BRSR names among Polymed's customers.

  • Public and charitable blood banks get bags through government procurement, which the DGHS support above covers. HS 90189032 "blood transfusion apparatus" is one of 19 device lines reserved since Mar-2021 for Class-I local suppliers (at least 50% local content), and the DoP's illustrative list of such suppliers names Polymed.
  • Private blood banks buy through distributors, which for Polymed are blood-banking specialists.
  • Exports go through overseas distributors, who sell from the full export catalogue.

Prices and the channel split are [not disclosed]. We read the market as price-led: value per collection is low, incumbent capacity exceeds domestic demand, and blood centres' own charges for blood follow national rates, last revised in Jun-2022.

Competition: two incumbents with more capacity than India collects

In 2018 Terumo Penpol made 30mn bags and exported 55%, leaving ~13.5mn units for a country that collected 11mn units (our calculation). HLL Lifecare, a government enterprise, can make 12.5mn pieces a year and claims a "dominant market share". We see Polymed as a smaller challenger that competes on breadth of range and on local-supplier status in tenders.

The global leaders are moving into equipment: Terumo's blood business grew on Reveos automated whole-blood processing, with tender wins in Asia, and on Rika plasma collection. Polymed sells only disposables (an ACD bag, but no apheresis kits), and we see equipment-linked systems as its main long-run threat in large blood centres.

Regulation and trends

  • Indian licensing. Blood bags were on the short list of devices already licensed before the 2020 amendment to the Medical Devices Rules, so we read the Oct-2023 licensing of Class C and D devices, which helped renal, as having changed little here. HLL was the first maker certified by BIS to the ISO 3826 blood-bag standard (Aug-2023); Polymed's status is [not disclosed].
  • DEHP. DEHP typically makes up 40% of plasticized PVC by weight and helps preserve stored red cells, which we read as making red-cell bags harder to convert than tubing. The EU requires authorization for its use in devices after 1 July 2030, with applications due by 1 January 2029 (Section B5). Polymed's catalogues call its bags "medical grade PVC sheet" without naming the plasticizer; only the TOTM platelet bag and the DEHP-free administration sets are specified. HLL's bags are also PVC.

Company-specific growth drivers, ranked

  1. Mix shift to component systems (structural; our view). Higher separation rates favour triple, quadruple, SAGM and filtered bags without extra donations; Polymed's range covers them, so the lever is its share of the upgrade.
  2. Palwal capacity (management plan; Phase-1 by Mar-2027). Doubling capacity pays off only if tenders and exports absorb it.
  3. Collection growth (structural but slowing). Collections already exceed the requirement estimate, and rising discards may make blood centres collect more carefully rather than more.
  4. Exports and DEHP-free bags (our view; 2029-2030). Low donation rates abroad and the EU deadline favour makers with validated alternatives; Polymed's readiness is [not disclosed].
  5. Adjacent products (management ambition): filters, the Haemodraw segment device and, possibly, blood-bank equipment.

Company-specific margin drivers, ranked

  1. Tender pricing against surplus capacity (our view): public buying and two large incumbents cap prices on standard bags.
  2. Mix: multi-bag, SAGM and filtered systems carry more value per donation; realizations by configuration are [not disclosed].
  3. Utilization: doubling capacity ahead of demand would weigh on absorption from FY28 until volumes follow (our inference).
  4. Materials: bought-in PVC film and the cost of any move away from DEHP drive the cost of goods.

What to watch

Open questions for management (question bank): the split between bags, sets and tubes; why the BRSR share differs from the placement documents; volumes and prices by channel; current and Palwal capacity; the plasticizer in red-cell bags; and BIS certification.

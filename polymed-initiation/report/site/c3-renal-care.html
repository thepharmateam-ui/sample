Renal care

Q30Must-askRenal

Management says Chinese dialysers enter India duty-free, paying 5% GST, while Polymed pays customs duty of 2.5% on imported raw materials and 18% GST. What is the landed price gap per dialyser today, and what happens to renal pricing if the anti-dumping investigation into dialysers from China and Malaysia does not lead to duties?

Why it matters
Dialyser pricing sets renal margins and the payback on the PLI-backed capacity (Section A4.3).
Our context
Polymed filed the petition (initiated 25-Jun-2026) and claims an injury margin of 20%. It told DGTR that the domestic industry made financial and cash losses and a negative return on capital in CY25. Renal revenue fell 3.8% yoy in 1QFY27 as Polymed chose price over volume.
Listen for
Reassuring: cost-down plans that close the gap without duties. Concerning: a strategy that depends on the trade remedy.
Follow-up
What duty would restore dialyser profitability, and how large were the losses the petition describes?

Q31Must-askRenal

What is the annual consumables pull-through per installed dialysis machine – bloodlines, fistula needles and dialysers – and what share of machines is sold outright versus placed against consumable commitments?

Why it matters
The razor-and-blade logic is the case for the machine business; placement terms decide its capital intensity.
Our context
Polymed placed 450 machines in FY26 against 500-600 guided, taking the installed base to about 1,000 machines. It had bid for more than 600 machines by Feb-2026.
Listen for
Reassuring: contractual consumable commitments and measured pull-through per machine. Concerning: machines sold at low margins without consumable lock-in.
Follow-up
How many service engineers support the installed base, and what uptime do you commit to?

Q32If time permitsRenal

Management said the single-use share of dialysers moved to 30-35% of the market and could reach 50% single use and 50% multi-use. What drives conversion – PMNDP rules, private chains or infection data – and what does it do to your volumes?

Why it matters
Each point of single-use conversion multiplies dialyser volumes, because a reused dialyser serves several sessions.
Our context
Management said reuse was the norm a few years earlier, with each dialyser reused 6x-8x (May-2023).
Listen for
Reassuring: named policy or chain decisions behind the shift. Concerning: conversion assumed rather than observed.
Follow-up
What price difference makes a centre switch from reuse to single use?

Q33If time permitsRenal

Your dialyser market share target moves from 10-12% to 15-17% and to 20-25% by 2030. Which customer groups get you there: PMNDP operators, dialysis chains or hospitals?

Why it matters
The customer groups differ in price, payment terms and switching costs.
Our context
PMNDP operates in 751 districts with 1,704 centres, according to the company.
Listen for
Reassuring: named chain contracts and PPP operators. Concerning: reliance on government tenders alone.
Follow-up
Are you on the approved list of the largest private dialysis chains?

Q34If time permitsRenal

Parliamentary data show no PLI incentive paid to Polymed so far, although it has invested Rs. 556mn of the Rs. 720mn it committed. Which thresholds have not been met, and what happens to the economics of the dialyser capacity if the incentive never comes?

Why it matters
The PLI's economics depend on hitting thresholds; the FY26 annual report discloses no PLI receipt (Section B3).
Our context
Management described an incremental-revenue requirement of Rs. 600mn a year. Nipro, the other domestic dialyser maker, received Rs. 349mn of PLI incentive over FY23-FY25.
Listen for
Reassuring: incentive accrued on track. Concerning: thresholds missed and incentive at risk.
Follow-up
What happens to the dialyser capacity if the PLI lapses?

Q35If time permitsRenal

Government reimbursement per dialysis session rose from ~Rs. 1,200 to Rs. 1,800 under the National Dialysis Programme and Ayushman Bharat, according to management. Does any of that reach consumable prices, and who sets the consumables price in PPP contracts?

Why it matters
Tests whether renal pricing has any link to reimbursement.
Our context
Management cited the change on the 3QFY25 call.
Listen for
Reassuring: contracts with operators that pass through part of the increase. Concerning: consumables priced purely on tender.
Follow-up
Who is the largest PPP dialysis operator you supply?

Q36If time permitsRenal

How much of the dialysis machine is made in India, and which modules are still imported?

Why it matters
Local content drives cost, lead times and eligibility for procurement preferences.
Our context
Management put local content at around 50%.
Listen for
Reassuring: a plan to localize pumps and electronics. Concerning: dependence on one foreign module supplier.
Follow-up
What is the warranty cost per machine?

Q37If time permitsRenal

Do you have a plan for peritoneal dialysis or home haemodialysis, where the market is moving in developed countries?

Why it matters
Home dialysis is a structural trend that could bypass centre-based consumables (Section B5).
Our context
Polymed's range already includes peritoneal-dialysis sets; there is no disclosed home-dialysis programme.
Listen for
Reassuring: a considered position either way. Concerning: no view.
Follow-up
Which global player would you partner with in home dialysis?

Q38Must-askRenal

In May-2025 you said renal was not loss-making and had a positive operating margin. DGTR's initiation notice summarizes the petition as reporting financial losses, cash losses and a negative return on capital for the domestic dialyser industry in CY25. How do the two statements fit together: different scope, different period or a different cost allocation?

Why it matters
This is the only published data point on dialyser economics, and it points the other way from earlier commentary (Section A4.3).
Our context
Renal revenue was Rs. 1.9bn in FY26; management does not disclose a renal margin. The petition's period of investigation is CY25; the May-2025 comment covered the renal line as a whole.
Listen for
Reassuring: a clear bridge, for example dialysers loss-making while bloodlines, needles and machines are profitable, with a path to breakeven. Concerning: no reconciliation, or losses across the whole line.
Follow-up
At what utilization of the dialyser plant does the product break even?

Q39If time permitsRenal

Your share claims for renal have ranged from 6-7% (May-2023) to 10-12% (Feb-2025) and 10% (Feb-2026). What market is the denominator: dialysers or all consumables, value or volume, public and private centres?

Why it matters
The claims move from call to call and cannot be tested without a stated base (Section A4.3).
Our context
The targets have moved too, from 10-15% within about five years (May-2021) to 20-25% by 2030 (Feb-2025).
Listen for
Reassuring: one consistent definition, with dialyser units and sessions behind it. Concerning: shares quoted on different bases.
Follow-up
What is your share in PPP centres versus private chains?

Q40If time permitsRenal

How long does a public dialysis tender take from bid to installation, and what share of FY26 machine sales went to government or PPP buyers versus private hospitals and chains?

Why it matters
FY26 machine sales of 450 units fell short of the 500-600 guided because government contracts slipped; the tender cycle decides how lumpy renal growth is.
Our context
Management had bid for more than 600 machines by Feb-2026 and plans a 20% rise in machine sales in FY27.
Listen for
Reassuring: a mix weighted to private buyers, or tenders already awarded. Concerning: dependence on a few state budgets.
Follow-up
Do tender contracts include consumables, or only the machines?

Q41If time permitsRenal

Where does the India-made HDF machine stand, and who will buy it if PM-JAY pays the same per session for HDF as for haemodialysis?

Why it matters
HDF is the premium end of the market and is dominated by MNCs; the reimbursement ceiling limits it to private centres.
Our context
Management put HDF at close to around 10% of Indian dialysis and the private sector at probably like 30%-40% of the market (Aug-2025).
Listen for
Reassuring: regulatory approval in hand and named private-chain trials. Concerning: launch dates slipping without a buyer.
Follow-up
Will the HDF machine use Polymed's own dialysers and bloodlines only?

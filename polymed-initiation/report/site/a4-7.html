A4.7 Gastroenterology, urology, surgery and wound drainage

These are three of Polymed's oldest ranges (feeding tubes, urine bags and catheters, and wound and chest drains), and their product lists in the FY16 annual report and the Aug-2024 placement document are the same. Surgery and wound drainage and urology together were 8.3% of FY26 standalone turnover, ~Rs. 1.4bn (our calculation); gastroenterology is not shown separately. None is among the MD's focus areas (orthopaedics, cardiology, neonatology, oncology and renal care); he says Polymed has "not covered" urology or gastroenterology as therapies, and small segments such as surgery are "clubbed under the General category". On our calculation both reported lines shrank in FY26 while standalone revenue grew. The one upgrade under way is in enteral feeding.

What is disclosed: a five-year gap, then two BRSR shares a year

The Feb-2021 placement document is the only source with rupee revenue for all three lines (Exhibit 61, Exhibit 62). Over FY18-FY20 surgery and wound drainage compounded at 8.1% a year, urology at 12.6% and gastroenterology at 13.3%, all below the 14.7% of total product sales (our calculation). Investor decks gave rounded shares until May-2022; since then the three lines sit inside "others", and the MD called such figures "confidential" in May-2025.

The BRSR restarted the series for the standalone company:

  • FY26. Surgery and wound drainage fell from 5.7% of turnover to 5.3%, and urology from 3.3% to 3.0%. That implies Rs. 908mn falling to Rs. 889mn (-2.1%) and Rs. 527mn to Rs. 497mn (-5.7%), our calculation. With blood management (Section A4.4), the three named lines outside infusion and dialysis were 17.9% of FY26 turnover (our calculation).
  • Across the gap. Surgery's share rose from 4.3% of product sales in FY20 to 5.7% of standalone turnover in FY25, its revenue up 3.1x against 2.4x for the company on the same mixed bases; urology's share slipped from 3.8% to 3.3%, its revenue up 2.1x (our calculation). Surgery outgrew the company; urology lagged it.
  • Gastroenterology is not listed. The residual "other" line, where we would expect it, was 1.0% of FY26 turnover.

The MD's Feb-2025 remark that each of the 5-6 categories outside infusion and renal is "under 5%" of revenue does not fit surgery, whose standalone BRSR figure alone was 5.4% of FY25 consolidated revenue (our calculation); the BRSR category may be broader than management's.

Gastroenterology and enteral feeding: small, but the one range being upgraded

The range is Ryle's (nasogastric) and Levin's tubes, Novofeed infant feeding tubes, a 1200 ml feeding bag, and umbilical catheters that Polymed files under gastroenterology; stomach tubes appear only in the veterinary range. Each insertion uses one tube, replaced when blocked, displaced or at protocol intervals. Revenue was last disclosed at Rs. 39mn for 9MFY21.

The enteral-connector transition. The enteral part of the ISO 80369 small-bore connector series, known as ENFit, uses fittings that cannot mate with IV luer connectors, so feeds cannot be given into a vein by mistake, and regulated markets are converting to it. 360iResearch calls the "broad implementation of standardized enteral connectors" a key shift.

  • Export range. The Oct-2024 export catalogue lists enteral-connector versions of the infant (POLYFIT), Ryle's and Levin's tubes, a feeding bag, an extension set with purple connectors and an enteral stopcock designed to prevent connection to "vascular, respiratory, or epidural lines". The website also lists them under critical care, the ICU division management is building (Section A4.6).
  • India. The Oct-2025 domestic catalogue lists no enteral-connector versions. The 31st AGM deck (Sep-2026) shows a Ryle's tube and a 1200 ml feeding bag with enteral connector, and an enteral syringe, among recent launches; we read this as the connector range being extended.
  • Gap. No Polymed document uses the ENFit name or cites the enteral part of ISO 80369, so conformity is [not disclosed].

360iResearch (Sep-2026, a vendor estimate) sizes enteral feeding devices at $3.18bn in 2026, growing 7.7% a year to $4.99bn by 2032. It profiles three Indian makers, Angiplast, Romsons and Sterimed, alongside BD, B. Braun, Cardinal Health and Medline, but not Polymed. Polymed lists no enteral product with the FDA.

Urology: catheters and bags without the premium materials

Polymed sells two-way Foley balloon catheters of "silicone elastomer bonded with latex rubber"; PVC Nelaton, female and rectal catheters; urine bags (the Polyuro Premium with a T-type bottom outlet and sampling port, the Deluxe and Echo, leg and paediatric bags); the Polyurimeter measured-volume meter; and TUR and irrigation sets. Each catheterization episode uses a catheter and a bag or meter, changed when full or at protocol intervals.

CAUTI and the closed-system upgrade. Grand View Research lists concerns over catheter-associated urinary tract infection (CAUTI) as a restraint on the Foley market and "rising demand for antimicrobial catheters" as a driver. Polymed's part in the closed-system upgrade is on the drainage side (Exhibit 63): bags with needle-free sampling ports and optional air filters, and a urimeter whose sampling port "maintains closed system, reduces the chance of Urinary Tract Infection".

Market. Grand View (Jun-2026, a vendor estimate) values the global Foley market at $1.9bn in 2025, rising to $3.0bn by 2033, a 5.6% CAGR from 2026. IMARC puts intermittent catheters, where Polymed has only PVC Nelaton catheters, at $3.1bn (also a vendor estimate). Frost & Sullivan's 2021 data in Healthium's prospectus gave Coloplast 39.3% of UK urology consumables and Coloplast with BD 47.9% (FY21), while cheaper independents gained: Healthium's Clinisupplies took its UK collection-device share from 10.6% to 14.0% in FY19-FY21.

India's public tenders. Urine and stool catheters (HS 90183910) are one of 19 device lines reserved since Mar-2021 for Class-I local suppliers, those with at least 50% local content. The government's illustrative supplier list for the line names Polymed alongside Romsons, Angiplast, Sterimed, Healthium, Nipro India, Biorad and a long tail of small makers, which we read as a fragmented, price-led market.

Surgery and wound drainage: the largest of the three

The range covers closed wound suction (Polyvac bellows units, the spring-loaded Novovac and the 50 ml Neovac for minor and plastic surgery), Ultravak high-pressure vacuum bottles and Redon drains, Polyseal under-water-seal chest drainage with Ultra Cath Novo thoracic catheters, Polysuction Yankauer sets and an abdominal drainage set. The 30th AGM deck (Sep-2025) listed a decompressor needle and a centesis catheter as recent launches; no skin staplers or sutures are listed. Each operation that needs a drain uses one set, and each chest-drain insertion one catheter and seal system.

Future Market Insights (Aug-2025, a vendor estimate) sizes surgical drainage devices at $1.7bn in 2025, growing 7.7% a year to $3.6bn by 2035, and Polymed serves its largest segments (Exhibit 63). In India, CRISIL (commissioned by Polymed, Aug-2024; a vendor estimate) puts general and plastic surgery at 5% of the device market in FY24, growing 11-12% a year to FY28, and 1Lattice counts ~165.7mn surgical procedures in FY25.

Competition: MNC specialists above, many Indian and Chinese makers below

Polymed's position rests on breadth within the distributor basket and on local manufacture, not on product leadership: no vendor report profiles it, and we found no share claim for any of these lines.

Customers, channel, pricing and regulation

No customer, channel or price data are disclosed for these lines, sold through the infusion network: in India, 506 distributors and a sales team of 440 covering 8,000 hospitals and nursing homes (Jun-2024), with private buyers ~90% of domestic sales. Management is shrinking government business, ~10-12% of domestic sales and down 18% in 3QFY26, because prices are lower and states pay late. That cuts against urology's one protected channel, the local-supplier reservation in public tenders.

  • Price caps and trade margins. MRP increases on devices are capped at 10% a year. In Sep-2026 a Maharashtra FDA survey found a catheter (type and maker not named) bought for Rs. 29.4 with an MRP of Rs. 310, a 955% mark-up, and on 18 Sep the Health Minister asked the DoP to explore curbs on mark-ups on hospital consumables (Section B3). The mark-up accrues to distributors and hospitals, so a cap would hit the channel before Polymed's price (our view).
  • Europe. Management said Polymed "had to drop many products" in the MDD-to-MDR transition; whether any were in these ranges is [not disclosed].
  • US. Polymed lists a leg bag, a urine flow-rate meter, a non-powered suction apparatus and a vacuum collection bottle with the FDA, none with a 510(k) in its name.

Company-specific growth drivers, ranked

  1. Surgical volumes and drainage (our view; ongoing). Surgery is the largest line and the only one that gained share from FY20 to FY25, and vendors expect drains to grow faster (7.7% a year) than Foley catheters (5.6%). The FY26 decline needs an explanation first.
  2. Enteral-connector conversion (company launches; our view on uptake). The connector tubes, syringe and feeding bag replace funnel-end tubes with a higher-specification item as regulated markets switch; conformity, registrations and pricing are [not disclosed].
  3. ICU and neonatal cross-selling through critical care (our read-across). The division already carries the enteral range and reached 500 hospitals by Nov-2025, a route for urimeters and closed urine bags; infant feeding tubes and umbilical catheters fit neonatology, a stated focus area.
  4. Therapy entry (management option, deferred). In Jul-2024 the MD named urology (from renal) and gastroenterology (from critical care) as adjacencies while saying Polymed would stay with its "current 6 segments" for the next 4-5 years; in Aug-2026 he ruled out anything new "substantially in the next few years".

Company-specific margin drivers, ranked

  1. Price competition. These are commodity items with many local makers, Chinese makers such as KDL are registering the same products in Europe, and Grand View lists "pricing pressure and competitive tendering" as a challenge for Foley makers.
  2. Raw materials. PVC compounds, latex and silicone: Polymed imported 74.0% of its raw-material needs in 1QFY25, management cited a ~20% rise in raw-material costs in early FY27, and price increases lag.
  3. Mix. Limited: enteral-connector versions are the only premium upgrade under way (Exhibit 63).
  4. Channel. Leaving lower-priced government tenders helps margin but costs volume in all three lines.

Margins for these lines are [not disclosed]; asked which products earn more, the MD replied, "you are asking the secret recipe for my business?". Romsons, whose range overlaps in urology, earned an EBITDA margin of 26.8% on Rs. 8.6bn of revenue across its whole range in FY24, which suggests these categories can pay at scale.

What to watch

Open questions for management, from revenue by line to ENFit conformity and MDR lapses, are in the question bank.

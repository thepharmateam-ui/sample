A5 Geographies and legal entities

Polymed is an Indian exporter first and a multinational second. 69% of FY26 revenue was earned outside India, yet only 11.3% came from foreign subsidiaries (our calculation). The rest was shipped from the Indian plants to distributors abroad. FY26 split the two apart: the holding company's own exports fell 3.2%, while the subsidiaries' sales rose 213% as PendraCare and Citieffe were consolidated (our calculation). We therefore treat international revenue as two businesses with different drivers:

  1. Indian-made disposables sold through distributors, which move with distributor stocking, tenders, product approvals, freight and Chinese pricing;
  2. acquired European device makers that sell partly direct to surgeons and hospitals, which move with clinical relationships, tender calendars and the euro.

Where the revenue comes from

The annual report gives two views that together show where FY26 went wrong and where it went right.

  • Consolidated regions. Revenue is split into India, Europe and the rest of the world (RoW) in the MD&A and the presentations.
  • The holding company's exports. The consolidated notes split Poly Medicure Ltd's export sales into five regions and add the foreign subsidiaries' sales as a separate line.

Three points stand out.

  1. Europe is still the largest destination but lost share. Europe was 46% of the holding company's FY26 exports against 49% a year earlier (our calculation). Section A3 sets out the destocking, Chinese pricing and certification delays that management blamed (Exhibit 14).
  2. Asia was the only export region that grew (+15.9%, our calculation). The Asia line includes West Asia, where the Gulf conflict hit only in the last weeks of FY26.
  3. The Americas are the second-largest export destination (26% of exports). Latin America is the bulk of it: management has called LatAm its second-largest export market after Europe, and the US contributed less than $3-3.5mn of revenue in FY26 by management's account.

The regional mix has barely moved in two years: India, Europe and RoW each earn about a third of revenue, which the MD calls his long-run goal of 1/3 India and 2/3 RoW. That stability hides a change in how the non-Indian revenue is earned. In FY26 17% of revenue from outside India came from subsidiaries, with PendraCare and Citieffe consolidated for only part of the year; in 1QFY27 the two acquisitions alone were 19% of international revenue (both our calculations).

The legal structure: an Indian exporter, a Dutch holding company and the acquired businesses

The listed company in India owns the Indian plants and makes almost all group volume. Nearly everything abroad is held through Poly Medicure BV in Amsterdam, a holding and financing company with no employees. It received €42.8mn of new equity from India in FY26, which it used to buy Medistream (Citieffe) and to lend €14.6mn to Risor Holdings for PendraCare.

The acquisitions changed the balance sheet as much as the income statement.

  • Assets abroad. Non-current assets located outside India rose 16x in a year, from Rs. 170mn to Rs. 2.8bn (our calculation). They sit in Switzerland (Rs. 1.4bn, mostly the Medistream acquisition), the Netherlands (Rs. 691mn), Italy (Rs. 518mn), the US (Rs. 93mn), Mexico (Rs. 85mn) and China (Rs. 47mn of investment at cost).
  • Debt abroad. Bank term loans in the foreign subsidiaries were Rs. 681mn at 31 Mar 2026, all of it Citieffe's, at 1.5% to 3.12% plus 3M Euribor. The Indian parent has only working-capital debt.
  • Minorities. The Rs. 431mn of non-controlling interest is the sellers' retained stake in PendraCare, which Polymed can buy out by 2030 (Section A4.8).

What the table says about each part:

  • Plan1Health is the one foreign subsidiary that was already working. Its turnover rose 43% in rupees in CY25 (our calculation) and it made a profit in each of the three years. Section A4.2 covers it in detail.
  • PolyHealth is a support company, not the US business. Its revenue of $211k in FY26 (+102%, our calculation) is a small fraction of the US revenue that management puts at below $3-3.5mn, most of which is shipped from India direct to US distributors and group purchasing organizations (GPOs). It lost $346k in FY26, needed USD 375,000 of fresh capital from Poly Medicure BV, and its FY25 accounts carry a going-concern note that relies on parent support.
  • The acquired businesses made losses in their first months. Risor's post-deal loss and Citieffe's currency-driven CY25 loss are analysed in Sections A4.8 and A4.9. The ~Rs. 90mn one-off provision management booked in 4QFY26 'in one of our international subsidiaries' has not been attributed to an entity.
  • The China plant has shrunk into a loss (see Asia below).

Europe: distributors, a direct foothold in Italy and a regulatory reset

Europe is 32% of group revenue and the market Polymed built deliberately. Europe rose from 10-15% of exports around FY18 to almost 40% by FY22 after a Europe CEO was hired in 2017. Six Western European markets (the UK, France, Italy, Germany, Spain and Sweden) produced 80-85% of European revenue in May-2024.

How Polymed sells there. The Indian-made range goes through distributors: 75+ of them, with 82 products approved and 15 in the pipeline (May-2026). The acquired companies bring their own channels: Citieffe sells direct in Italy plus 12 distributors, PendraCare uses 17 distributors and 2 OEM relationships, and Plan1Health 20+ distributors. Italy, through Citieffe, is the only European market where the group sells direct; Plan1Health sells through distributors. The MD said in Aug-2026 that he wants to go direct in 3-4 more geographies, starting with new products, because 'our distributors only sell our existing products'.

What drives European revenue, in the order we rank them:

  1. Distributor stocking. European distributors normally hold 4-5 months of inventory; they cut it to 2-3 months in 2025, which management cited as a cause of the FY26 contraction alongside Chinese pricing and certification delays. The 1QFY27 organic growth of 17.6% came from 'new customer acquisitions' plus restocking. Management guided to 5-6 new customers from Apr-2026.
  2. Approvals under the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR). 89 products received MDR certificates, but management admits it 'had to drop many products initially' while waiting. The pipeline is 30+ products under registration and ~25 products due for CE marking within three to four months of Aug-2026. Under the EU's 2023 extension of the transition, legacy certificates run to 31 Dec 2027 for class III and implantable IIb devices and to 31 Dec 2028 for the rest. That gives time to products still on legacy certificates; Citieffe and Plan1Health say their whole portfolios are already MDR-approved (Sections A4.2 and A4.9), which leaves PendraCare's pipeline as the acquired business most exposed.
  3. Chinese competition, now partly fenced off in public tenders. Management blamed 1HFY26 on Chinese product redirected from the US. Since 30 Jun 2025 the EU has excluded Chinese bidders from public medical-device tenders of €5mn or more and capped China-origin content at 50%; such tenders are ~59% of EU device procurement. The company names the acquisitions as its route into tender markets, which suggests the standalone business sells little through EU tenders today (our inference).
  4. Clinical selling. Polymed has hired 25+ clinical staff to support distributors, after concluding that distributors 'are not able to do justice' to the clinical case for its products; it plans such teams in 25-30 markets. The MD's reasoning: to win on price alone 'I have to be 10% cheaper than China', which Polymed's scale does not allow.

The UK deserves separate mention. It was 5-6% of revenue in FY25, the National Health Service does ~90% of purchasing, and the India-UK trade agreement cut UK duty on most of Polymed's products from 4-5% to zero. Polymed bid for 6-7 new product categories, mostly vascular access, in the NHS's 2026 cycle and formed Poly Health Ltd to sell direct. The offset: NHS contracts slipped 1-1.5 years after the change of government.

United States: small revenue, a long approval queue and tariff whiplash

The US is the largest device market but a small Polymed market. Management guided US revenue of $2-3mn for FY25, said FY26 was below $3-3.5mn, and expects $3.5-4mn in FY27. The medium-term target of $15-20mn has kept its size but not its date: management called it a three-year goal in Jul-2024, three to four years in Oct-2024 and two to three years in Aug-2025.

The reasons it is still small, as management describes them:

  1. Approvals. The FDA database lists 6 510(k) clearances in Poly Medicure's name, all class II infusion devices (safety IV catheters and IV sets), the latest dated 1 Apr 2026. Management said in Nov-2025 that the FDA had started rejecting data from Indian test labs, so products had to be re-tested in the US and 'all work we have done in the past was negated'. It expected the new tests to finish by Apr-May 2026, re-filing to follow and new approvals from mid-2026.
  2. Channel. Polymed supplies through 4 distributors and GPO contracts ('two large GPOs and distributors'); 'our job is limited to manufacturing in India'. The FDA listing of a suction catheter kit at the Jaipur plant under the name of McKesson, a US distributor, is the only public sign of private-label supply.
  3. Tariffs. The US was 'a red flag because of ... tariff situation', which 'delayed our launch to the U.S. market quite a lot'.

The acquisitions give Polymed its first direct US presence. Citieffe sells to US trauma centres through its own reps and commissioned agents, not GPO contracts; the US was 16.9% of its CY25 sales, and management points to US orthopaedic prices of 4-5x Europe's. PendraCare's guiding catheter is cleared and management expects US sales to start in FY27.

West Asia: demand intact, shipments stuck

West Asia (the Gulf states and their neighbours) was 6-8% of revenue before the conflict that began in Feb-Mar 2026, by management's estimate. Management described demand as intact but called shipping 'a logistics nightmare': revenue from the region fell 32% yoy in 1QFY27, with 'all the orders ... still lying at ports or in factories'. That cut organic RoW growth to 4%.

  • Costs. Management said logistics costs rose 2-3 times in the six months to Aug-2026. Hapag-Lloyd added a war-risk surcharge of $1,500 per TEU, and Indian exporters faced 40-50% higher insurance on Gulf-routed cargo. The Drewry container index rose from $2,287 per 40ft on 2 Apr 2026 to $4,500 per 40ft on 17 Sep 2026.
  • The acquisitions add exposure. PendraCare had 'over-indexed exposure' of ~20% to the region, which management says is why it is not growing.
  • Recovery. Management expects West Asia to grow 20-25% once the situation improves. We would treat that as a rebound from a depressed base, not a trend.
  • Access. Saudi Arabia requires a marketing authorization and a licensed local authorized representative for every device.

Africa: a small export market, served partly through Egypt

Africa took Rs. 480mn of the holding company's FY26 exports, 4% of the total and -1.0% yoy (our calculation). Management has said Africa and South-East Asia 'don't contribute too much', and in Feb-2024 said the global currency crisis was hitting West Asia and Africa harder than Latin America. The Egyptian associate, which CRISIL describes as catering directly to 'the African and other markets', is Polymed's local manufacturing presence.

Latin America: Brazil goes direct

Latin America is Polymed's second-largest export region, with Brazil and Mexico growing and 'a good market share' in Argentina (May-2022). Registration is slow: management said in 2022 that registering a product in Mexico took almost two years.

Brazil is where Polymed is changing its model.

  • Market. Management sizes Brazil's device market at $13-15bn, the largest in South America. Brazil was the second-largest destination for India's exports of catheters, cannulas and needles (HS 901839) in 2025, at $46.7mn.
  • Rules. Under ANVISA's device rules of 2022, a registration must be held by a legal entity in Brazil and is valid for 10 years. A foreign maker selling through distributors leaves the registrations with them.
  • Moves. Polymed set up a Brazilian subsidiary in 1QFY26 (pre-operational; FY26 loss BRL 142k), then on 29 Apr 2026 bought Medyneo, a distribution company with ANVISA and import licences but no operations, for Rs. 3.4mn (about $40,000). Management says the purchase saves 18-24 months. Renato Rocha (ex-Descarpack, B. Braun and GE Healthcare) became CEO of Polymed Brazil in Aug-2026, and the Board has allocated funds for direct sales. The plan is to sell new products direct while distributors keep the existing range.
  • Mexico is covered by Citieffe's direct sales company, where Citieffe claims ~12% of its segment.

Asia: South-East Asia grows, the China plant winds down

South-East Asia is one of the two export markets management singled out for FY27 growth, alongside Europe. Polymed exhibited in Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and Vietnam in 2025 and names the region in its go-direct plans. The competitive risk is Chinese: in ASEAN, B. Braun reports 'enormous pricing pressure' and says Chinese manufacturers expanded their market shares, and Weigao's Indonesian joint venture is due to start local production in the second half of 2026.

China. Poly Medicure (Laiyang) has been a wholly owned plant in Shandong since 2007. The MD described its purpose in 2022 as understanding Chinese technology, sourcing and 'making some critical components'. It is now shrinking:

  • Sales fell 31% in renminbi between FY24 and FY26 to RMB 8.82mn (our calculation); exports were only RMB 361k.
  • Margins. Gross margin fell from 26.2% to 21.5% (our calculation), and the plant made a net loss of RMB 526k in FY26.
  • Future. The MD said in Jul-2024 that the plant (fewer than 40-50 people, revenue under $2mn) would 'probably close' because Chinese costs are 'much, much higher' than India's and its ~20-year lease expires within 18-24 months. The business licence runs to 23 May 2027 and the FY26 accounts say an extension is 'in process'. The two statements conflict; we treat closure as likely but unconfirmed.

Egypt: a profitable associate that pays dividends

Ultra for Medical Products (Ultra Med) runs a plant of 12,500 sq m in Assiut making disposable devices. Polymed owns 23%; CRISIL describes it as a joint venture with the El-Agar group.

  • Growth. Sales in rupees rose 53% between CY23 and CY25 to Rs. 1.2bn (our calculation), recovering from the Egyptian pound's devaluation, which had cut CY22 sales to Rs. 736mn.
  • Profit. The associate's CY25 profit was about Rs. 249mn, a net margin of ~20% (our calculation from the AOC statement). Polymed's share, Rs. 57mn, is its only equity-accounted profit.
  • Cash and trade. Polymed received Rs. 33mn in dividend and governing-council share in FY26 and sold it Rs. 63mn of goods. The investment is carried at Rs. 152mn against a cost of Rs. 8.9mn.

India: the growth engine is the private hospital

India produced Rs. 5.8bn of revenue in FY26, up 19.6%, the fastest of the three regions. Management is deliberately shrinking government business in India.

  • Private vs government. Private customers were ~90% of domestic revenue in 3QFY26 and grew 22.5%. Government business (10-12%) fell 18%. Management's reasons are late state payments and tenders that do not reward quality. The target is 92-93% private and 6-7% government.
  • Coverage. Polymed reaches ~8,000 hospitals, against the ~20,000 larger Indian hospitals that management counts as addressable. The MD put coverage at 'one-third of the hospital[s]' in Feb-2025 and wallet share in covered hospitals at 5-7% in Nov-2025, having said below 20% earlier.
  • Channel. 650+ distributors in India and the group's 590+ direct reps. Management plans 100+ sales hires in FY27, uses ~Rs. 10mn of revenue a year per rep as a benchmark, and has ~40-45 clinical staff in India. Indranil Mukherjee (ex-B. Braun and Translumina) joined in 2026 as CEO for India and Asia-Pacific. The MD says 'you can see Polymed products in every top private chain in the country'.
  • Policy. The GST cut on devices (12% to 5% from 22 Sep 2025) lowered end prices, though management linked a 2QFY26 domestic slowdown to channel inventory adjusting to it. The larger risk is trade-margin regulation. A Maharashtra FDA survey in Sep-2026 cited a Polymed IV set bought at Rs. 10.5 with an MRP of Rs. 187, and a parliamentary committee has recommended capping the gap between landing price and MRP at 20% (Section B3).

Company-specific drivers by region, ranked

  1. Europe organic recovery and tender access (management target, partly visible). Mechanism: new customers, restocking, MDR approvals and the EU's exclusion of Chinese bidders. Evidence: 17.6% organic growth in 1QFY27. Timing: FY27. Our view: the most important swing factor for export growth.
  2. India private-hospital penetration (management target, visible). Mechanism: more reps, clinical staff and products per hospital. Evidence: private revenue grew 22.3% in 2QFY26 and 22.5% in 3QFY26. Timing: continuous.
  3. West Asia normalization (management expectation). Mechanism: pent-up orders ship once routes reopen. Evidence: none yet; -32% in 1QFY27.
  4. Direct models in Brazil and new European markets (management plan, early). Mechanism: own sales teams for new products. Evidence: Medyneo licences and a CEO hire; no revenue yet. Our view: a margin cost before it is a revenue gain.
  5. US (management target, repeatedly deferred). Mechanism: 510(k) re-filings and GPO contracts, plus Citieffe and PendraCare products. Evidence: $3.5-4mn expected in FY27. Our view: immaterial to FY27-FY28 revenue unless tariffs stay at 10% and the re-filed products clear.

Two margin effects follow, in our view. Direct selling raises gross margin but adds fixed sales cost ahead of revenue. The Gulf and container-freight shock raises delivered cost on the export business. Management raised prices at the start of FY27 to offset raw-material inflation, but has not said how much of the freight increase it recovers.

Open questions for management: revenue by country for the top ten markets; West Asia, US and Latin America revenue in rupees; PendraCare's and Citieffe's revenue by region; which subsidiary booked the 4QFY26 provision; whether the China plant closes or renews its licence; the cost and revenue plan for the Brazilian direct model; and the timetable for the next European direct market.

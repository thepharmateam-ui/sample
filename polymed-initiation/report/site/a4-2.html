A4.2 Vascular access and oncology (Plan1Health)

Vascular access and oncology cover the catheters that stay in for days to months rather than hours: central venous catheters, PICCs, midlines, arterial lines, implantable chemotherapy ports and the Huber needles used to reach them. They sell for many times the price of a peripheral cannula and need clinicians trained to place them. Polymed's route in was Plan1Health, a small Italian port and PICC maker bought in two tranches in 2018 and 2019, whose turnover has roughly tripled in euro terms since 2020. Group revenue for the line is [not disclosed]: it sits in "others", and management declines to break oncology out.

What the line includes and how it is used

Specifications show how the line is positioned:

  • Ports tolerate up to 2,000 punctures with a 22G Huber needle. Power versions are rated to 5 ml/s for CT contrast.
  • HealthPICC kits bundle accessories from other makers: a MicroCLAVE connector from ICU Medical and a Grip-Lok securement device from TIDI.
  • The Secureport PLP port was listed among purchased stock-in-trade in the 2024 placement document. At least part of the port range is therefore traded rather than made at the reporting level.

The unit economics differ from infusion. A port patient generates a Huber needle for every chemotherapy cycle and maintenance items for the life of the port, so the installed base of ports drives recurring volume.

Plan1Health: the Italian base

History.

  • The company: Plan1Health, of Amaro in north-east Italy, dates from 1995 on Polymed's slides. The 2024 placement document says it was incorporated in 1990.
  • The purchase: Poly Medicure B.V. paid EUR 3,468,600 and EUR 761,400 on 18 June 2018 and 6 March 2019, booking goodwill of €3.83mn. That is why Polymed's documents date the deal both FY18 (Rs. 342mn, placement document) and 2019 (enterprise value ~€4.2mn, company slide).
  • Scale: it had 31 employees in CY25.

What it makes. Polymed's slides describe Plan1Health as an "integrated developer & seller of long-term infusion devices, oncology products and an innovative pain-control device", a "specialist in long-term implantables" and the "sole approved supplier for one of the largest OEM for Class III implants" (Section A4.10).

  • Europe: it sells through 20+ distributors, with its whole portfolio of four categories approved.
  • US: its only clearance is the Painfusor catheter (K111031, 2011), which the company describes as an approval held for an OEM.
  • Italy: it sells through distributors, like the rest of its European business; the group's direct sales presence in Italy, its only direct market in Europe, belongs to Citieffe (Section A4.9). Plan1Health's stated plan is more Italian and EU tenders and new device launches in Italy.

Growth and margins. Plan1Health's revenue fell 5% in the COVID year, then grew 30% (FY22), ~50% (9MFY24) and 40%+ (FY24). Management said it was still growing 30-35% in Feb-2026.

The margin record is less clear, as Exhibit 26 shows. Management has put the EBITDA margin at 16-17% for FY24 and 16-17% in Nov-2025, yet its own acquisition slide shows 10.9% for CY24 and 13.4% for CY25. The statutory Italian accounts give an EBIT margin of 10.2% in CY25 (our calculation) and net profit of €681k. We use the slide figures, which fit the statutory EBIT, and treat the higher figures as management rounding or a different definition.

The company's own slides also disagree on the margin gain since the acquisition: 970bps (1QFY27 deck), ~670bps (Sep-2026 AGM deck) and ~600bps (4QFY26 deck). The 1QFY27 deck also claims 3.2x revenue and 11.8x EBITDA growth since 2020.

Links with the parent.

  • Trade: Polymed sold goods worth Rs. 147mn to Plan1Health in FY26.
  • Management: Plan1Health's chief executive, Alessandro Balboni (formerly chief executive of Delta Med SpA and Paul Hartmann SpA), also runs Polymed's EMEA business and sits on its board as a non-independent director. He was paid management fees of Rs. 29mn in FY26 (Section A9).
  • The "P1H playbook": management presents Plan1Health as the model for PendraCare and Citieffe: faster launches, manufacturing moved to India to cut cost, direct sales, wider distribution and joint R&D.

Oncology in Vision 2030 and in India

Management lists oncology among the five focus areas for the next five years, with orthopaedics, cardiology, neonatology and renal care, and says the shift "helps us to even get a better gross margin".

The Indian plan has four strands:

  • Local manufacture. Oncology products are sold in India through the critical-care team. Management says "a lot of products which we are making in Italy today" will be shifted to India, where it says demand is 80-90% imported.
  • Higher-class devices. Chemoports, a Class III implantable, have been launched in India. Management frames this as a shift from short-term devices to "longer term use devices" in Class III, with competition mostly from multinationals and "a couple of good Indian companies also like Meril".
  • Public cancer care. The government is setting up standalone specialty oncology (day-care) centres: 200 in FY26 and 700 over three years, on management's account.
  • New products. Recent launches include neonatal PICCs of 1Fr & 2Fr ("one of the first" made in India), an antimicrobial CVC, tear-away introducers, midlines, PICC-ports and a safety Huber needle.

Market and competition

Commercial research firms (vendor estimates, used as indications only) put these global markets at:

  • implantable ports: $1.4bn in 2025;
  • PICCs: $1.43bn in 2026;
  • central venous catheters: $3.07bn in 2026;
  • catheter introducer sheaths: $1.93bn by 2030.

Their company lists put B. Braun, BD, Cook Medical, AngioDynamics, Smiths Medical and Teleflex in ports and PICCs. Poly Medicure is listed among PICC and CVC suppliers.

The large competitors are:

  • BD: ports sit within its Peripheral Intervention unit ($2.0bn revenue in FY25), and PICCs and midlines within Medication Delivery Solutions.
  • Teleflex: its Vascular product category, mainly Arrow-branded catheters plus tip-positioning and intraosseous systems, generated $917.7mn in CY25.
  • ICU Medical: it sells Port-A-Cath ports, PowerWand midlines and Deltec Gripper non-coring needles within a consumables business of $1.1bn.

In India, management names multinationals and Meril as the competitors in oncology implants.

We see Plan1Health's advantage as regulatory, not scale. It holds MDR approval for its whole portfolio and supplies a Class III OEM, and management says each country registration takes two to three years. Polymed's contribution is lower-cost manufacturing and a distribution network in 125+ countries.

Regulation specific to the line

  • Class III. Ports are Class III implants, the highest risk class in Europe. Management says Class III devices take "very much, much longer to register", often two to three years including clinical trials.
  • Italian payback. A 2015 Italian law requires device suppliers to pay the government when regional device spending exceeds annual ceilings; the Constitutional Court upheld it in Jul-2024. BD booked a $62mn accrual for it in FY24 and settled its 2015-2018 obligations at 25% of invoiced amounts under a Jun-2025 decree. Italian public spending on medical devices and services is about €10bn a year, according to the industry association Confindustria Dispositivi Medici (2025 edition of its sector figures). Plan1Health plans to bid in more Italian tenders and Citieffe sells direct in Italy, so we infer some group exposure; it is [not disclosed].
  • EU procurement. Since Jun-2025 the EU has excluded Chinese bidders from public medical-device tenders worth €5mn or more (Section A4.1). For Plan1Health, which plans to bid in more Italian and EU tenders, that removes Chinese rivals from the larger ones (our view).
  • US. Beyond Painfusor, Plan1Health is "evaluating product submissions for FDA approvals"; no filing has been disclosed.

Growth and margin drivers, ranked

  1. India import substitution in ports and PICCs (management target; our view: largest long-run lever). The drivers are chemotherapy volumes, the day-care cancer centres and hospitals preferring local suppliers as insurance spreads. Timing depends on moving Plan1Health products to Indian manufacture and approval.
  2. Plan1Health's European growth (management: 30-35% a year). The company's stated levers are more tenders in Italy and other EU markets and new device launches in Italy; Plan1Health has 1 product in the European approval pipeline.
  3. Recurring consumables per port (our view). Huber needles and maintenance items follow the installed base of ports.
  4. Margin: scale and Indian manufacturing (company target). The company-reported EBITDA margin rose from 3.7% to 13.4% as revenue tripled. The company lists using India manufacturing to reduce cost among the "repeatable plays" already run at Plan1Health, and management plans to shift "a lot of" the products made in Italy to India.
  5. Risk: Italian payback and tender pricing (our view). These could offset part of the gain; the exposure is [not disclosed].

What to watch

Open questions for management (also in the question bank):

  • oncology and vascular-access revenue at group level;
  • which Plan1Health products are made in Italy, bought in, or being moved to India;
  • the definition behind management's higher EBITDA margin figures for Plan1Health;
  • any Italian payback accruals;
  • the identity and terms of the Class III OEM customer.

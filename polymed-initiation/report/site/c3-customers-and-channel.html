Customers and channel

Q71Must-askCustomers and channel

What share of revenue is OEM or private label today, who are the largest OEM customers by product line, and what are the contract terms – tenor, exclusivity and price resets?

Why it matters
OEM supply gives volume but ties pricing to a few buyers; the split is not disclosed (Section A4.10).
Our context
Management put the OEM/private-label share at 28% in FY22; top-10 customers were 29.0% of FY24 revenue; no customer reached the 10% disclosure threshold in FY26.
Listen for
Reassuring: diversified OEM customers on multi-year contracts. Concerning: one or two global customers with annual price resets.
Follow-up
Are any OEM customers also competitors in your own-brand markets?

Q72If time permitsCustomers and channel

In India, how is revenue split between distributors, direct hospital accounts and government tenders, and how has that changed?

Why it matters
The channel mix drives exposure to the trade-margin debate and to government payment cycles (Section B1).
Our context
Private-sector revenue grew 25% in 1QFY26 while government business fell 10%.
Listen for
Reassuring: rate contracts with large chains and fewer, larger distributors. Concerning: dependence on a few super-distributors.
Follow-up
How many hospitals do you cover directly?

Q73If time permitsCustomers and channel

What level of inventory cover do you regard as normal for your European distributors, and how do you monitor it – through sell-out reporting, order patterns or estimates?

Why it matters
Inventory in the channel was the main stated cause of the FY26 slowdown; without visibility of sell-out, the next destocking cycle would again surprise.
Our context
Management said European distributors cut inventory cover from 4-5 months to 2-3 months in 1HFY26.
Listen for
Reassuring: contractual sell-out reporting from the larger distributors. Concerning: estimates from order patterns only.
Follow-up
Which distributors share sell-out data with you today?

Q74If time permitsCustomers and channel

What are payment terms by region, and how do you manage credit risk with distributors in emerging markets?

Why it matters
Working capital is structurally high for an exporter selling through distributors.
Our context
Receivable days were 73 days in FY24.
Listen for
Reassuring: credit insurance and letters of credit in higher-risk markets. Concerning: extended terms to win share.
Follow-up
What were bad-debt write-offs over the last three years?

Q75If time permitsCustomers and channel

Your OEM and CDMO pipeline: what did the two contract-manufacturing agreements signed in 1QFY26 cover, and how large could that business become?

Why it matters
CDMO is a potential new revenue line using existing plants.
Our context
Management reported two contract-manufacturing agreements in 1QFY26.
Listen for
Reassuring: named product categories and customers' regulatory filings. Concerning: pilot projects without volume.
Follow-up
Do CDMO customers fund tooling and validation?

Q76If time permitsCustomers and channel

How do OEM and private-label margins compare with own-brand margins, and who are the largest OEM customers?

Why it matters
The OEM share and its margin gap are undisclosed but decide the value of the mix shift (Section A4.10).
Our context
Management put own brand at 72% and OEM at 28% in FY22, with a target of as close to 80% own brand; CRISIL named "Atroge and Asept" among the big players Polymed supplies.
Listen for
Reassuring: a narrow margin gap and diversified customers. Concerning: OEM priced near cost.
Follow-up
Has the OEM share changed since FY22?

Q77If time permitsCustomers and channel

How large is the components business – catheters, guidewires, introducers, stopcocks and tubing sold to other device makers – and who buys from it?

Why it matters
The size and customers of the business-to-business components line are not disclosed (Section A4.10).
Our context
The components catalogue lists the range, but revenue is [not disclosed].
Listen for
Reassuring: a revenue range and repeat customers. Concerning: occasional sales.
Follow-up
Does the components business overlap with PendraCare's OEM work?

Q78If time permitsCustomers and channel

What are revenue and growth for your top ten export countries, and which countries account for most of European revenue?

Why it matters
Only regional splits are disclosed; country concentration drives risk (Section A5).
Our context
The UK alone was 5-6% of revenue in FY25, per management.
Listen for
Reassuring: a diversified country list. Concerning: dependence on a few distributors in two or three countries.
Follow-up
How many NHS contracts did you win in the 2026 cycle?

Q79If time permitsCustomers and channel

Which European market will be your next direct-sales market, when, and with what headcount and first-year cost?

Why it matters
Direct selling raises margin but adds fixed cost (Section A5).
Our context
Management has said it plans direct presence in more European countries, following Italy.
Listen for
Reassuring: a country, a date and a payback plan. Concerning: an open-ended plan.
Follow-up
Will you buy distributors to go direct?

Q80If time permitsCustomers and channel

The US revenue target of $15-20mn has been rolled forward since 2024. What is the new timeline, and which products carry it?

Why it matters
A repeatedly deferred target tests the US strategy (Section A5).
Our context
US exports are running at $3.5-4mn a year.
Listen for
Reassuring: product-level volumes from GPO contracts. Concerning: the target restated without a plan.
Follow-up
Do you need a US warehouse or US-based distribution?

Q81If time permitsCustomers and channel

Which subsidiary booked the one-off provision of about Rs. 90mn in 4QFY26, and what was it for?

Why it matters
Management described it in different ways on the call (Section A5).
Our context
The provision hit 4QFY26 consolidated margins.
Listen for
Reassuring: a named, non-recurring item. Concerning: a recurring cost in an acquired business.
Follow-up
Are further provisions expected as integration proceeds?

Q82If time permitsCustomers and channel

What does the Egyptian associate Ultra for Medical Products make, how much do you sell to it, and would you raise the stake?

Why it matters
A profitable 23% associate that serves Africa (Section A5).
Our context
Its CY25 revenue was Rs. 1.2bn, and it paid Polymed Rs. 33mn in FY26.
Listen for
Reassuring: a clear strategic role. Concerning: a legacy holding without a plan.
Follow-up
Does the associate compete with Polymed in any export market?

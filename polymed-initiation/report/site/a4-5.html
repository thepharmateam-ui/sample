A4.5 Diagnostics (blood collection)

Diagnostics at Polymed means venous blood collection (Haemochek vacuum tubes, needles, holders and a safety butterfly set), sold mainly in India to laboratories and hospitals. Management pushed the line in 2021-2022 and opened a dedicated plant in FY24, but has not mentioned it on a results call since Oct-2024; its revenue, undisclosed since FY22, sits inside the BRSR's "Blood Management & Blood Collection" family with blood bags (Section A4.4).

Diagnostics at a glance

Products and the unit of demand

Haemochek tubes are PET and colour-coded by additive, and so by test: serum and gel-separator tubes for chemistry, citrate for coagulation, K2 or K3 EDTA for blood counts, heparin for plasma chemistry, fluoride for glucose, plus ESR and CPDA (blood-grouping) tubes. A multi-sample needle (20G to 23G, including the HaemoFlash flashback version) in a standard holder fills several tubes from one venepuncture; the Nuovo Safe winged set, with a locking needle shield, is for small or difficult veins.

Demand is one needle per venepuncture and a tube per group of tests, so volumes follow laboratory test counts. The catalogues list no lancets; the heparinized blood-gas syringe (80 IU in the 3 ml size) is sold with infusion products, and the Aug-2025 deck still shows a VTM kit under diagnostics.

How the line was built, and whether Polymed makes the tubes

  • Early range. Tubes, needles, safety sets and a holder were in the FY16 product list. The Oct-2019 deck, the only one to split the line out, put diagnostics at 3% of sales (blood transfusion 9%).
  • COVID. When tubes ran short, laboratories reliant on imports turned to Polymed, which "had the production of these items" (MD, Jul-2021). VTM kits and respiratory products added ~Rs. 200mn to 1QFY22 domestic sales, demand that was gone by FY23.
  • The 2021-2022 push. A diagnostics head (Nov-2021), dedicated distributors and new test kits were meant to "fill the same pipe" into laboratories already buying Polymed's tubes, with equipment to follow.
  • PLI. By Feb-2022 Polymed had approval under the IVD category of the PLI scheme for pharmaceuticals (renal is in the separate medical-devices scheme): 5% of incremental sales from FY23, for a committed Rs. 500mn of investment over FY22-FY28. Approved products are blood-collection tubes, viral transport media, rapid tests (HIV, hepatitis, malaria, dengue, typhoid) and an RNA extraction kit; the FY22 annual report counts 5 IVD applicants among 55 selected.
  • Plant. The first of four new plants, in Faridabad, started production in 1QFY24; management confirmed it was for diagnostics.

Made or bought. The Feb-2021 placement document lists tubes among products "manufactured", yet the Aug-2024 one names blood-collection tubes first among traded goods in FY22-FY24. FY22 traded sales (Rs. 88mn, including dialysis machines) were below even the lowest FY22 diagnostics figure management gave (~Rs. 150mn), so part of the range was already made in-house (our inference). Since the new plant opened, traded sales have roughly halved (Exhibit 52). We read this as the remaining bought-in tubes moving in-house from FY24, though the company has not named its later traded items.

What has been disclosed

Besides traded goods, Exhibit 53 lists every figure given. The PLI incentive is the only later pointer to scale: at the 5% rate, the ~Rs. 14mn received for FY24 implies ~Rs. 280mn of eligible sales above the scheme's base year (our calculation). It is not revenue, since base-year sales are undisclosed; in Feb-2022 the MD had expected ~Rs. 15mn a year.

Market and customers

Demand follows test volumes, which are low in India. Frost & Sullivan (Oct-2025, in the Integris Medtech prospectus) puts yearly diagnostic tests per capita at less than 5 in India and China against more than 20 in the US, and sees standalone laboratories giving way to chains, citing revenue CAGRs of 12.5% for Dr. Lal PathLabs (2019-2024) and 11.5% for Metropolis (2015-2024). CRISIL (commissioned by Polymed) sizes India's IVD and reagents segment at Rs. 82.0bn in FY24, growing 12-13% a year to FY28. We found no size for India's tube market.

Customers. In May-2021 the MD named Thyrocare, Metropolis, Dr. Lal PathLabs, SRL and Pathkind among its laboratory customers, adding Vijay Diagnostics in Feb-2022. Prices and customer concentration are [not disclosed].

Global. 360iResearch projects the tube market at $4.64bn in 2026 and $7.23bn by 2032, a 7.3% CAGR (vendor estimate). Filed numbers grow more slowly: BD's Specimen Management unit (Vacutainer tubes, needles, wingsets, blood-gas devices) sold $1.9bn in FY25, up 2.0% despite a decline in China.

Competition

BD's move of Specimen Management into Medical Essentials, alongside its infusion consumables, reads to us as treating blood collection as a consumables business, as Polymed does; Polymed's MD calls vacuum tubes "vacutainers".

Against them Polymed offers local manufacture, public-procurement preference and a full collection basket, but claims no market share.

Regulation

  • India. Class A and B devices have needed licences under the Medical Devices Rules since 1 Oct 2022; management said in Aug-2022 that most of its devices are Class B. For incentives and procurement the tubes count as IVD products: the Department of Pharmaceuticals' Oct-2024 order deeming PLI makers Class-II local suppliers lists them under the pharmaceuticals scheme.
  • Exports. Under EU-style rules tubes are IVD "specimen receptacles". Saudi Arabia's SFDA defines them as devices "whether of a vacuum-type or not" and puts them in its lowest IVD class (Class A), and the IVDR applies in Europe, where Polymed had no diagnostics business in May-2022.

Growth and margin drivers, ranked

  1. Test volumes and chain consolidation (structural; our view). More draws, bought by chains Polymed already supplies.
  2. Cross-selling kits through the tube channel (management strategy, 2021-2022). No result has been disclosed, and the MD conceded "there are many companies for diagnostic products".
  3. Public procurement (policy). Deemed local-supplier status helps in government laboratory tenders; its value is [not disclosed].
  4. Margin: make rather than buy (our inference). Own output should earn more than traded tubes, and PLI adds 5% on eligible incremental sales; plant utilization is [not disclosed]. In May-2021 the MD expected margins similar to the core business.
  5. Margin: price (our view). Tubes are a commodity that Chinese makers win on price at home; import pricing is the main risk to the line's margin.

What to watch

Open questions (question bank): line revenue since FY22; products still bought in; plant capacity and utilization; FY25-FY26 PLI claims; the kit range; export registrations.

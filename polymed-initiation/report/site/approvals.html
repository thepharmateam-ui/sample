Regulatory approvals by market

Approvals decide where Polymed can sell, and they are slow to obtain. The company says its products are registered in 100+ countries and that it employs 200+ quality and regulatory professionals. Management said in May 2022 that a CE mark had come to take 18-24 months, and in May 2026 it put the time saved by buying a Brazilian company with licences in hand at 18-24 months. This appendix lists the approvals we could verify in regulator databases and company filings, market by market.

United States: 510(k) clearances

The MD calls the US "a very high price market". Devices that need FDA clearance reach it through the 510(k) route, in which the FDA finds a device substantially equivalent to one already on the market; every decision in the table below is of that kind. Polymed's own clearances are all infusion devices and all date from 2023 onwards; the older clearances came with Plan1Health, PendraCare and Citieffe.

What the list shows:

  • The count. The FDA database holds more 510(k) records for the four applicants than the US approvals the company cites (Exhibit 191).
  • Reconciling the two. The May-2026 deck splits the company's count by entity and says only that PendraCare's guiding catheter is approved (Exhibit 191). Polymed's and Citieffe's figures equal their Traditional submissions in the table, so we read the company's count as leaving out Special 510(k)s and PendraCare's clearances.
  • Recent additions. Since Apr 2025 the group has added the Primum guiding catheter, the Estremo fibular nail, Special 510(k)s for the Polyshield catheter and the Polywin Safety size range, and PendraCare's SimplGuide introducer.
  • A testing setback. In Nov 2025 the MD said the FDA had stopped accepting test results from Indian laboratories, so Polymed had to retest its products in the US: "all work we have done in the past was negated". The Aug-2026 deck still showed 11-13 US approvals in the pipeline.
  • The quality rule. From 2 Feb 2026 the FDA's device quality rule incorporates ISO 13485 by reference, the standard to which every Indian plant was certified at the Aug-2024 placement.

United States: establishment registration and device listing

The registration and listing records show which Polymed plants are registered with the FDA, which devices each is set up to ship to the US, and, through proprietary names, some of the private-label supply.

The records show:

  • A clean record. The database holds 19 listings under the Polymed name, and openFDA returned 0 recall or enforcement records for the company.
  • Private label. The suction catheter kit listed at Jaipur carries the proprietary name "McKesson", which the MD has named among the large US distributors and purchasing organizations; we read it as private-label supply.
  • Two clearances without a listing under the Polymed name. The Polyfusion IV sets (K220312) and the Nouvo Safety Set (K232234) do not appear in the listings retrieved under the Polymed name, while the listing for the Polyshield catheter cleared under K252677 was created on 10 Sep 2026. We could not tell whether the first two are listed under another firm's registration or not yet shipped, and we add the point to our questions.

European Union: MDR and CE marking

Europe produced 32% of FY26 revenue, and a device sold there needs a CE mark. Depending on its risk class, the maker must involve an independent notified body before it can affix the mark. When the Medical Device Regulation, EU Regulation 2017/745, took effect on 26 May 2021, the number of designated notified bodies was very low, which the European Commission says created bottlenecks in pre-market certification. Management said in Feb 2026 that Polymed "had to drop many products initially in the bargain because it was taking a long time to get the approvals".

The FY26 annual report does not name the notified body behind the MDR certificates. The company's EU product count has not moved in a straight line, and the counts are not on a consistent basis: "medical device products" certified in one deck, "approved" products in another and an approximate figure on a call. None of the disclosures says whether a product is a family, a code or a certificate.

The transition rules set the deadline for any directive-era certificates still in use:

  • Transition. Under EU Regulation 2023/607, devices certified under the old directives may stay on the market until 31 Dec 2027 for class III and implantable class IIb devices and until 31 Dec 2028 for other classes, provided the maker had applied for MDR certification by 26 May 2024 and signed with a notified body by 26 Sep 2024.
  • Revision. The Commission proposed a targeted revision of the MDR in Dec 2025, and the European Parliament's health committee began work on it on 14 Jul 2026.
  • The acquired makers. Citieffe says its whole portfolio is already certified under MDR, while PendraCare's products carry a mix of directive and MDR approvals; PendraCare's acquisition call described EU approvals as "extremely challenging" to obtain under MDR.

Plant quality certificates: ISO 13485, MDSAP and Korea GMP

ISO 13485 is the device quality-system standard that the FDA's quality rule incorporates from 2 Feb 2026. MDSAP certification covers Brazil, the US and Australia, and the MD said it "fast tracks our registration process".

Plant names follow the Aug-2024 placement document. The certificates for the FY26 facilities, the new Haridwar plant and the gamma sterilization facility, come from the FY26 annual report; Citieffe's plant certificates are not disclosed. By the company's count, ~70% of its facilities held ISO 14001 in Aug 2026.

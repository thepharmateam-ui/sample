Poly Medicure (POLYMED): plant-visit question bank

Questions for management on the business, its drivers, the industry and competition, with context and what to listen for

Draft as of 24 Sep 2026Data to the 1QFY27 results and the 31st AGMScope business and industry; no forecasts or valuation

Section references (A1-A9, B1-B5) point to the initiation report. Every number in a context line is taken from a filing, presentation or call transcript; hover over it to see the source. The notes area under each question is lined for handwriting when printed.

How to use this bank

  1. Short meeting of about an hour. Work through the must-ask list below, in order. It covers strategy, the FY26 reset, the four core lines, customers, competition, policy, the plant, margins and governance.
  2. Full plant visit. Add the "if time permits" questions for the business-line and plant heads you meet. They are grouped by theme in report order.
  3. On the floor. Use the observation checklist at the end to test what you are told against what you see.
  4. Afterwards. Compare the answers with the context line on each card, and note any answer that changes a driver in Section A7 of the report.

The question bank

This bank turns the open issues in the initiation report into questions for a meeting with management during a plant visit. Each question card gives:

  • why the question matters, meaning the driver or disclosure gap it tests;
  • one line of context from our research;
  • what a reassuring or a concerning answer sounds like;
  • a follow-up.

It has three layers:

  • The must-ask list (below) is for a short meeting of about an hour.
  • The full bank is for a day-long visit with time for business-line heads, grouped by theme in the same order as the report.
  • The floor-walk checklist at the end lists things to observe rather than ask.

Compliance: ask about structure, not the current quarter

Listed-company managements cannot share unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI) under the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015.

  • What the questions do. They ask about business structure, strategy, economics and data the company has already disclosed.
  • What they avoid. Current-quarter trading, order-book numbers, unannounced transactions and changes to guidance.
  • If an answer strays into UPSI, stop the line of questioning and note it.
  • Numbers in the context lines come from public filings, presentations and call transcripts cited in this report.

Strategy and Vision 2030

Q01Must-askStrategy

Vision 2030 targets 2x revenue by FY30. How much of that do you expect from today's portfolio and plants, how much from the three new plants, and how much from acquisitions not yet made?

Why it matters
Separates organic capability from dependence on M&A. The answer tests every growth driver in Section A7.
Our context
FY26 revenue was Rs. 18.8bn, of which organic growth was 5.4% (our calculation). FY27 guidance is Rs. 23-24bn including the acquired businesses.
Listen for
Reassuring: a bottom-up bridge by therapy and region with capacity and approvals behind it. Concerning: a top-down number that leans on unnamed acquisitions.
Follow-up
Which two therapy lines have to beat plan for the target to hold?

Q02If time permitsStrategy

In mid-2024 you said the focus would stay on the existing segments; within about a year you had bought into cardiology catheters and trauma implants. What changed in your view of where Polymed should play?

Why it matters
Tests the consistency of the capital-allocation framework behind the acquisitions (Section A9).
Our context
Management's stated criterion is technology and regulatory approvals in adjacencies, because organic development takes 3-5 years.
Listen for
Reassuring: a clear filter (disease burden, approvals, manufacturing transferability) that also explains the deals rejected. Concerning: opportunism, or "we saw a good price".
Follow-up
Which adjacency would you not enter, and why?

Q03If time permitsStrategy

Between a low-cost volume manufacturer and a branded therapy company, where do you want Polymed to sit in 2030, and what share of revenue should be own-brand?

Why it matters
Positioning determines pricing power, margins and channel strategy for every line.
Our context
CRISIL puts own-brand sales at 70% of revenue; management put the OEM/private-label share at 28% in FY22.
Listen for
Reassuring: a deliberate plan to grow branded share in higher-value lines while keeping OEM for scale. Concerning: no view, or a view that ignores CRISIL's point that India's low-cost image limits premiums.
Follow-up
In which product lines do you already command a price premium over Chinese makers?

Q04If time permitsStrategy

Which region carries the most weight in Vision 2030 – India, Europe, the US or the rest of the world – and why?

Why it matters
Regions differ sharply in pricing, regulation and working capital.
Our context
Company-stated FY20-FY26 CAGRs: India 21%, Europe 20%, rest of world 15%. US exports are only $3.5-4mn a year.
Listen for
Reassuring: specific channel and approval plans for the US and for direct European markets. Concerning: the US treated as upside without a route to market.
Follow-up
What would it take for the US to become a double-digit share of revenue?

Q05If time permitsStrategy

You are building direct sales in Italy, Brazil, the UK and the US. What mix of direct versus distributor sales do you want by 2030, and what does going direct do to margins and working capital?

Why it matters
Going direct raises gross margin but adds fixed cost and receivables, a trade-off not visible in guidance.
Our context
The group has 590 direct-sales representatives across India, the US, Italy and Mexico, and bought Medyneo for its Brazilian licences.
Listen for
Reassuring: country-by-country payback periods. Concerning: direct selling pursued for control without a cost plan.
Follow-up
Which distributor relationships would you have to unwind, and at what cost?

Q06If time permitsStrategy

How do you decide whether to develop a product in-house or buy it, and how will the plan to double R&D spend change that balance?

Why it matters
R&D intensity is low against global peers, and it sets how fast the product range can move up the value chain.
Our context
R&D was 1.6% of revenue in FY26; management plans to double R&D spend over three to four years; the pipeline has 100+ products.
Listen for
Reassuring: named platforms, clinical programmes and a view on which regulatory markets each targets. Concerning: launch counts without revenue targets.
Follow-up
What share of FY26 revenue came from products launched in the last three years?

The FY26 reset and the recovery

Q07Must-askFY26 reset

Looking back at FY26, how much of the export shortfall was distributor destocking and how much was lost share or lower prices – and how do you know?

Why it matters
Decides whether FY26 was a cycle or a change in the export franchise (Section A3).
Our context
Standalone revenue grew only 4% in FY26 against consolidated growth guidance of 20%. Management cited destocking, faster Suez transit, Chinese product diverted to Europe and MDR renewals.
Listen for
Reassuring: named customers who destocked and have since reordered at similar prices, plus a volume-versus-price split. Concerning: a generic reference to market conditions.
Follow-up
Did you lose any customer in FY26, and to whom?

Q08If time permitsFY26 reset

What do the new European customers you are adding look like – tender wins, distributor switches or OEM programmes – and on what contract lengths?

Why it matters
New-customer quality determines whether the European recovery is durable.
Our context
Europe grew 17.6% organically in 1QFY27; management said it was adding 5-6 new European customers from April 2026.
Listen for
Reassuring: multi-year contracts with minimum volumes. Concerning: spot orders at lower prices.
Follow-up
How do the prices on these new contracts compare with the customers who destocked?

Q09If time permitsFY26 reset

What did the EU MDR transition cost you in dropped products and delayed certificates, and how much of the range is still waiting for certification?

Why it matters
MDR is a gate for European revenue and a moat against smaller competitors (Section B4).
Our context
Polymed received MDR certification for 89 products in FY25 and expected 15 products more within six months as of Feb-2026.
Listen for
Reassuring: a certificate schedule by notified body with dates. Concerning: open-ended waits or products abandoned.
Follow-up
Which competitors dropped products under MDR, and did you pick up their customers?

Q10If time permitsFY26 reset

West Asia is 6-8% of revenue. How are you serving the region while logistics are disrupted, and does the business come back at the same prices once routes reopen?

Why it matters
A regional shock with a working-capital tail; it also bears on PendraCare's West Asia exposure.
Our context
West Asia revenue fell 32% in 1QFY27 with orders held at port; management expects growth of 20-25% once the situation normalizes.
Listen for
Reassuring: orders deferred rather than cancelled, and no receivable stress. Concerning: distributors in financial difficulty.
Follow-up
What share of PendraCare's revenue goes to West Asia?

Q11If time permitsFY26 reset

Freight costs rose sharply. How much of the increase came from rates, how much from mode (air versus sea) and how much from volume, and what is a normal freight-to-revenue ratio for Polymed?

Why it matters
Freight is a swing margin line for a company that exports about two-thirds of its output.
Our context
Standalone freight was Rs. 334mn in FY26, against Rs. 245mn in FY25 and Rs. 113mn in FY24.
Listen for
Reassuring: contract rates, and customers bearing freight under their incoterms. Concerning: air freight used to recover service levels.
Follow-up
What share of export sales is on CIF versus FOB terms?

Q12If time permitsFY26 reset

How has FY26 changed the way you set and communicate guidance?

Why it matters
Guidance credibility matters for how the market reads the FY27 and FY30 targets (Section A3).
Our context
The upper end of FY27 standalone revenue guidance moved from Rs. 19.5bn to Rs. 20.0bn while being described as maintained.
Listen for
Reassuring: explicit assumptions (FX, acquisitions, West Asia) and ranges. Concerning: guidance defended as unchanged when it has moved.
Follow-up
Which single assumption carries the most risk in FY27 guidance?

Infusion therapy

Q13Must-askInfusion

What share of your IV cannula volume is safety-engineered today in each region, and what price premium does a safety cannula carry over a conventional one?

Why it matters
The shift to safety cannulas is the main price and margin lever in the largest line (Section A4.1).
Our context
Polymed is the world's third-largest IV cannula maker, with about 11% global share and 600mn units a year.
Listen for
Reassuring: rising safety mix with a stable premium, and passive designs that you own. Concerning: safety cannulas priced at parity to win tenders.
Follow-up
Which safety designs (passive or active) are growing fastest, and do you hold the patents?

Q14Must-askInfusion

Prefilled saline flush syringes were 10.2% of FY26 standalone turnover. Where do you sell them, what capacity and utilization does the syringe line run at, and how do US duties on Chinese syringes change the opportunity?

Why it matters
A large product family that management rarely discusses, and a possible beneficiary of US trade action against Chinese syringes (Sections A4.1 and B4).
Our context
Launched in Nov-2020; management claimed 25-30% of the Indian market in May-2022, and the BRSR implies about Rs. 1.7bn of FY26 revenue (our calculation).
Listen for
Reassuring: US and European contracts with committed volumes, and the capacity to add lines. Concerning: flush syringes sold mainly as price-led tender volume.
Follow-up
None of Polymed's FDA listings is a syringe: when will you file a US 510(k) for PolyFlush, and do you make barrels and plungers in-house?

Q15If time permitsInfusion

How have realized prices per cannula moved over the last three years in Europe and in India, and what share of volume is under multi-year contracts?

Why it matters
Price erosion on mature products is offset by volume and cost; the balance determines infusion margins.
Our context
Management gave Indian price points of Rs. 10-20 a unit for mass products; the placement document describes selling prices that fall over a product's life.
Listen for
Reassuring: annual price resets linked to raw-material indices. Concerning: repeated price cuts to hold share against China.
Follow-up
Where did you walk away from a tender on price in the last year?

Q16If time permitsInfusion

What stops the US from being a much larger market for your infusion range: FDA clearances, group purchasing organization (GPO) contracts, OEM agreements or tariffs?

Why it matters
The US is the largest infusion market, and Polymed barely participates (Sections A4.1 and A5).
Our context
US exports run at $3.5-4mn a year. The tariff on Indian devices fell from 50% in Aug-2025 to 10% in Aug-2026.
Listen for
Reassuring: named GPO or OEM discussions and a US regulatory plan. Concerning: waiting for tariffs to fall.
Follow-up
How many 510(k)s do you need to offer a full US infusion catalogue?

Q17If time permitsInfusion

Needle-free connectors, closed systems, stopcocks and disinfecting caps: are they sold as kits with cannulas, and do they carry better margins than the cannula itself?

Why it matters
Accessory attach rates are a mix lever inside infusion.
Our context
The domestic catalogue offers needle-free connectors, closed IV catheters and the Polyswab disinfecting cap alongside cannulas.
Listen for
Reassuring: hospital-level bundles with measurable attach rates. Concerning: accessories sold only on distributor demand.
Follow-up
Which accessory has grown fastest over the last two years?

Q18If time permitsInfusion

What is your cost per cannula against the leading Chinese producers, and where does the gap come from – automation, needle-making, sterilization or raw materials?

Why it matters
The cost position is the core of the moat in commodity infusion (Section A8).
Our context
Management says it has 530 automatic assembly machines and 130 robots; CRISIL attributes margins to lower labour costs and in-house tooling.
Listen for
Reassuring: a quantified cost advantage by process step. Concerning: parity on cost with an advantage only on quality or service.
Follow-up
How has the gap moved since Chinese makers faced higher US duties?

Q19If time permitsInfusion

What were IV cannula revenue and volumes in FY24, FY25 and FY26, and how do they split between conventional, safety, blood-control and closed-system catheters?

Why it matters
The cannula is the anchor product but has no regular disclosure; the mix drives price per unit (Section A4.1).
Our context
The MD put cannulas at 25%-30% of revenue in Jul-2024 and volumes at about 600mn units a year.
Listen for
Reassuring: a volume figure with a rising safety share. Concerning: conventional export volumes falling.
Follow-up
Which cannula family grew fastest in FY26?

Q20If time permitsInfusion

What is the basis for the 11% global IV catheter share and the third-place ranking – volume or value, which competitors and which year?

Why it matters
A share claim that cannot be sourced weakens the scale argument (Section A8).
Our context
600mn units a year at 11% implies a market of about 5.5bn units (our calculation), far above the 1.5bn units a year cited in a 2024 clinical-trial record.
Listen for
Reassuring: a named data source and a definition that includes or excludes China. Concerning: an internal estimate.
Follow-up
Who are the two makers ranked above you?

Q21If time permitsInfusion

Why do annual infusion revenue figures sit below the sum of the four quarters, and why does the BRSR's infusion share imply a larger business than the annual report's segment table?

Why it matters
Growth rates and segment size depend on which classification is used (Sections A1 and A4.1).
Our context
The gap between the quarterly and annual infusion figures was Rs. 399mn in FY26; the BRSR implies Rs. 11.6bn against Rs. 10.0bn in the segment table (our calculations).

Reassuring: a reconciliation and a stable definition. Concerning: products moved between segments without disclosure.
Follow-up
Which products sit in infusion in the BRSR but in "others" in the presentations?

Q22If time permitsInfusion

What volumes and prices are the two US group purchasing organization contracts and the US distributor contract delivering, and how are they affected by the Section 301 duty?

Why it matters
US revenue has lagged targets since 2022 despite several clearances (Section A5).
Our context
Management said in Aug-2025 that it had signed contracts with two large GPOs and distributors; Indian goods have paid a 10% Section 301 duty since 24-Jul-2026.

Q23If time permitsInfusion

What will the polymer-tubing project supported by the government's MIS-RID scheme make, when does it start, and which imports does it replace?

Renal care

Q30Must-askRenal

Management says Chinese dialysers enter India duty-free, paying 5% GST, while Polymed pays customs duty of 2.5% on imported raw materials and 18% GST. What is the landed price gap per dialyser today, and what happens to renal pricing if the anti-dumping investigation into dialysers from China and Malaysia does not lead to duties?

Why it matters
Dialyser pricing sets renal margins and the payback on the PLI-backed capacity (Section A4.3).
Our context
Polymed filed the petition (initiated 25-Jun-2026) and claims an injury margin of 20%. It told DGTR that the domestic industry made financial and cash losses and a negative return on capital in CY25. Renal revenue grew -3.8% yoy in 1QFY27 as Polymed chose price over volume.
Listen for
Reassuring: cost-down plans that close the gap without duties. Concerning: a strategy that depends on the trade remedy.
Follow-up
Which customers switched to Chinese dialysers, and have any come back?

Q31Must-askRenal

What is the annual consumables pull-through per installed dialysis machine – bloodlines, fistula needles and dialysers – and what share of machines is sold outright versus placed against consumable commitments?

Why it matters
The razor-and-blade logic is the case for the machine business; placement terms decide its capital intensity.
Our context
Polymed placed 450 machines in FY26 against 500-600 guided, taking the installed base to about 1,000 machines. It had bid for 600 machines by Feb-2026.
Listen for
Reassuring: contractual consumable commitments and measured pull-through per machine. Concerning: machines sold at low margins without consumable lock-in.
Follow-up
How many service engineers support the installed base, and what uptime do you commit to?

Q32If time permitsRenal

Management said the single-use share of dialysers moved to 30-35% of the market and could reach 50% single use and 50% multi-use. What drives conversion – PMNDP rules, private chains or infection data – and what does it do to your volumes?

Each point of single-use conversion multiplies dialyser volumes, because a reused dialyser serves several sessions.
Our context
Management said reuse was the norm a few years earlier, with each dialyser reused 6x-8x (May-2023).
Listen for
Reassuring: named policy or chain decisions behind the shift. Concerning: conversion assumed rather than observed.
Follow-up
What price difference makes a centre switch from reuse to single use?

Our context
PMNDP operates in 751 districts with 1,704 centres, according to the company.

Reassuring: named chain contracts and PPP operators. Concerning: reliance on government tenders alone.
Follow-up
Are you on the approved list of the largest private dialysis chains?

# Symbiotec's Vegan D3 Bet Enters a Crowded Niche

Global vitamin D3 is a **~185-tonne molecule** that the industry reports as a ~14,740-tonne business. Chinese capacity tables count 500,000 IU/g feed powder, China holds **83%** of that capacity, and actual demand of roughly 7,000–10,800 t of powder (about 85–135 t of pure D3) leaves the sector running at about half its capacity ([Tianfeng Securities](http://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202412161641320358_1.pdf); [BP Equities](https://s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/bpwealth-cdn/pdf/1618577883-BP%20Equities_%20Fermenta%20Biotech%20Ltd._Initiating%20Coverage_BUY_Trgt%20402.pdf)). Zhejiang Garden Bio leads with ~28% of capacity, a self-claimed 30–40% share and ~70% of the NF-grade lanolin cholesterol that every conventional producer needs. DSM-Firmenich has reportedly cut back its own D3 production and its animal nutrition and vitamins business is being sold to CVC, so Fermenta is the only large integrated D3 maker outside China ([Garden Bio IR, Apr 2025](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2025-04-07/1223021870.PDF); [dsm-firmenich](https://our-company.dsm-firmenich.com/en/our-company/news/press-releases/2026/dsm-firmenich-announces-agreement-to-divest-animal-nutrition-and-health-to-cvc-capital-partners.html)). Feed takes 85–90% of the tonnes, but feed prices swing 5–10x while human-nutrition and pharma grades hold steady. Feed D3 is now trading at **~RMB 95–105/kg (October 2026)**, against RMB 600+ list quotes in 2025, so nutrition and pharma earn more than half of a producer-level market worth only a few hundred million dollars ([Mysteel](https://list1.m.mysteel.com/zhishi/slwssD3jg.html)). Non-animal D3 exists but is small. Lichen and microalgae hold one or two micrograms of D3 per gram of biomass, and yeast-fermented D3 has not been launched commercially. The only route that scales is semi-synthesis from plant sterols (phytosterols), which is already commercial at Kerui/Chundi in China and at Fermenta in India (FSSAI approval July 2026, ~1 t/yr line targeted for FY28). Symbiotec plans a 40 MIU/g Ph.Eur.-grade vegan D3 built on its in-house phytosterol fermentation and photochemistry, with dedicated capacity from FY28. That fits its platform better than it would fit any other Indian steroid maker's. But Symbiotec arrives third, holds no vitamin D drug master file (DMF), European certificate of suitability (CEP) or FSSAI approval, and management's **US$600m** estimate of the niche is larger than the entire producer-level D3 market ([ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)). Analysts should value vegan D3 at close to zero in FY27–29 estimates. A one-tonne line is worth ₹7–90 cr of revenue depending almost entirely on a vegan price premium that no supplier publishes, and plant cholesterol and calcifediol are more valuable adjacent products.

## China's lanolin chain controls 83% of a 185-tonne molecule

### Capacity is counted in feed powder, not in D3

Every capacity number in this market has to be converted before it can be compared with anything Symbiotec or Fermenta might build. Garden Bio's filings define pure crystalline D3 as 40 MIU/g and feed-grade powder as 500,000 IU/g, so **80 t of feed powder contains 1 t of cholecalciferol** ([Garden Bio H1 2026 report](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-08-15/1225473446.PDF)). Chinese broker tables are the only producer-level capacity data available, and they use this 500 KIU/g basis. Tianfeng's December 2024 table puts global nameplate capacity at **~14,740 t (≈184 t pure D3)**, with China at 83.04%. BP Equities, citing Boyar Hexun, put it at 14,600 t around 2019, with China at 13,100 t ([Tianfeng Securities](http://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202412161641320358_1.pdf); [BP Equities](https://s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/bpwealth-cdn/pdf/1618577883-BP%20Equities_%20Fermenta%20Biotech%20Ltd._Initiating%20Coverage_BUY_Trgt%20402.pdf)). Other totals range from 10,840 t (Pacific Securities, which lists Hisound at 800 t and Fermenta at 300 t) to 12,800 t (Hzeyun). The spread comes mostly from out-of-date figures for Hisound, DSM, Fermenta and Zhejiang Medicine ([Pacific Securities](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202408051639134632_1.pdf); [Hzeyun](https://m.hzeyun.com/research/2596346.html)).

Outside China there are few producers. Europe's only large-scale synthesiser was DSM-Firmenich, which still says its D3 is made at Sisseln, Switzerland ([dsm-firmenich](https://www.dsm-firmenich.com/en/businesses/health-nutrition-care/products/vitamins/vitamin-d.html)). BASF made D3 in the 2001 cartel era, but its current activity looks like formulation only: an active US DMF for "Dry Vitamin D3 100" and a combined vitamin A + D3 feed product launched in October 2025. Whether BASF still synthesises D3 could not be confirmed ([FDA DMF list](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-master-files-dmfs/list-drug-master-files-dmfs); [BASF](https://www.basf.com/global/en/media/news-releases/2025/10/p-25-206)). Dishman Carbogen Amcis's Veenendaal site in the Netherlands (formerly Solvay's) makes cholesterol, vitamin D2 and vitamin D analogues rather than bulk D3, and its cholecalciferol CEP was withdrawn in 2023 ([Carbogen Amcis brochure](https://www.carbogen-amcis.com/images/brochures/cgam/2023/Cholesterol_Vitamin_D_Analogs_CARBOGEN-AMCIS.pdf); [EDQM CEP database](https://extranet.edqm.eu/4DLink1/4DCGI/Web_View/mono/72)). India has one producer, Fermenta, with plants at Dahej and Kullu ([Fermenta AR FY26](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual-Report-2025-26-1.pdf)). Japan and Singapore appear only as cholesterol suppliers. The United States has no producer: the American Feed Industry Association told a Section 301 hearing that US D3 supply is 100% dependent on China ([Expana](https://www.expanamarkets.com/insights/article/afia-highlights-non-china-vitamin-and-amino-acid-supply-at-section-301-hearing/)).

### Overcapacity and concentration let producers, not demand, set the price

In 2019 China produced **5,650 t against 13,100 t of capacity**, about 43% utilisation, and exported 4,050 t of it ([BP Equities](https://s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/bpwealth-cdn/pdf/1618577883-BP%20Equities_%20Fermenta%20Biotech%20Ltd._Initiating%20Coverage_BUY_Trgt%20402.pdf)). Tianfeng's high theoretical demand estimate of 10,836 t assumes 8.4 g of D3 per tonne across 1.29 bn t of global feed. Even on that basis, nameplate capacity exceeds need by ~35% ([Tianfeng Securities](http://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202412161641320358_1.pdf)). Price therefore reflects how disciplined producers are, not physical scarcity. When China's market regulator (SAMR) conditionally cleared the Garden–DSM 7-DHC joint venture in October 2019, it measured market concentration (HHI, based on output shares) at **2,236 for veterinary D3 and 2,798 for human D3 globally**. The remedies required Garden to sell cholesterol to rival D3 makers on fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory (FRAND) terms and not to unreasonably restrict output, and they lapsed automatically after five years ([SAMR decision](https://www.shanwei.gov.cn/shanwei/swzdly/jgsf/jgsf03/content/post_452053.html)). The lapse coincided with Chinese producers' "stop quoting, stop signing" stance from May 2024, which came before the 2024–25 price spike ([Sina](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2025-03-24/doc-inequprn0560695.shtml)). The industry has history here: the European Commission's 2001 vitamins cartel decision fined Solvay €9.1m and BASF €7.56m over the D3 market ([EU Decision 2003/2/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec/2003/2(1)/oj/eng/pdf)).

Two capacity details matter for Symbiotec in particular. Hisound is No. 2 in Tianfeng's table and led prices up, raising its quote to RMB 600/kg in April 2025. One source describes it as a controlled subsidiary of Zhejiang Xianju, a Chinese steroid API maker that Symbiotec's RHP names as a competitor, but Xianju's 2024 annual report does not list Hisound among its subsidiaries ([Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/888396671_135357); [Tencent](https://gu.qq.com/sz002332/gp/notice/nos1220987252); [Xianju 2024 AR](https://www.xjpharma.com/Upload/202504/20250429131008_4153.pdf); [Symbiotec RHP](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf)). If the link is real, a direct steroid competitor already runs a fifth of global D3 capacity. Separately, Gansu Jiahui's new D3 project faces strict environmental review, and dsm-firmenich says Chinese expansion plans were pushed back to mid-2026, not cancelled ([Gelonghui](https://m.gelonghui.com/p/2089901); [dsm-firmenich Q1 2026 outlook](https://www.dsm-firmenich.com/anh/en_NA/news/articles/vitamin-market-update-q1-2026.html)).

### DSM's retreat leaves Fermenta as the only large non-Chinese maker

Chinese brokers report that DSM gradually shut its vitamin C, vitamin B and D3 capacity during 2023–24 ([Dongwu Securities](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202507161710004178_1.pdf)). Garden says it **renewed its D3 contract with DSM in April 2026**, alongside 10–15-year agreements for cholesterol, 7-DHC and 25-OH-D3 ([Garden Bio IR, Apr 2026](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-29/1225257262.PDF); [Sina](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/relnews/cn/2024-07-06/doc-inccemmq1357995.shtml)). No DSM statement confirms a closure. DSM still holds valid EDQM certificates for cholecalciferol that name Sisseln, one revised as recently as February 2026 ([EDQM](https://extranet.edqm.eu/4DLink1/4DCGI/Web_View/mono/72)). That fits DSM remaining a regulated supplier of pharma-grade forms made partly from Chinese material. Its Animal Nutrition & Health unit, which includes vitamins, is being sold to CVC at an enterprise value of ~€2.2bn and split into an "Essential Products Company" (vitamins) and a premix company. dsm-firmenich keeps 20% and a long-term vitamin supply agreement for its human and pet businesses, and closing is expected at end-2026 ([dsm-firmenich](https://our-company.dsm-firmenich.com/en/our-company/news/press-releases/2026/dsm-firmenich-announces-agreement-to-divest-animal-nutrition-and-health-to-cvc-capital-partners.html)). That leaves Fermenta as the only large, integrated source outside China. It describes itself as "one of the world's three largest manufacturers of pharmaceutical-grade Vitamin D3", makes its own cholesterol from wool grease at Dahej, and in 2021 claimed ~25% of the human-grade D3 API market ([Fermenta AR FY26](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual-Report-2025-26-1.pdf); [BP Equities](https://s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/bpwealth-cdn/pdf/1618577883-BP%20Equities_%20Fermenta%20Biotech%20Ltd._Initiating%20Coverage_BUY_Trgt%20402.pdf)). Even Fermenta has its feed-grade D3 500 made by a toll manufacturer in Germany rather than in its own plants, which suggests feed-grade D3 is not economic to make outside China.

## Non-animal D3 scales only through phytosterol chemistry

"Vegan" D3 covers four chemically different supply chains that marketing copy tends to blur: lichen extracts, microalgae, yeast fermentation of sterol precursors, and semi-synthesis from plant sterols. Only the last has shown tonne-scale capacity.

Lichen is the brand-led segment. ESB Developments' Vitashine (UK) has sold wild-harvested lichen D3 since about 2012. Euromonitor certified Nutraland's Vegadelight as the top-selling vegan D3 ingredient in the US by 2025 business-to-business value, though Nutraland commissioned the research and no volumes were disclosed ([NutraIngredients, 2012](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2012/12/07/Lichen-based-vegan-vitamin-D3-gains-momentum-as-Nordic-Naturals-introduces-new-product/); [Nutritional Outlook](https://www.nutritionaloutlook.com/view/vegadelight-earns-euromonitor-recognition-top-selling-vegan-vitamin-d3-ingredient-us)). A long tail of Chinese and Indian sellers lists 100,000 IU/g lichen powders. None discloses capacity, and the biology explains why. Lund University measured just **0.67–2.04 µg of D3 per gram** of dry *Cladina arbuscula*, with its precursor 7-DHC below detection ([Lund University](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/f49ca442-2470-4d4e-95d1-1af92d805d5c)). At those levels, a kilogram of pure D3 needs roughly 490–1,490 t of dry lichen (my arithmetic). Direct extraction therefore yields kilograms of D3 for premium oils, not tonnes, and any tonne-scale "lichen" claim implies an extra conversion step. Sourcing is opaque enough that a Vitamin Retailer commentary called undisclosed lichen species "problematic", and Innobio sells stable-isotope proof of plant origin as a selling point ([Vitamin Retailer](https://vitaminretailer.com/synthetic-vs-vegan-d3-impact-on-health-sustainability/); [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Product-innovations/isotope-verified-vegan-d3-from-pine)).

Microalgae is thinner still. AIDP's VegD3, supplied as pure crystal since 2016 under self-affirmed GRAS (generally recognised as safe) status, is the only named business-to-business algae D3 ingredient ([NutraIngredients, 2019](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2019/04/29/AIDP-There-is-a-significant-market-potential-for-vegan-D3/)). *Nannochloropsis oceanica* produces at most ~1 µg/g after UV-B exposure ([Food Chemistry](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308814620304891)), and DTU's AlgaeVita scale-up project was still at the R&D stage in July 2025 ([DTU](https://www.food.dtu.dk/english/newsarchive/2025/07/researchers-aim-to-upscale-new-source-of-vitamin-d3-and-omega-3-fatty-acids)). Algae D3 makes sense as a by-product of omega-3 algae cultivation, not as a supply route in its own right.

Fermentation works technically but has no commercial D3 product. Engineered *S. cerevisiae* yeast reached **3.80 g/L of 7-DHC in 24 hours in a 50 L fermenter** at Jiangnan University, and 4.28 g/L in a 10 L vessel in another study ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39480248); [Metabolic Engineering](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1096717624000843)). At ~4 g/L, one 100 m³ batch would yield ~400 kg of 7-DHC (my arithmetic), which is large relative to vegan demand. Yet no commercial launch of yeast-made 7-DHC converted to D3 was found. The one commercial yeast route is dsm-firmenich's Hy-D. It ferments a sterol (5,7,24-cholestatrienol) with genetically modified *S. cerevisiae* strain CBS 146008 and converts it chemically to **25-OH-D3** for animal feed. It is authorised in the EU under Regulation 2024/1070, and EFSA extended its opinion to all animal species in 2025 ([EFSA Journal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12159549/)). In China, Ruipu Bio is building a "calcifediol cell factory" with the Chinese Academy of Sciences' Tianjin institute ([Pharnexcloud](https://www.pharnexcloud.com/zixun/sx_47012)). The barrier is cost. Yeast-made 7-DHC has to compete with lanolin cholesterol when feed D3 was selling at about USD 0.065 per MIU in April 2025 (my conversion of the RMB 230–250/kg quote; [Garden Bio IR, Apr 2025](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2025-04-07/1223021870.PDF)). GMO status also sits awkwardly with "natural vegan" marketing.

Semi-synthesis from phytosterols is where the industrial capacity is, and it is Symbiotec's home ground. Kerui Bio (科瑞生物), controlled by Zhejiang Ang Likang since 2023, makes plant-source cholesterol from phytosterols by "**biological fermentation plus multi-step chemical synthesis**". It claims to be China's first mass producer of plant cholesterol and plant vitamin D, and sells mainly to overseas pharma, nutrition and feed companies ([Ang Likang 2025 AR](https://disc.static.szse.cn/download/disc/disk03/finalpage/2026-04-29/d1db6d43-291e-489d-a3b0-d99559193f23.PDF)). Kerui reports **30 t/yr of plant cholesterol**. Its subsidiary Jiangxi Chundi earned **RMB 71.6m of revenue and RMB 15.6m of net profit in 2023**, and has a five-year 25-OH-D3 supply contract with a European company ([PopCJ](https://www.popcj.com/depth/0642502404363946)). A certification body's page credits Chundi with lines for 10 t/yr of D3 crystal, 10 t/yr of 25-OH-D3 crystal and 2 t/yr of D2 crystal ([SINOQUAL](https://www.halalkosher.com/zh-CN/articles/legacy-39)). But 30 t of plant cholesterol cannot feed 20 t of crystal at typical multi-step yields, so those are either nameplate figures or lines that also run on lanolin cholesterol. Kerui's specialty-intermediates sales declined in 2025 "due to fluctuating overseas customer demand" ([Ang Likang 2025 AR](https://disc.static.szse.cn/download/disc/disk03/finalpage/2026-04-29/d1db6d43-291e-489d-a3b0-d99559193f23.PDF)).

Fermenta's own documents call its VITADEE Green a phytosterol-derived 40 MIU/g D3, though trade press reports it as lichen-based. Its patent EP3906247 (priority July 2019) describes an entirely chemical route. Crude phytosterol is converted to cholesterol by tosylation, ozonolysis and a Grignard rebuild of the side chain, then to 7-DHC by allylic bromination, then to D3 by sensitised UV photolysis ([Fermenta Q1FY27](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Investor-PresentationQ1FY27.pdf); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3906247A1/en); [vegconomist](https://vegconomist.com/ingredients/fermenta-biotech-receives-indian-approval-plant-based-vitamin-d3-ingredient/)). VITADEE Green won FSSAI approval on 6 July 2026. Fermenta's ₹110 cr Dahej programme includes ~₹60 cr for plant-source D3 and calcifediol, reportedly ~1 t/yr of plant D3 plus 50 kg of derivatives, with commercial output targeted for FY28 ([Fermenta AR FY26](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual-Report-2025-26-1.pdf); [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/biotech/fermenta-biotech-to-invest-rs-110-crore-for-dahej-plant-expansion-18512)). Innobio launched its LocVita D3 in March 2024 from loblolly-pine oil, which is a phytosterol source. It won FSSAI vegan certification in March 2025 but is not sold in mainland China, because no regulation there covers plant-derived D3 ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/07/22/pine-derived-vitamin-d3-approved-as-vegan-ingredient-from-fssai)). Evonik makes plant cholesterol (PhytoChol) at Hanau, but sells it for mRNA lipid nanoparticles rather than D3 ([Evonik](https://www.evonik.com/en/products/hc/pr_52050742.html)).

Published market sizes for this segment are unusable at the ingredient level. One publisher puts lichen D3 at ~USD 0.1bn and "vegan D3" at USD 1.2bn for 2025, and Future Data Stats says USD 1.5bn ([Market Intelo lichen](https://marketintelo.com/report/vitamin-d3-from-lichen-market); [Market Intelo vegan](https://marketintelo.com/report/vegan-vitamin-d3-market); [Future Data Stats](https://www.futuredatastats.com/vegan-vitamin-d3-market)). Those numbers only make sense as retail value of finished products. Price evidence is modelled rather than taken from transactions. IndexBox puts lichen D3 oil at 100,000 IU/g at **USD 150–300/kg**, against USD 10–18/kg for commodity D3 powder of the same strength, an 8–30x premium per IU. Retail vegan softgels sell at only 1.5–3.5x lanolin equivalents, because the ingredient is a tiny share of the shelf price ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/search/vitamin-d-ingredients-price-evidence-world-2026/); [OneGreenPlanet](https://www.onegreenplanet.org/natural-health/lichen-vs-lanolin-why-the-source-of-your-vitamin-d3-matters-in-2026/)). Regulation is permissive in the US (self-affirmed GRAS) and increasingly explicit in India (FSSAI approvals for Innobio in 2025 and Fermenta in 2026). It is unresolved in the EU and closed in mainland China, which pushes Chinese plant-D3 makers to export markets ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/07/22/pine-derived-vitamin-d3-approved-as-vegan-ingredient-from-fssai); [vegconomist](https://vegconomist.com/ingredients/fermenta-biotech-receives-indian-approval-plant-based-vitamin-d3-ingredient/)).

## Feed takes 85% of tonnes but under half of today's value

### Volume and value split

No independent body publishes a global split by end use. Every estimate goes back to Chinese data compilers or to feed tonnage multiplied by an inclusion rate. Fhyanbao's 2024 estimate is the most detailed: **7,719 t for feed** (1.287 bn t of feed × 6 g/t), **472 t for food additives** and **943 t for pharma and healthcare**, a total of ~9,134 t that is ~85% feed ([Fhyanbao](https://www.fhyanbao.com/article/Y3yu4i1q)). Pacific Securities, citing Boyar Hexun, gives China's domestic split as **90% feed, 6% pharma and 4% food**. An older global data point of 7,250 t of demand, of which 5,500 t was feed, implies ~24% non-feed use once the feed-heavy Chinese mix is diluted ([Pacific Securities](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202408051639134632_1.pdf)). Converted to pure D3, feed uses roughly 85–100 t a year and all human uses together perhaps 10–25 t.

Value splits very differently. Published "vitamin D3 market" sizes run from USD 337.5m (Market.us, 2024) to USD 1.7bn (GMI), USD 2.49bn (SkyQuest) and USD 4.2bn (Dataintelo) ([Market.us](https://market.us/report/vitamin-d3-market/); [GMI](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/vitamin-d3-cholecalciferol-market); [SkyQuest](https://www.skyquestt.com/report/vitamin-d3-market); [Dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/global-vitamin-d3-market)). The higher figures can only be counting formulated and finished products. Bottom-up arithmetic is more useful. On my numbers, ~7,400–7,700 t of feed demand at October 2026's ~RMB 100/kg is worth only about **USD 100m** at the producer level, and about USD 260m at the early-2025 traded level of ~RMB 250/kg. Fermenta anchors the human-grade figure. Its FY26 human-nutrition D3 revenue of ₹293 cr (≈USD 33–34m), combined with its 2021 claim of ~25% of the human D3 API market, implies a human-grade market of roughly **USD 130–150m** ([Fermenta AR FY26](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual-Report-2025-26-1.pdf); [BP Equities](https://s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/bpwealth-cdn/pdf/1618577883-BP%20Equities_%20Fermenta%20Biotech%20Ltd._Initiating%20Coverage_BUY_Trgt%20402.pdf)). That share claim is five years old and comes from the company. The producer-level market is therefore ~USD 200–400m depending on where feed is in its cycle, and human and pharma grades now earn more than half of it. Fermenta's own mix shows the tilt: human nutrition was **73% of its FY26 D3 revenue**, whereas six years earlier animal nutrition made up nearly two-thirds of group revenue ([Fermenta AR FY26](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual-Report-2025-26-1.pdf)).

### Customers buy through formulators, and DSM's sale reopens contracts

Big supplement brands do not disclose their D3 suppliers. No public source names the supplier for Haleon, Bayer, Nestlé Health Science, Pharmavite, Abbott or By-health. What is visible is how the channel works. Garden sells to the global formulators and premix makers (DSM, Taiyo, Cargill, Adisseo, BASF and Trouw/Nutreco) under long-term agreements, and **53% of its 2024 export revenue went to the US** ([Garden Bio IR, Apr 2025](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2025-04-07/1223021870.PDF); [Sina](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/relnews/cn/2024-07-06/doc-inccemmq1357995.shtml)). Fermenta sells crystal, oils and spray-dried powder to **400+ customers in 60+ countries**. Its largest customer is 9% of revenue and its top 10 are 42%. FY26 sales by region were India 39%, Europe 30% and North America 13%, and India rose to 47% in Q1 FY27 ([Fermenta AR FY26](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual-Report-2025-26-1.pdf); [Fermenta Q1FY27](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Investor-PresentationQ1FY27.pdf)). Kingdomway uses its own D3 in its Doctor's Best brand, about 80% of which is sold through global e-commerce ([10jqka](https://stock.10jqka.com.cn/20260908/c679715272.shtml)). The likely pattern is that brand owners buy formulated, pharmacopoeia-grade D3 with DMF or CEP support from DSM, BASF and Fermenta, while premix makers and formulators buy crystal directly from China. That is an inference from these customer lists, not confirmed for any named brand. The CVC transaction matters here. Once it closes at end-2026, dsm-firmenich's human-nutrition arm becomes a contract buyer from the CVC-owned vitamins company, so human-grade supply relationships are likely to be renegotiated in 2027 ([ThePoultrySite](https://www.thepoultrysite.com/news/2026/02/dsm-firmenich-announces-agreement-to-divest-animal-nutrition-health-to-cvc-capital-partners)).

### Pharma D3 is sub-tonne and certificate-gated; analogues are kilogram businesses

India is the standout pharma market. In 2020 there were 1,115 vitamin D products in India, of which **457 were cholecalciferol (41%)**. Of those, 63.5% were 60,000 IU strengths sold as capsules, sachets, tablets and injections, up from cholecalciferol being ~10% of preparations around 2013 ([PMC8477736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8477736/); [IJPS](https://www.ijpsonline.com/articles/vitamin-d-supplements-in-the-indian-market.html)). The latest hard market value is ₹5.38bn for 2018 ([PMC8477736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8477736/)). Cholecalciferol is under price control by the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA), which pushes formulators toward cheap API, and about one in five Indians is vitamin D deficient ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/health/report-warns-india-faces-deepening-vitamin-d-crisis-offers-roadmap-to-address-it)). In tonnes the market is tiny. One kilogram of pure D3 makes ~667,000 doses of 60,000 IU, so even several hundred million sachets a year use well under a tonne (my arithmetic). Europe has licensed high-dose products such as Dekristol 20,000 IU and Fultium-D3 20,000 IU. In the US, the classic prescription vitamin D (Drisdol) is D2, not D3 ([fachinfo.de](https://fachinfo.de/pdf/011346); [HPRA](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/30446/a6764095-4502-4748-b336-ffb4e3a79e04.pdf); [FDA DMF list](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-master-files-dmfs/list-drug-master-files-dmfs)).

At the regulatory level, pharma-grade D3 is an oligopoly. Valid EDQM certificates for cholecalciferol are held only by **Fermenta, Garden and DSM-Firmenich**. Dishman Netherlands' was withdrawn in 2023. Active US DMFs belong to DSM, Fermenta, Dishman Netherlands, Taizhou Hisound, BASF (for a formulated form) and Supreem of Mysore (formulated forms). NHU and Kingdomway hold none, and Garden's US DMF is inactive ([EDQM](https://extranet.edqm.eu/4DLink1/4DCGI/Web_View/mono/72); [FDA DMF list](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-master-files-dmfs/list-drug-master-files-dmfs)). Vitamin D analogue APIs are a separate specialist field. The repeat filers across calcitriol, alfacalcidol, calcipotriol, calcifediol, paricalcitol and doxercalciferol are Formosa Laboratories, TAPI NL, Cerbios and Carbogen Amcis, plus Chinese entrants (Chongqing Huapont, Nanjing Heron, Anhui Haoyuan, ALP Pharm). The Indian filers are Sun Pharma (doxercalciferol), Neuland and Sai Life (paricalcitol) ([FDA DMF list](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-master-files-dmfs/list-drug-master-files-dmfs); [EDQM calcitriol](https://extranet.edqm.eu/4DLink1/4DCGI/Web_View/mono/883)). The finished drugs are worth tens to low hundreds of millions of dollars per molecule. US paricalcitol capsules sold ~USD 110m on a moving annual total basis in 2014, Faes Farma's calcifediol franchise sold €63.5m in 2025, and OPKO's Rayaldee earned USD 29.8m in 2025 ([Dr. Reddy's 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001135951/000119312514262694/d756631d6k.htm); [Investing.com](https://ng.investing.com/news/company-news/faes-farma-fy-2025-slides-23-revenue-surge-masks-profit-pressures-93CH-2367801); [OPKO 10-K](https://www.opko.com/investors/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001193125-26-076596/opk-20251231.htm)). Calcifediol stands out: only **Carbogen Amcis, Formosa and Łukasiewicz** hold valid CEPs ([EDQM calcifediol](https://extranet.edqm.eu/4DLink1/4DCGI/Web_View/mono/1295)), and Garden, Fermenta and Kerui are all moving toward it. Symbiotec holds about 45 active US DMFs on the FDA's Q3 2026 list (its RHP cites 43), all for corticosteroids, sex hormones and their intermediates, and **none for any vitamin D compound** ([FDA DMF list](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-master-files-dmfs/list-drug-master-files-dmfs)).

Feed-grade D3 spends long stretches near the cash cost of the marginal Chinese producer, about RMB 50–130/kg, broken by spikes of 3–10x that last one to three quarters. Every spike in the record came from supply, not demand: environmental closures, COVID logistics, plant accidents, knock-on effects from BASF, and producer discipline. Demand is price-insensitive because D3 is under 0.02% of feed cost ([Gelonghui](https://m.gelonghui.com/p/2089901)). Analysts should model on traded reference prices, not list prices. In October 2026, **Garden's RMB 360/kg list price sits ~3.5x above the ~RMB 95–105 traded price** ([Mysteel](https://list1.m.mysteel.com/zhishi/slwssD3jg.html)). The 2026 down-leg shows up in every listed D3 producer. Garden's Q3 2026 gross margin fell 17 percentage points to **36.75%** and net profit fell 50.6%, with 9M net profit down 28.3%. In April 2026 it also pushed back its B6, biotin and L-alanine projects ([Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/15214002.html)). Fermenta's animal-nutrition realisations fell **40% year on year** in Q1 FY27, and the company "chose volume discipline over chasing a weak price" ([Fermenta Q1FY27 press release](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/1786437408-Pressrelease-unauditedfinancialresultsofQ1FY27.pdf)). A secondary source puts dsm-firmenich ANH's H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin at 4.4%, down from 19.0% ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/322156/20260730/dsm-firmenich-cut-1000-jobs-after-animal-nutrition-sale-leaves-stranded-costs.htm)). No broker or industry consensus forecast for 2027–28 was found. With nameplate capacity 1.4–2x demand and Chinese expansions delayed rather than cancelled, the base case is feed D3 staying in the RMB 80–150/kg band until the next supply shock, which history suggests arrives every two to five years.

### Human and pharma grades follow a separate, steadier price ladder

Boyar Hexun describes food and pharma D3 prices as "relatively stable", and Fermenta's results bear this out ([Pacific Securities](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202408051639134632_1.pdf)). In Q1 FY27 its human-nutrition revenue was ₹76.5 cr, up 24% quarter on quarter with volumes up 18%, while feed realisations collapsed ([Fermenta Q1FY27](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Investor-PresentationQ1FY27.pdf)). Comparing grades on a pure-D3 basis helps. At RMB 100/kg, feed powder holds 12.5 g of D3 per kg, which works out to **~USD 1,100/kg of pure D3**. Food-grade D3 100 (100,000 IU/g) was quoted at **USD 13.2/kg FOB Shanghai in June 2025**, ≈USD 5,300/kg of pure D3, though formulation cost dominates at that strength ([ChemAnalyst](https://www.chemanalyst.com/NewsAndDeals/NewsDetails/rising-demand-and-lean-inventories-push-vitamin-d3-prices-higher-in-china-and-us-37514)). IndexBox models pharma crystalline D3 API at **USD 800–1,500/kg** ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/search/vitamin-d-ingredients-price-evidence-world-2026/)). North American feed contracts ran at US$35–42/kg in September 2025, well above Chinese domestic levels, partly because Canada has fewer approved suppliers ([dsm-firmenich Q4 2025](https://www.dsm-firmenich.com/anh/en_NA/news/articles/vitamin-market-outlook-q4-2025.html)). Feed-grade 25-OH-D3 is reported at ~RMB 4,000/kg, an unverified figure ([Gelonghui](https://m.gelonghui.com/p/2089901)). No public bulk price exists for EP/USP crystal, D3 oil or vegan crystal; all of these are quote-based.

### Cholesterol, not lanolin, is the cost chokepoint

Conventional D3 is made from NF-grade (≥95%) cholesterol extracted from wool grease. Global NF cholesterol capacity is only **~1,820 t/yr**: Garden 1,200 t, NK (Singapore) 250 t, Dishman 250 t and Nippon Fine Chemical 120 t. The non-Garden figures date from 2014, and Garden holds **~70% of global NF lanolin cholesterol** ([Tianfeng](http://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202412161641320358_1.pdf); [Pacific Securities](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202408051639134632_1.pdf)). China's GB 7300.202-2019 standard allows only lanolin-derived cholesterol for feed D3 ([Tianfeng](http://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202412161641320358_1.pdf)). Raw wool grease is plentiful; Garden calls supply "ample" ([Garden Bio IR, Apr 2025](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2025-04-07/1223021870.PDF)). The advantage lies in converting it to cholesterol. Cholesterol is **~45% of raw-material cost** for a producer that buys it in, and Fermenta's own cholesterol plant at Dahej was expected to cut that cost by 20–25%, worth ~300 bp of gross margin ([BP Equities](https://s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/bpwealth-cdn/pdf/1618577883-BP%20Equities_%20Fermenta%20Biotech%20Ltd._Initiating%20Coverage_BUY_Trgt%20402.pdf)). Garden's proprietary oxidation-reduction route adds a yield advantage: **46.9% overall D3 yield versus ~30%** for the older bromination route ([Tianfeng](http://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202412161641320358_1.pdf)). The trough results show what that is worth. Garden's D3 segment still earned a **47.9% gross margin in 2023**, when feed D3 averaged ~RMB 50–56/kg, while Fermenta's EBITDA was negative in both FY23 (−₹39.7 cr) and FY24 (−₹44.5 cr) ([Lianhe Ratings](https://www.lhratings.com/reports/B0648-P66511-2022-GZ2024.pdf); [CARE, Nov 2023](https://www.careratings.com/upload/CompanyFiles/PR/202311131111_Fermenta_Biotech_Limited.pdf); [CARE, Oct 2025](https://www.careratings.com/upload/CompanyFiles/PR/202510131020_Fermenta_Biotech_Limited.pdf)). European cholesterol looks cost-disadvantaged too. Dishman cut its "Cholesterol SF" sales because they lost money as wool prices escalated through December 2024 ([Dishman Q3FY25](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachHis/5fbfa54d-d4f0-43bb-a800-0853fc2d4b39.pdf)). Cholesterol rose ~20% in early 2025, and Garden blamed part of its Q3 2026 margin squeeze on raw materials ([Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/888396671_135357); [Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/15214002.html)). At Garden's yields, actual D3 output needs only ~180–300 t of cholesterol a year (my arithmetic). Most NF cholesterol therefore goes to cosmetics, shrimp feed and lipid nanoparticles, so D3 makers that buy cholesterol compete for it with other users and depend on their largest D3 rival for supply.

This is where a phytosterol route changes the economics. Symbiotec already buys soy phytosterols at scale, partly from the US and from large food processors such as Cargill, and ferments them in-house into steroid starting materials ([Symbiotec RHP](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf); [ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)). A plant route to cholesterol avoids Garden's chokepoint entirely. The catch is that no published cost curve compares phytosterol-route and lanolin-route D3. The only yield data come from Fermenta's patent examples: 97% for the cholesterol-forming step and 54% across bromination, elimination and hydrolysis to 7-DHC ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3906247A1/en)). Trade policy offers little protection. India has no anti-dumping duty on D3, although the 2025 duty on vitamin A palmitate shows the trade-remedies authority (DGTR) will act on Chinese vitamins when a domestic producer files a case. D3 does not appear among the 41 products in the bulk-drug production-linked incentive (PLI) scheme ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/india-imposes-anti-dumping-duty-on-imports-of-vitamin-a-palmitate-from-china-eu-and-switzerland-1421207); [ISID](https://isid.org.in/pdf/WP239.pdf)).

## Symbiotec arrives third in a niche smaller than its pitch

### What Symbiotec has and has not said

Symbiotec has been listed since 1 September 2026. It reported FY26 revenue of **₹869 cr and EBITDA of ₹238 cr (27.4%)**, and holds an estimated 38% of global corticosteroid API volume ([ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf); [Symbiotec RHP](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf)). Its RHP says it intends to introduce "speciality ingredients such as Vegan Vitamin D3 and Algal based Docosa Hexanoic Acid (DHA)", and to extend into "steroidal derivatives such as vitamin D2 and D3 using our photochemistry capabilities" ([Symbiotec RHP](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf)). Its nutraceutical website lists vegan cholecalciferol as "Developed" at 40 MIU/g, compliant with the Ph.Eur. monograph, under the tagline "The only plant path to the pharma ceiling". It cites "phytosterols through a 3-stage bioprocess", lists a "developed" yeast-derived D2, and promises a "2026 Nutra Launch" ([nutra.symbiotec.com](https://nutra.symbiotec.com/)). ICICI Direct, citing management, says vegan D3 is "a niche market valued at US$ 600 million and is growing at ~10%" with "few competitors", and that a **dedicated capacity is expected to be functional in FY28** ([ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)). Several things are absent. A text search of the RHP finds no mention of 7-DHC, calcifediol, calcitriol or lanolin. No D3 capacity in tonnes, capex, customer, joint venture or regulatory filing has been disclosed. Q1 FY27 commentary focused on biotech contract manufacturing (CDMO) and injectables, and did not mention vitamin D ([Arthneeti](https://www.arthneeti.com/insights/symbiotec-pharma-q1-fy27-earnings); [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/news/symbiotec-pharmalab-targets-20-25-fy27-growth-anticipating-h2-cdmo-revenue)).

### The technical fit is real and closest to Kerui, not Fermenta

Symbiotec's process begins with "bioconversion of phytosterol to … bisnor alcohol, 9-hydroxy androstenedione or androstenedione". It then uses chemistry that includes bromination and debromination, Grignard reactions and hydrogenation. Its R&D includes a photoreactor, and it runs continuous-flow production for progesterone ([Symbiotec RHP](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf)). Bisnor alcohol (BA) matters because it is a 22-carbon intermediate that keeps a side-chain handle, which can be extended back to cholesterol's 27-carbon skeleton. From cholesterol, allylic bromination gives 7-DHC, and UV photolysis followed by thermal isomerisation gives D3. Every step resembles chemistry Symbiotec already runs. The likely route, combining in-house fermentation with chemistry, therefore mirrors Kerui's "fermentation plus multi-step synthesis" more closely than Fermenta's patented route from crude phytosterol, which uses ozonolysis and Grignard chemistry ([Ang Likang 2025 AR](https://disc.static.szse.cn/download/disc/disk03/finalpage/2026-04-29/d1db6d43-291e-489d-a3b0-d99559193f23.PDF); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3906247A1/en)). This is an inference; Symbiotec has not disclosed its route. What is genuinely new for Symbiotec is scaling up photoreactors, handling D3 (a light- and air-sensitive secosteroid), building D3-specific DMF, CEP and FSSAI dossiers, and checking freedom to operate against Fermenta's European application and its Indian process patent (granted September 2025 per Fermenta's annual report) ([Fermenta AR FY26](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual-Report-2025-26-1.pdf)). Two points work in its favour. Its Ujjain fermentation site already holds FSSAI approval and FSSC accreditation ([Symbiotec RHP](https://assets.upstox.com/ipo/documents/rhp/symbiotec-rhp.pdf)). And under India's fortification rules, vitamin D may be added to milk and edible oil voluntarily only from plant sources ([ICRIER PB55](https://www.icrier.org/pdf/pb55_Towards-Vitamin-D-Kuposhan-Mukt-Bharat-through-Food-Fortification-FSSAI.pdf)).

### The economics hinge on a premium nobody publishes

Scale is the first problem with the management figure. One tonne of pure D3 holds 4×10¹³ IU, enough for **8 billion 5,000 IU softgels**, or about 22 million people taking one daily for a year (my arithmetic). Even modest phytosterol-route capacity is therefore large against vegan demand. Revenue per tonne depends entirely on price. At IndexBox's modelled pharma crystal price of USD 800–1,500/kg, a 1 t line earns only **₹7–13 cr** a year. At USD 5,000–10,000/kg, roughly the per-IU price of food-grade beadlets, it earns ₹44–88 cr, or 5–10% of Symbiotec's FY26 revenue. Lichen-oil price parity (USD 60,000–120,000/kg of pure-D3 equivalent) would imply USD 60–120m per tonne. That equals the entire human-grade D3 market, so the lichen premium cannot survive once tonne-scale phytosterol supply arrives. On the same arithmetic, management's US$600m niche would need 5–10 t of pure vegan D3 sold at lichen-oil prices, which is more than lichen can supply. It is also larger than the whole producer-level D3 market of ~USD 200–400m, so it can only be a finished-product retail figure ([ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf); [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/search/vitamin-d-ingredients-price-evidence-world-2026/)). Fermenta's own spending gives the best clue to realistic pricing. It is investing ~₹60 cr for ~1 t of plant D3 plus 50 kg of derivatives, and at commodity crystal prices that line would earn back its capex only after 5–9 years of revenue. Fermenta must therefore expect either a multiple of commodity pricing or calcifediol to carry the investment ([Fermenta AR FY26](https://fermentabiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual-Report-2025-26-1.pdf); [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/biotech/fermenta-biotech-to-invest-rs-110-crore-for-dahej-plant-expansion-18512)). The real-world ceiling so far is Kerui's subsidiary Chundi, the most established plant-D player, with ~USD 10m of revenue in 2023. Its parent's specialty-intermediates sales then fell in 2025.

Timing pushes any contribution further out. If capacity arrives in FY28, customer audits, stability data and CEP/DMF or FSSAI approvals typically take 12–36 months after validation batches (my inference). That points to meaningful revenue no earlier than FY29. ICICI's FY29E estimate of ₹542 cr EBITDA treats vegan D3 as one of several small new APIs inside an 8–10% API growth rate ([ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)). Vegan D3 also competes for management attention with ~₹1,475 cr of gross capex, only ~40% of which earns revenue today. That capex is for CDMO (alternative protein, insulin, GLP-1) and double-chamber vials, and its start-up costs pulled Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin down to 21% ([ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf); [Arthneeti](https://www.arthneeti.com/insights/symbiotec-pharma-q1-fy27-earnings)). The strongest Symbiotec-specific positive is policy. If FSSAI makes plant-source vitamin D fortification of milk and oil mandatory, India becomes a protected domestic market of a few tonnes of pure D3 (my rough estimate). A 2020 report said a mandate was months away, but a 2025 policy brief still describes fortification as voluntary ([NutraIngredients-Asia](https://nutraingredients-asia.com/Article/2020/09/22/Mandatory-fortification-in-India-Regulation-set-to-compel-firms-to-fortify-edible-oil-and-milk-FSSAI-exclusive); [ICRIER PB55](https://www.icrier.org/pdf/pb55_Towards-Vitamin-D-Kuposhan-Mukt-Bharat-through-Food-Fortification-FSSAI.pdf)).

## Conclusion

For Symbiotec, the question is pricing, not capacity or Chinese cost. Its phytosterol route avoids Garden's cholesterol chokepoint and the feed-grade price cycle altogether, so the RMB 95/kg feed price is irrelevant to it. What matters is whether "plant-origin, pharma-grade crystal" holds a lasting premium. That creates a paradox. The scalability that makes phytosterol D3 viable is what destroys the scarcity premium lichen D3 enjoys: Chundi's reported 10 t line, Fermenta's 1 t and any Symbiotec line together could supply billions of doses into a market where ingredient value is measured in tens of millions of dollars. The empirical ceiling so far, Chundi at ~USD 10m of revenue with its parent's specialty sales shrinking in 2025, fits that view better than management's US$600m does. Analysts should value vegan D3 at zero in base estimates. The triggers to revisit are a disclosed tonnage and capex, a D3 CEP, DMF or FSSAI filing, Fermenta's first published VITADEE Green prices and orders, and a mandatory-fortification notification.

Symbiotec's real advantages are in-house fermentation of BA from soy sterols, independent of Chinese lanolin, plus a regulatory track record of 43 DMFs and 23 CEPs. These are worth more on high-value, kilogram-scale secosteroids than on vegan D3 tonnage. Calcifediol has only three valid CEP holders while Garden, Fermenta and Kerui all move toward it. Plant cholesterol has a separate pharma-excipient market in lipid nanoparticles, where Evonik and Kerui are the benchmarks. The DSM–CVC separation, closing at end-2026, is likely to reopen Western human-nutrition supply contracts in 2027. A non-Chinese supplier holding a CEP would matter in that window. Fermenta is ready for it; Symbiotec is not yet.

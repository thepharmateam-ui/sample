# Supply shocks keep reshuffling US testosterone vials

Generic testosterone cypionate (TC) multi-dose vials make up almost the entire US testosterone injectables market. They account for **about 92–95% of injectable prescriptions** in public-payer data. Xyosted (about 3.5%) is the only other form above 1%, and injectables as a whole are **67% (Medicare) to 82% (Medicaid) of all TRT scripts**. Among generic vial labelers in 2025, **Hikma leads with about 24%, followed by Padagis (about 18%), Pfizer's Hospira authorized generic (about 16%), Xiromed (about 12%), Wilshire (about 11%) and Cipla (about 10%)**. Labels hide who actually makes the product. Hikma's plant in Portugal also fills vials sold under the Padagis and Cipla labels, so it is the source of **about 38–47% of generic volume**. About a quarter of volume comes from contract manufacturers (CMOs). The supply angle is real but cuts both ways. Recalls and exits cluster among small labelers whose product is made in India or by a CMO: Sun exited, and Cipla, Wilshire and Eugia all had recalls. Yet the biggest share shift today comes from the market leader. **Pfizer's combined share fell from about 25% to under 10% of Medicaid cypionate Rx between 2024 and Q1 2026** as it hit manufacturing delays. For Symbiotec, the data say share can be won: Xiromed went from launch to about 15% of volume in two years. But the winners were reliable suppliers with their own plants. Symbiotec has no approved generic application (ANDA) and only a pilot-scale sterile site, and selling finished vials would put it in competition with its own API customers.

*Data basis: all shares in this report are Medicaid SDUD prescriptions and/or Medicare Part D claims, pulled 21 Oct 2026. These sources leave out commercial insurance and the cash-pay telehealth channel, so the shares are directional proxies, not IQVIA national TRx shares.*

## Generic cypionate vials hold about 95% of injectable scripts

About nine TC applications are active, including Pfizer's brand Depo-Testosterone, which is itself approved as an ANDA. The newest is **BPI Labs, approved 3 Sep 2026** ([openFDA Drugs@FDA](https://api.fda.gov/drug/drugsfda.json?search=products.active_ingredients.name:%22TESTOSTERONE+CYPIONATE%22&limit=100)). Everything else is a niche.

Three points matter for a vial entrant. **First, value and volume diverge.** Xyosted costs about $793 per Part D claim against about $33 for generic TC, so it takes 45% of injectable spend on under 4% of scripts. **Second, pharmacy data miss in-clinic use.** Medicare Part B logged 313,953 in-office TC claims and 14,649 Aveed claims ($19.94M) in 2024 ([CMS Part B](https://data.cms.gov/data-api/v1/dataset/76a714ad-3a2c-43ac-b76d-9dadf8f7d890/data?keyword=testosterone)). In-office TC fell 26% between 2020 and 2024 while pharmacy-dispensed TC rose 45%, a shift towards self-injected retail vials. **Third, volume is growing while price falls.** Part D generic TC claims rose 63% from 2020 to 2025 (about 10% a year), yet gross spend stayed flat at about $41M ([CMS Part D annual](https://data.cms.gov/data-api/v1/dataset/7e0b4365-fd63-4a29-8f5e-e0ac9f66a81b/data?keyword=testosterone)). Pharmacy acquisition cost (NADAC) for a 200 mg/mL 1 mL vial is **$11.01, down from $15.15 in Jan 2020**. A 10 mL vial costs $35.20 ([NADAC 2026](https://data.medicaid.gov/dataset/fbb83258-11c7-47f5-8b18-5f8e79f7e704)). The latest national figures are older. IQVIA put total TRT at 8.0M TRx in 2021 and the injectable TRT market at $545M at list price (WAC) ([Antares FY2021 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1016169/000101616922000028/atrs-20211231.htm)). It put TC vials at **$226.8M for the 12 months to Aug 2023** ([Aurobindo filing](https://www.aurobindo.com/api/uploads/corporateannouncements/Fy24_TESTOSTERONE%20CYPRIONATE%20INJ%20APPROVAL.pdf)). Our estimate is that injectables hold 65–75% of national TRT scripts, towards the top of that range once cash-pay telehealth is included.

Re-cut by the plant that fills the vial, 2025 generic volume splits into **Hikma Portugal 38.3%** (46.6% in Q1 2026), Farmalán 15.8%, Pfizer 15.7%, Pii 10.9%, Padagis's CMO 7.6%, LSNE for Cipla 5.9%, Eugia 4.7% and American Regent 1.2%. That puts **about 76% of volume on own or group plants and about 24% on CMOs**. Counted by label, though, about half the labels sell product filled in someone else's plant. The scarce asset is a US-approved sterile line for oil-based fills, not the US label itself. **Pfizer is the only player that makes its own API**; no Indian finished-dose player lists its own testosterone ester API ([FDA DMF list](https://www.fda.gov/media/192069/download?attachment)). Concentration has loosened. The top three labelers fell from about 80% of volume in 2022–23 to about 58% in 2025, and the HHI fell from about 2,400–2,650 to about 1,520–1,580. Both rebounded in Q1 2026 (top three about 65%) as Pfizer's authorized generic lost volume.

## Small labelers made in India or by CMOs take most quality failures, but Pfizer is today's gap

The answer to the low-share question is **yes for quality failures, with one large exception**. Every manufacturer-level TC vial recall from 2019 to 2026 came from Cipla, Sun, Wilshire or Eugia (plus one unapproved product). None came from Pfizer, Hikma, Padagis, Xiromed or Alvogen ([openFDA enforcement](https://api.fda.gov/drug/enforcement.json?search=product_description:testosterone+AND+report_date:%5B20190101+TO+20261231%5D&limit=100)).

| Cipla (15% Medicaid 2022 → ~10%) | 2019 recall; Goa warning letter 2020; 10 mL discontinued 2022; moved filling to a Spanish CMO | Site compliance | Hikma, Padagis |

| Pfizer (brand + AG: 24.6% of Medicaid 2024 → 9.5% Q1 2026) | Brand on backorder, "manufacturing delays", no ETA; Canada shortage Jan–Oct 2025; New Zealand shortage Oct–Dec 2025 | Factory delays | Xiromed + NorthStar (4.1% Q4 2024 → 18.6% Q3 2025), Hikma (32% Q1 2026) |

Three patterns follow. **Crystallization keeps recurring**: Wilshire in 2023 and Eugia in 2025 had complaints of crystals that would not redissolve. Pfizer itself calls the 200 mg/mL strength a "supersaturated" oily solution ([Pfizer letter](https://cdn.pfizer.com/pfizercom/products/Depo-Testosterone_USI729509-02C.pdf)). That points to how the API's particles and crystal form are controlled, which an API maker can engineer. **The two largest disruptions both started at the API**: Hikma's industry-wide API problem in 2019 and Sun's 2022 recall of lots made with rejected API ([CA Board of Pharmacy](https://www.pharmacy.ca.gov/about/recall_alerts/060822_sun.pdf)). **The share now up for grabs belongs to a top-three player.** FDA has never listed TC as being in shortage. ASHP's bulletin, open since Sept 2019, shows only the Pfizer brand as affected in Sept 2026 ([ASHP](https://www.ashp.org/drug-shortages/current-shortages/drug-shortage-detail.aspx?id=592)). ASHP does not track Xiromed, NorthStar, Alvogen, Wilshire or BPI, so their reliability is simply unobserved, not proven.

|---|---|---|
| **When Sun exited**: late 2022 (claims) vs late 2023 (ASHP) | Sun's Medicaid share fell from 18.0% in Q4 2022 to 3.1% in Q1 2023; Part D fell from 27.0% in 2022 to 0.9% in 2023. "Late 2023" is best read as the formal discontinuation after inventory ran out. Competitors backfilled from 2023, not after Eugia's Oct 2023 approval |
| **When Pfizer's brand backorder began** | Consumer blogs date it to early or mid 2023 ([Medfinder](https://www.medfinder.com/blog/testosterone-shortage-what-providers-prescribers-need-to-know-2026)). In 2023 the brand actually *gained* share (8.9% of Medicaid) while absorbing Sun's volume. In 2024 the brand fell while Pfizer's AG rose and Pfizer's total climbed from 21.0% to 24.6%, a mix shift within Pfizer. Pfizer's total then fell to 18.1% (2025) and 9.5% (Q1 2026), matching factory-delay notices in Canada and New Zealand | **Brand SKU deprioritized from 2024; Pfizer's real capacity constraint dates from 2025 into 2026.** Use ASHP for current status, claims for timing, and discount the blog dating |

## Conclusion: Symbiotec's edge is reliable supply, not a new product

The market rewards dependable supply more than new formats. Since 2022, two supply shocks (Sun, Pfizer) each moved double-digit share within a few quarters, and four new sources entered. **Xiromed reached about 15% of volume within two years from a dedicated hormone plant in Spain**, and Eugia reached about 5%. A mid-single-digit share is a reasonable base case for a credible new source. At the last public IQVIA value, 5% is **about $11M a year**, less after the roughly 9–10% annual fall in price per claim seen in Part D (our estimate). That is modest for the market but meaningful next to Symbiotec's complex-injectables vertical, which was 3.80% of FY26 revenue ([Bajaj Broking RHP summary](https://www.bajajbroking.in/blog/symbiotec-pharmalab-ipo)). Symbiotec holds active US API master files (DMFs) for both cypionate (29023) and enanthate (26166) ([FDA DMF list](https://www.fda.gov/media/192069/download?attachment)). It was named as API supplier on Eugia's 2023 label, but that entry was dropped in Mar 2025 ([Eugia SPL v1](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/spl/data/f901e2b2-f143-4237-8e95-0d134585490b/f901e2b2-f143-4237-8e95-0d134585490b.xml)). It has no finished-dose ANDA. Its Mhow facility is built for 20M double-chamber vials a year, is still at pilot scale, and its regulatory approvals are "in process".

That points to **partnering first, building later**. CMO-dependent labelers account for about 24% of volume. Padagis has used at least three CMOs since 2006 and has not disclosed its current filler, and Cipla has already moved its filling once. Private-label channels such as NorthStar exist. A captive-API source for one of these buyers, filled at an approved CMO, is faster than an own ANDA plus a pre-approval inspection at Mhow. FDA's Feb 2026 product-specific guidance reportedly waives in-vivo bioequivalence for formulations that match the reference product's ingredients and amounts ([FDA PSG](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/psg/PSG_216318.pdf)). DEA rules are an administrative cost, not a cap. Testosterone is Schedule III, so production quotas do not apply ([21 USC 826](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/826)), but every import shipment needs a Form 236 declaration filed at least 15 days ahead ([21 CFR 1312.18](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-II/part-1312/section-1312.18)). The risks are real. **Selling vials puts Symbiotec against its own API customers**, and its claimed 76.4% global testosterone volume share comes from a report the company commissioned. Indian sterile sites have a poor record in this product (Sun, Cipla Goa, Eugia). Chinese cypionate API master files were filed in 2025, and BPI Labs entered in Sept 2026. DEA telehealth prescribing flexibilities expire on 31 Dec 2026 unless a final rule lands first ([Holland & Knight](https://hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/dea-and-hhs-extend-telemedicine-prescribing-flexibilities-through-2026)). Enanthate vials are a small adjacent niche: about 20K government scripts, all from Hikma.

# Symbiotec's steroid franchise sits beside classical fermentation

Frost & Sullivan (F&S) defines classical fermentation APIs in Concord Biotech's July-2023 RHP as small molecules made by microbes, either directly or semi-synthetically. They cluster in four areas. Immunosuppressants include tacrolimus, cyclosporine, sirolimus and the mycophenolates. Oncology covers rapalogs, mitomycin and anthracyclines. Anti-infectives span mupirocin, glycopeptides, polymyxins, echinocandins and beta-lactam starting materials. The fourth area is statins. That market was worth about **$11–12bn in 2022**, inside a **$219bn global API market**. Symbiotec Pharmalab listed on 1-Sep-2026 after an RHP dated 18-Aug-2026, and it is not a classical fermenter. Its core business uses microbes to convert soy phytosterols into steroid building blocks (androstenedione, 9-OH-AD and bisnor alcohol), followed by many steps of chemistry. F&S's own 2026 taxonomy files that route under **"bioconversion", not "classical fermentation"**. The markets Symbiotec actually serves are small and slow-growing: **$421.2m in corticosteroid APIs and $300.7m in steroid-hormone APIs in 2025**, each growing 1–3% a year. Its true classical-fermentation market is only **$491.9m**. The headline "fermentation API" market of $100.4bn is 82% biologics. Its competitors fall into two groups that barely overlap. Concord and its immunosuppressant-led global rivals matter to Symbiotec only through its early pipeline, above all mupirocin. In its core products, Symbiotec competes with Chinese and Western steroid makers such as Zhejiang Xianju, Tianjin Tianyao/Tianfa, Pfizer CentreOne, Bayer and Curia. Every industry figure in both prospectuses comes from F&S reports paid for by the issuer, and the baselines shift from one edition to the next. Global API rises from $219bn (2022) to $305.5bn (2025). The biologic share of API swings from 37% to 24% to 35.7%. Symbiotec's claimed volume share in steroid hormones fell from **44.2% in the DRHP to 23.8% in the RHP**. Analysts should anchor on one edition, the Symbiotec RHP's CY2025 series, and treat comparisons across documents as directional only.

The issuer commissioned and paid for all three F&S reports. ## Eight therapy families make up the classical fermentation universe

The Concord RHP sets the definition that Indian offer documents still use. Per the RHP, "fermentation-derived APIs are active ingredients or intermediates made using microbial or cell line host fermentation", and they "can be naturally derived as well as semi-synthetically processed with a combination of fermentation and chemical synthesis". The RHP says they span "vaccines to anti-cancer cytotoxic drugs, antibiotics and antifungals, hormonal products, immunosuppressants, and vitamins". It defines semi-synthesis as microbial fermentation and purification to an intermediate, followed by "chemical synthesis to obtain final products" ([Concord RHP pp.109, 119](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1690775995930.pdf)). F&S then sizes a narrower **"small molecule fermentation API" market** and splits it into only three therapy areas: immunosuppressants, oncology and anti-infectives. Concord's business section sorts its **23 APIs** into fermentation-based products and semi-synthetics "manufactured with our in-house fermentation-based key starting materials". In practice, "classical fermentation" in Indian IPO usage means fermentation-derived small molecules, natural or semi-synthetic, and excludes recombinant biologics ([Concord RHP pp.140–141, 150–152](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1690775995930.pdf)).

Other sources extend the list. India's bulk-drug PLI scheme puts **penicillin G, 7-ACA, erythromycin thiocyanate and clavulanic acid** in Target Segment I, "Key Fermentation Based KSMs". It puts **rifampicin, betamethasone, dexamethasone and prednisolone** in Target Segment II, "Fermentation Based Niche" products, and Symbiotec appears among the TS-II approved applicants ([DoP PLI list](https://www.pharma-dept.gov.in/sites/default/files/List%20of%20approved%20applicants%20Dated%2006th%20November%2C%202023.pdf); [Civilsdaily](https://www.civilsdaily.com/news/pli-scheme-for-bulk-drugs-and-resilient-pharmaceutical-supply-chain/)). The 2026 F&S report in the Symbiotec RHP draws "classical fermentation" more narrowly: "utilization of naturally occurring microbes… to cultivate and produce natural metabolites", applied to "antibiotics (e.g., penicillin), amino acids, vitamins, and organic acids" ([Symbiotec RHP pp.186–187](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). Symbiotec's own pipeline under that heading covers "anti-infectives, anti-fungals, immunosuppressants, enzymes and anti-parasitic" ([Symbiotec RHP p.220](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). Its named launches are **mupirocin, serratiopeptidase, vitamin D3 and generic Premarin API** ([internal desk note](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1f9tjaaIxmkSk2HeXuh26ocFrbLbdihtrDS10-N7RphY/edit); [ICICI Direct, 1-Oct-2026](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)). Concord has since added **nystatin** (FY25) and **fusidic acid** (FY26) as second-source anti-infectives ([Concord Q4FY26 call](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/CONCORD_05062026184012_Earnings_Call_Transcript_Q4_26.pdf)).

The table shows where the value sits. **Immunosuppressants and oncology are about 3% of fermentation API volume but over 20% of its value.** Anti-infectives are the volume base, growing only 2–4%. The loss-of-exclusivity wave for small molecules has largely passed: tacrolimus went generic in 2008, mycophenolate mofetil in 2007, pimecrolimus in 2015 and everolimus in 2020. F&S expected only **about four fermentation small molecules worth about $280m** to lose exclusivity in 2023–25, against **22 fermentation biologics worth about $60bn** ([Concord RHP pp.112–120](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1690775995930.pdf)). Concord confirmed the soft pricing in FY26: "there has been no price growth for us" ([Concord Q4FY26 call](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/CONCORD_05062026184012_Earnings_Call_Transcript_Q4_26.pdf)). The three sub-segments F&S sized add up to about $5.6bn, roughly half of the $11–12bn total. The unsized half holds statins, vitamins and "hormonal products", so the 2023 report names steroids as part of the fermentation universe but never puts a number on them.

## Symbiotec's steroid route starts with bioconversion and ends in chemistry

Symbiotec's RHP describes its process plainly. "The initial stages of API manufacturing involve bioconversion of phytosterol to the desired starting materials or building blocks like **bisnor alcohol, 9-hydroxy androstenedione or androstenedione**." These building blocks then go through acetylation, Birch reduction, bromination, cyanation, epoxidation, fluorination, Grignard and hydrogenation steps. In one worked example, a single key starting material made from 9-OH-AD feeds a chain of **four chemically derived APIs** ([Symbiotec RHP p.240](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). The phytosterols are soybean-oil by-products, partly imported from the US. They are "converted into KSMs through bio-transformation processes using fermentation" ([Symbiotec RHP p.233](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf); [ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)). The RHP does not name the microbes. In the literature, *Mycobacterium* side-chain cleavage of phytosterols to AD is a commercial route dating to the 1970s ([US Patent 6,071,714](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6071714)). Engineered strains reach about 31 g/L of 4-AD, and fungal and bacterial hydroxylation and dehydrogenation steps carry intermediates toward hydrocortisone and prednisolone ([Microbial Cell Factories 2021](https://microbialcellfactories.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12934-021-01653-9/tables/2); [PMC6680611](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680611); [Molecules 2020](https://www.doi.org/10.3390/molecules25092192)).

The distinction from classical fermentation is mechanistic. In classical fermentation, the microbe builds the drug's carbon skeleton from cheap sugars as a natural secondary metabolite; tacrolimus and mupirocin are examples. In steroid biotransformation, the four-ring skeleton comes ready-made from plants, and the microbe only trims and modifies it. **F&S's 2026 taxonomy makes exactly this split.** It defines "Bioconversion (Biotransformation)" as "utilization of microbial enzymes to selectively convert a substrate into a more valuable compound", with "steroid modification" as the first listed application. Classical fermentation sits in a separate row ([Symbiotec RHP pp.186–187](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)).

The boundary is not applied consistently, though. The same 2026 report tags eight of Symbiotec's top ten products as using fermentation ("Yes") and two as "Yes – indirectly". It calls "antibiotics and steroids" the fermentation API market's "traditional stronghold" ([Symbiotec RHP pp.190, 205–206](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). Concord's 2023 F&S report counts "hormonal products" as fermentation-derived ([Concord RHP p.109](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1690775995930.pdf)), and the PLI scheme calls corticosteroids "fermentation based niche" APIs. By Concord's own definition of semi-synthesis (a fermented intermediate plus chemistry to the final product), Symbiotec's steroids look structurally like Concord's mycophenolate mofetil. The difference is weighting. Concord ferments the complex part of the molecule, while Symbiotec buys the complex part from soybeans and adds most of the value through chemistry.

Capacity data confirms that weighting. Symbiotec has **584.67 MT of chemical-synthesis capacity** but only **300 KL of pharma fermentation** at Pithampur (4 × 5 KL plus 8 × 35 KL, 72.91% used in FY26). The other 400 KL of the headline 700 KL sits at Ujjain. That plant is FSSAI- and FSSC-approved food-grade capacity built for industrial-biotech CDMO, not for steroid APIs ([Symbiotec RHP pp.30, 218–221](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). Concord's 1,250 KL, by contrast, is all pharma fermentation ([Concord RHP](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1690775995930.pdf)). Hawkwood, a partner that calls itself "100% in with Symbiotec", promotes the CDMO side on cost (about 50% below Western CMOs) and build speed (400 KL in 18 months). Its view is advocacy rather than independent diligence ([Hawkwood expert call](https://app.notion.com/p/39137a2240098044b953cdef181c708b)).

Symbiotec's true classical-fermentation business is new and still immaterial. The company launched "classical fermentation-based APIs at the Pithampur Facility" in 2023 and holds a **ten-year take-or-pay contract** for them ([Symbiotec RHP pp.232, 266](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). Even so, APIs, which are overwhelmingly steroids, made up **96.07% of FY26 revenue**, and CDMO made up 0.13% ([Symbiotec RHP pp.28–29](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). The fermentation step's main strategic value is security of key starting materials (KSMs). Symbiotec claims make-versus-buy flexibility on more than 80% of products by revenue. F&S notes "China's Cortisone and steroid export dominance" ([Symbiotec RHP pp.202, 212, 220](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). Yet raw-material imports from China were **52.8%, 16.8% and 23.9% of total expenses in FY24, FY25 and FY26** ([Symbiotec RHP pp.36–37](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). ICICI Direct says the company still buys 45–50% of its requirements from China despite "90–95% backward integration" ([ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)). For comparison, Concord's China imports were 32.0% of materials consumed in FY23 ([Concord RHP p.28](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1690775995930.pdf)).

**For policy and for headline market sizing, Symbiotec belongs in the fermentation-derived API universe. In technology and economics, it is a steroid chemistry company with an in-house biotransformation front end**, plus a small classical and precision-fermentation business. Concord is therefore an imperfect comparable. The 8-percentage-point EBITDA margin gap between them in FY26 (26.6% versus 34.8%) fits that difference, though it does not prove it.

## A $305bn API market narrows to a $722m steroid pool

The global API market depends on which F&S edition you use. In the Concord RHP it is **$219bn in 2022, rising to $278bn by 2026 (6.1% CAGR)**. Biologics are 37% of that, captive production about 60% versus 40% merchant, and the largest therapy areas are oncology at $42bn, anti-infectives at $32bn and immunosuppressants at $16bn ([Concord RHP pp.106–107](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1690775995930.pdf)). Anthem's F&S report (Dec-2024) puts it at $262.2bn in 2023 ([Anthem F&S](https://anthembio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Frost-Sullivan-FS-Report.pdf)). The Symbiotec DRHP gives $285.4bn for 2024, rising to $399.9bn by 2029 ([Symbiotec DRHP p.186](https://www.bseindia.com/corporates/download/379013/IPO%20Prior/DRHP_fillingversion_20251219165255.pdf)). The Symbiotec RHP gives **$305.5bn in 2025, rising to $424.6bn by 2030 (6.8%)**, with small molecules at 64.3% and North America at 53.4% of value ([Symbiotec RHP pp.183–184](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)).

The two fermentation figures measure different things. Concord's F&S sizes only small molecules: **$11bn in its text or $12bn in its chart for 2022, rising to about $14bn by 2026 at 3.6%**. In volume terms that is 51,519 MT rising to 61,673 MT (4.6%). North America takes 43% of value on 13% of volume, while Asia takes 51% of volume but only about 25% of value ([Concord RHP pp.109–110](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1690775995930.pdf)). Symbiotec's F&S sizes all modalities: **$100.4bn in 2025, rising to $162.5bn by 2030 (10.1%)**, of which biologics are $82.3bn. Small molecules are $18.1bn in the chart (5.5% CAGR), and the "classical fermentation API market" is just **$491.9m, growing 1.5–4.0%**. A footnote says that market "includes products from Symbiotec's product portfolio" ([Symbiotec RHP pp.187–188](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). On their starting points the two editions agree more than they seem to. Small-molecule fermentation was about $11–12bn around 2020–22 in both (the Symbiotec RHP shows $12.0bn for 2020), and in both it is **about 5–6% of global API value**. They split on growth. Concord's forecast path implies about $13.3bn by 2025, while Symbiotec's F&S reports $18.1–19.1bn. Neither document explains the gap.

India's API market is only partly sized. The Concord RHP puts it at **$17bn in 2022**: $12bn consumed domestically (forecast at $19bn by 2026, 11.1% CAGR) and $5bn exported. It also notes that India filed half of all US DMFs in 2022 (263 of 524) ([Concord RHP pp.122–125](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2023/1690775995930.pdf)). The Symbiotec RHP gives only exports: **$4.9bn in 2025, rising to $6.4bn by 2030 (5.5%)**. It also cites Invest India's estimate that about 500 Indian makers supply "approximately 5.2%" of global API value ([Symbiotec RHP pp.207–208](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). On RHP numbers that implies about $15.9bn, close to Concord's $17bn. Third-party estimates range from $8.65bn ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-api-market)) to $11.6bn ([Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/articles/india-api-market-dependency-to-dominance)) and $14.18bn ([Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-active-pharmaceutical-ingredients-market)). **No document sizes an Indian fermentation API market.** Despite the PLI scheme, China's share of India's API, KSM and intermediate imports rose from about 68% in FY21 to 74% in FY25 ([IPA Newspack](https://ipanewspack.com/pharma-pli-attracts-over-rs-46700-crore-investments-but-china-still-dominates-api-imports/)).

The steroid markets appear only in Symbiotec's documents, and they are small. The **corticosteroid API market grew from $378.9m in 2020 to $421.2m in 2025 (2.1% CAGR)**, with a 1.6–3.0% forecast to 2030. The **steroid-hormone API market grew from $276.6m to $300.7m (1.7%)**, with a 1.0–2.5% forecast. F&S says both are "based on Symbiotec's focus products" ([Symbiotec RHP pp.191–193](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787119258529.pdf)). The DRHP gave $414.5m and $296.6m for 2024 ([Symbiotec DRHP pp.194–195](https://www.bseindia.com/corporates/download/379013/IPO%20Prior/DRHP_fillingversion_20251219165255.pdf)). The combined **$722m** pool would reach about $772–828m by 2030 on F&S's ranges. Symbiotec's FY26 API revenue of ₹8,350m is about $95m at ₹88/$, or roughly **13% of the pool by value**. Its claimed volume shares are higher, 38.2% in corticosteroids and 23.8% in hormones, which means it sells a mix that is heavy in volume but low in price per kg. Progesterone, for instance, fetches about $215/kg, against about $4,358/kg for clobetasol. Management, as quoted by ICICI Direct, describes a market of about $1bn in which Symbiotec has about $100m, or about 10% ([ICICI Direct](https://mailcontent.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_symbiotec_convictionidea.pdf)). Broader research houses report much bigger numbers: about $2.4bn for corticosteroid APIs alone ([QYResearch via DRI](https://www.dri.co.jp/auto/report/qyr/251011-corticosteroids-api-global-market-share.html)) and $3.54bn for "steroid hormone API" ([Towards Healthcare](https://www.towardshealthcare.com/insights/steroid-hormone-api-market-sizing)). The 5–10x gap almost certainly reflects scope, such as all corticosteroids, contraceptive progestins and veterinary uses, rather than a different view of the same molecules.

## Conclusion

The "fermentation" label on Symbiotec's equity story points to markets its core business does not address. The steroid pool it serves grows 1–3% a year. Symbiotec already holds about 13% of that pool by value, and 76–80% of volume in hydrocortisone, testosterone and methylprednisolone. Its own classical-fermentation market is under $0.5bn and growing about as slowly. The double-digit growth rates in its F&S report belong to biologics and CDMO, which brought in 0.13% of FY26 revenue. Start-up costs at the new businesses then cut Q1FY27 consolidated profit in half. Growth in the core steroid business therefore has to come from mix, pricing, KSM costs and new molecules such as conjugated estrogens, not from the market. In effect, investors are buying a CDMO and injectables option funded by a mature steroid cash engine whose growth depends on taking share. A steroid market of under $1bn caps that cash engine more tightly than its "fermentation API" positioning suggests.

For comparables, Concord matches Symbiotec on API revenue but not on market. The two share no products except mupirocin. Symbiotec's pricing power is set by Xianju, Tianyao/Tianfa, Henan Lihua and the Western legacy producers, not by Indian fermenters. Several of those rivals are shrinking, which helps Symbiotec gain share, but no source explains why they are shrinking. A sensible comp set mixes Chinese and European steroid makers for competitive economics with Concord and Laurus for valuing the fermentation platform. Three questions should go to management. Which molecules make up the $491.9m classical-fermentation market? What are the company's value shares, as opposed to volume shares? What exactly explains the hormone share falling from 44.2% to 23.8%? Until these are answered, the F&S share claims are best treated as company supply data scaled to a market that F&S defined to fit the company.
